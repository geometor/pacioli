Cap. I — Epistle on the Divine Proportion to the Most Excellent Prince Ludovico Maria Sforza Anglo, Duke of Milan, Ornament of Peace and War, by Brother Luca Pacioli of Borgo Sansepolcro of the Order of Minorites, Professor of Sacred Theology
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **The Scientific Duel of February 9, 1498**: Pacioli opens Part I with a vivid eyewitness account of a *laudabile e scientifico duello* (an academic disputation) held on February 9, 1498 in the Castello Sforzesco of Milan before Duke :person:`Ludovico Maria Sforza <ludovico-sforza>` ("il Moro", 1452–1508). This courtly gathering brings together Franciscan Scholastic theologians, military commanders, court physicians, astrologers, jurists, and Renaissance artist-engineers.
2. **Franciscan & Courtly Prosopography**:
   - :person:`**Magister Gometius** <gometius>`: :person:`Gomez de Lisboa <gometius>` (d. 1513), Portuguese Franciscan theologian at Pavia and Milan, later Vicar General of the Conventual Franciscans.
   - :person:`**Domenico Ponzone** <domenico-ponzone>` and :person:`**Francesco Busti** <francesco-busti>`: Prominent Milanese Franciscan preachers and regents of the Convent of :person:`San Francesco <francis-of-assisi>` Grande.
   - :person:`**Galeazzo Sanseverino** <galeazzo-sanseverino>` (c. 1460–1525): Son-in-law of :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`, Captain General of the Milanese armies, and Pacioli's personal patron in Milan (to whom one of the three manuscript copies of *De Divina Proportione* was presented).
   - **Court Physicians & Astrologers**: :person:`Ambrogio Varese da Rosate <ambrogio-da-rosate>` (court astrologer and physician to :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`), :person:`Aloisio Marliani <aloisio-marliani>`, :person:`Gabriele Pirovano <gabriele-pirovano>`, :person:`Nicolò Cusano <nicolo-cusano>` (a Milanese physician, not the Cardinal Nicholas of Cusa who died in 1464), and :person:`Andrea da Novara <andrea-da-novara>`.
3. **Leonardo da Vinci's Works in Milan**:
   - **The Sforza Equestrian Monument**: Pacioli records the exact dimensions of Leonardo's colossal clay model for the monument to :person:`Francesco Sforza <francesco-sforza>`: 12 Milanese *braccia* from the horse'spoll (*cervice*, misprinted *cerulee* in 1509) to the ground, which Pacioli calibrates directly against a printed reference segment :g:`ab` on Folio 0017r ($37\frac{4}{5}$ times :g:`ab`), and an estimated bronze casting weight of 200,000 Milanese *libbre* (at 12 ounces per pound).
   - **The Last Supper (*:term:`Cenacolo <simulacro>`*)**: Described as *el ligiadro de l'ardente desiderio de nostra salute :term:`simulacro <simulacro>`* ("the graceful simulacrum of the ardent desire for our salvation", alluding to Luke 22:15, *Desiderio desideravi hoc pascha manducare vobiscum*) in the refectory of :term:`Santa Maria delle Grazie <santa-maria-delle-grazie>`.
   - **Leonardo's Treatises**: Pacioli provides priceless documentary testimony that by 1498 Leonardo had already completed a treatise on painting and human movements (:cite:`*libro de pictura e movimenti humani* <leonardo:pittura>`) and was actively writing a treatise on mechanics: local motion, percussion, weights, and accidental forces (*del moto locale, dele percussioni e pesi e dele forze tutte*).
   - :person:`**Giacomo Andrea da Ferrara** <giacomo-andrea>`: Architect and commentator on :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`, closely bound to Leonardo as a brother (*suo quanto fratello*, misprinted *quarto fratello* in the 1509 edition, as :person:`Winterberg <winterberg>` notes). He was executed by the French in May 1500 after the fall of Milan.
4. **Epistemological Primacy of Mathematics**: Pacioli cites :person:`Averroes <averroes>`'s commentary on :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`'s :cite:`*Metaphysics* <aristotle:met:II>` II (Book $\alpha$, comm. 14–16): mathematical demonstrations occupy the "first degree of certainty" (:cite:`*in primo gradu certitudinis* <averroes:met:II.16>`), whereas natural philosophy and medicine (exemplified by the classical controversy between :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`/:person:`Avicenna <avicenna>` on the heart, :person:`Galen <galen>` on the brain/liver, and Empedocles/Critias on the blood as the seat of life) remain subject to probable dispute.
