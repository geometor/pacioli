Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Vitruvius's Attic Base (Vitruvius III.5.2)**: :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` step-by-step arithmetic for the Attic column base (:ref:`Plate 0213 <plates-architectural-en>`) follows :cite:`Vitruvius III.5 <vitruvius:de-arch:III.5>`.2–3: total base height :math:`= \frac{1}{2}D`; plinth width :math:`= 1\frac{1}{2}D`; plinth height :math:`= \frac{1}{3}` of base height (:math:`= \frac{1}{6}D`), leaving :math:`\frac{2}{3}` of base height (:math:`= \frac{1}{3}D`), of which :math:`\frac{1}{4}` is the upper torus and the remaining :math:`\frac{3}{4}` is split equally (:math:`\frac{3}{8}` each) between the lower torus and the scotia (*trochilus*) with its fillets.

Cap. VI — On the Order and Construction of the Stylobate (Pedestal or Base-Pillar) of the Column, the Stereobate Foundation, the Ancient Roman Alphabet, and the Three Sets of Sixty Polyhedra Models Made by Pacioli alongside Leonardo da Vinci.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Pacioli and Leonardo da Vinci Living Together in Milan (1496–1499) and Florence (1500+)**: This famous passage on Folio 0072 (``f. 28v``) is one of the cornerstone documents of :person:`Leonardo da Vinci's <leonardo>` biography:
  1. It confirms that :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>` drew the 60 polyhedra plates in Milan between **1496 and 1499** while both he and :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` were on the payroll of Duke :person:`Ludovico Maria Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`;
  2. It records that after the fall of Milan to Louis XII of France in late **1499**, :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` and :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>` **left Milan together** (visiting Mantua and Venice) and **shared a household in Florence** (*e a Firenze pur insiemi trahemmo domicilio*);
  3. It documents the **three sets of 60 painted three-dimensional polyhedra models** constructed by :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` own hands: the first for :person:`**Ludovico Sforza** <ludovico-sforza>` (presented in 1498), the second for :person:`**Galeazzo Sanseverino** <galeazzo-sanseverino>` in Milan, and the third in Florence for **Gonfaloniere Piero Soderini** (kept in the Palazzo Vecchio, as also recorded in Florentine municipal payment records of 1505).
