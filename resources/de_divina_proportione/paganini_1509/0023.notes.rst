Cap. V — On the Fitting Title of the Present Treatise
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Coining the Name *:term:`Divina Proportione <divina-proportione>`***: Prior to Pacioli's treatise, Euclid's division of a line in extreme and mean ratio (Euclid VI.def.3, $\ddot{\alpha}\kappa\rho\omicron\varsigma \kappa\alpha\grave{\iota} \mu\acute{\epsilon}\sigma\omicron\varsigma \lambda\acute{\omicron}\gamma\omicron\varsigma$) was known in medieval Latin exclusively by Gerard of Cremona's and Campanus of Novara's rendering *proportio habens medium et duo extrema* ("proportion having a mean and two extremes"). Chapter V is the historic locus where Pacioli christens the ratio $\phi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ the *:term:`*Divine Proportion* <divina-proportione>`* (*:term:`Divina Proportione <divina-proportione>`*), grounding the name in five Scholastic-Platonic analogies:
   1. **Unicity**: Unlike :term:`multiplex <multiplex>` or superparticular ratios ($2:1, 3:1, 3:2, 4:3, \dots$), which form infinite families of species, :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` is unique (*una sola e non più*).
   2. **Trinitarian Structure**: :term:`Continuous proportion <proportione-continua>` requires a minimum of three terms ($a+b : a :: a : b$), mirroring the *una substantia in tribus personis* of Trinitarian theology.
   3. **Apophatic Ineffability**: The incommensurability of $\phi$ (:cite:`Euclid XIII.6 <euclid:elements:XIII.6>`) is compared to negative (apophatic) theology: just as God transcends finite verbal definition, $\phi$ transcends rational numerical expression (*occulta e secreta*).
   4. **Self-Similarity and Invariance**: Echoing the Neoplatonic and Augustinian maxim that God is *totus in toto et totus in qualibet parte*, $\phi$ remains invariant across all scales and self-replicates upon subdivision or addition (:cite:`Euclid XIII.4 <euclid:elements:XIII.4>`, :cite:`Euclid XIV.2 <euclid:elements:XIV.2>`).
   5. **Cosmological Formative Principle (Plato's *:cite:`Timaeus <plato:timaeus:53c-56c>`*)**: Combining Aristotle's *:cite:`De Caelo <aristotle:de-caelo:III.8>`* (the celestial *:term:`quinta essentia <quinta-essentia>`*) with Plato's *:cite:`Timaeus <plato:timaeus:53c-56c>`* 53c–56c (:term:`Tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` = Fire, Cube = Earth, :term:`Octahedron <octahedron>` = Air, :term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>` = Water, :term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` = the Cosmos/Heaven), :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` argues that because the regular pentagon and :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`—and the mutual proportions of all five Platonic solids inscribed in a single sphere (:cite:`Euclid XIII.13 <euclid:elements:XIII.13>`–:cite:`Euclid XIII.18 <euclid:elements:XIII.18>`)—depend essentially on $\phi$, the :term:`Divine Proportion <divina-proportione>` is the formal geometric principle of cosmic order.

Cap. VI — On Its Worthy Commendation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Campanus of Novara on Book XIV, Proposition 10 (*:term:`Irrationale <irrationale>` Simphonia*)**: Pacioli translates and adapts a celebrated passage from Campanus of Novara's 13th-century commentary on Book XIV, Proposition 10 of Euclid's *Elements* (a corollary added by Campanus to Hypsicles' *Book XIV* comparing the dodecahedron and icosahedron inscribed in the same sphere). Campanus marveled at the *mirabilis :term:`potentia <potentia>` lineae secundum proportionem habentem medium duoque extrema divisae*, writing: *"Cui cum plurima admiranda conveniant, hoc praecipue... ex invariabili natura superiorum principiorum sibi adest, ut tam diversa solida, tum magnitudine, tum basium numero, tum etiam figura, :term:`irrationali <irrationale>` quadam symphonia rationabiliter conciliet"* ("To which, though very many admirable properties belong, this above all is present to it from the invariable nature of higher principles: that it rationally reconciles solids so diverse in magnitude, in number of bases, and in figure, by a certain irrational symphony").

Cap. VII — On the First Effect of a Line Divided According to Our Proportion
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Proposition Citations in Chapter VII**:
   - **First Effect (Euclid XIII.1)**: If a line $AB = a+b$ is cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` at $C$ with greater segment $AC = a$, and $AD = \frac{1}{2}AB$ is added to $AC$, then $CD^2 = 5 \cdot AD^2$, i.e., $(a + \frac{a+b}{2})^2 = 5(\frac{a+b}{2})^2$.
   - **Campanus VI.19 = Heiberg Euclid VI.30**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` cites *la 19. del 6.* ("the 19th of the 6th"), which is the numbering of *Elements* VI.30 ("To cut a given finite straight line in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`") in the medieval Latin edition of :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>`, preceded by :cite:`Euclid VI.def.3 <euclid:elements:VI.def.3>`.
   - :cite:`**Euclid II.11** <euclid:elements:II.11>`: :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s geometric construction in Book II ("To cut a given straight line so that the rectangle contained by the whole and one of the segments is equal to the square on the remaining segment"), which constructs the :term:`golden section <goldener-schnitt>` by areas prior to the theory of proportions in Book V.
   - **Campanus Euclid IX.16**: In :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s commentary on *Elements* IX.16 ("If two numbers be prime to one another, the second will not be to any other number as the first is to the second"), :person:`Campanus <campanus>` proves as a corollary that no three integers can stand in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` (since if $a+b : a = a : b$ in lowest terms, $a$ and $b$ would be coprime, yet $a+b$ and $a$ would also be coprime, contradicting IX.16).
2. **Christological and Boethian Analogies**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` extends the theological analogies of Chapter V with a Christological comparison (Christ fulfilling the Mosaic Law, Matthew 5:17, and submitting to Mary and Joseph, Luke 2:51) and quotes the famous Neoplatonic hymn *O qui perpetua mundum ratione gubernas* from :person:`Boethius <boethius>`'s *Consolation of Philosophy* (Book III, metrum 9, line 3): *stabilisque manens dat cuncta moveri* ("remaining unmoved, He gives all things to move").
