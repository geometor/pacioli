Cap. XXXVIII — How the Exacedron or Cube Is Formed in the Octocedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0039 (``f. 12r``) contains diagram ``0039.g1.png`` showing the :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` and the cube whose 8 vertices sit at the centroids of the 8 triangular faces.
- **Exact Ratio**: If the :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` has edge :math:`s_{\text{octa}}`, its equatorial square has diagonal :math:`s_{\text{octa}}\sqrt{2}`. The centroids of two opposite faces lie at :math:`\frac{1}{3}` of the way from the equatorial midpoints toward the poles, so the face diagonal of the inscribed cube is :math:`\frac{2}{3}s_{\text{octa}}`, and the edge of the inscribed cube is :math:`s_{\text{cube}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}s_{\text{octa}}`, giving :math:`s_{\text{octa}}^{2} : s_{\text{cube}}^{2} = 9 : 2`.

Cap. XXXIX — How the Tetracedron Is Formed in the Octocedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Commensurability in Length (:math:`2:3`)**: Notice the remarkable rational consequence of composing :cite:`Campanus XV.4 <campanus:euclid:XV.4>` (:math:`s_{\text{cube}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}s_{\text{octa}}`) with :cite:`Campanus XV.2 <campanus:euclid:XV.2>` (:math:`s_{\text{tetra}} = \sqrt{2}\,s_{\text{cube}}`): the :math:`\sqrt{2}` factors cancel completely, leaving :math:`s_{\text{tetra}} = \frac{2}{3}s_{\text{octa}}`!

Cap. XL — How the Duodecedron Is Formed in the Ycocedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Duality of Icosahedron and Dodecahedron**: Just as ``Cap. XXXVII``–``Cap. XXXVIII`` established the dual reciprocity between the cube (6 faces, 8 vertices) and the :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` (8 faces, 6 vertices), ``Cap. XL`` (:cite:`Campanus XV.6 <campanus:euclid:XV.6>`) and ``Cap. XLIII`` (:cite:`Campanus XV.9 <campanus:euclid:XV.9>`) establish the dual reciprocity between the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` (20 faces, 12 vertices) and the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (12 faces, 20 vertices).

Cap. XLI — How the Exacedron or Cube Is Placed in the Ycocedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Elegance of Campanus's Mediated Inscription**: Rather than attempting a direct coordinate construction in the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>`, :person:`Campanus <campanus>` (and :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>`) exploits the dual :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (XV.6) together with :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s roof construction (:cite:`Euclid XIII.17 <euclid:elements:XIII.17>`): since every regular :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` contains 5 inscribed cubes (whose 12 edges are face diagonals of the 12 pentagons), inscribing the dual :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` inside the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` immediately places 8 of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>`'s 20 face-centers at the corners of a regular cube!

Cap. XLII — How the Tetracedron Is Formed in the Ycocedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Chain of Three Inscriptions**: Notice the genealogical chain of reductions: :term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>` :math:`\supset` :term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (XV.6, 20 vertices at face-centers) :math:`\supset` Cube (XV.7, 8 of those 20 vertices) :math:`\supset` :term:`Tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` (XV.2/XV.8, 4 of those 8 vertices).

Cap. XLIV — How the Ycocedron Is Placed in the Cube.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Two Ways to Inscribe the Icosahedron in the Cube**:
  1. **Edge-on-Face Inscription (Golden Rectangles)**: An :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` of edge :math:`a_{20}` has 12 vertices that form three mutually perpendicular golden rectangles of dimensions :math:`a_{20} \times \varphi\,a_{20}`. Placing these three golden rectangles inside a cube of side :math:`s_{\text{cube}} = \varphi\,a_{20}` puts 6 edges of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` (and all 12 of its vertices!) directly on the 6 square faces of the cube, with the cube edge and :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` edge in the :term:`**Divine Proportion** <divina-proportione>` (:math:`s_{\text{cube}} : a_{20} = \varphi : 1`).
  2. **Campanus XV.10 Inscription**: :person:`Campanus <campanus>` routes the construction through the :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` inscribed in the cube (XV.3) and the circles of :cite:`Euclid XIII.16 <euclid:elements:XIII.16>`.
