Cap. XLV — How the Octocedron Is Placed in the Duodecedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Geometric Insight of Campanus XV.11**: In :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s construction of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (:cite:`Euclid XIII.17 <euclid:elements:XIII.17>`), 6 of the 30 edges of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` are the roof ridges :math:`vx` centered over the 6 faces of the inscribed cube. The midpoints of those 6 edges lie on the 3 coordinate axes of the cube at distance :math:`\frac{1}{2}s_{\text{cube}} + \text{lesser} = \frac{\varphi}{2}s_{\text{cube}} = \frac{\varphi^{2}}{2}a_{12}` from the origin. Because the 3 coordinate axes are orthogonal and equal in length, those 6 edge-midpoints are the exact vertices of a **regular octahedron** of edge :math:`a_{8} = \frac{\varphi^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}a_{12}`!

Cap. XLVI — How the Exacedron and the Tetracedron Are Placed in the Duodecedron, and Why Only Twelve Inscriptions Are Possible.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0041 (``f. 13r``) shows diagram ``0041.g3.png`` for :cite:`Campanus XV.12 <campanus:euclid:XV.12>` (the cube and :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` inscribed in the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`).
- **Theological-Geometric Conceit of the Holy Cross**: Pacioli's explanation on Folio 0042 (``f. 13v``) of why :person:`Campanus <campanus>` inscribes all four solids in the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (XV.9, 11, 12) but only three in the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` (XV.6, 7, 8)—invoking the three mutually perpendicular axes of the :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` as the *glorioso segno dela sancta Croce*—is a classic example of Franciscan mathematical theology. Ironically, from a purely group-theoretic standpoint, the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` and :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` share the exact same symmetry group :math:`A_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_2` and the exact same 30 edge-midpoints (which form the 30 vertices of an :term:`**icosidodecahedron** <dodecahedron-abscisum>`), so the 6 midpoints of three orthogonal pairs of opposite edges of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` *do* form a regular :term:`octahedron <octahedron>`, just as in :cite:`Campanus XV.11 <campanus:euclid:XV.11>` for the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`!
