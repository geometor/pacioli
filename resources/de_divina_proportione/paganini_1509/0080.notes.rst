Cap. XVIII — How Stonemasons and Sculptors Are Highly Commended for Placing the Regular and Dependent Polyhedra in Their Works, and How Phidias Placed the Icosahedron in the Temple of Ceres in Rome.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Architectural Legacy of Pacioli's Polyhedra**: :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` advice in ``Cap. XVIII`` to sculptors, stonemasons, and *intarsiatori* to place the Platonic and Archimedean solids (solid and skeletal) atop capitals, pedestals, finials, and perspective panels had an immediate and lasting impact across Europe:
  - **Fra Giovanni da Verona** carved :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>`/:person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` skeletal polyhedra into the famous intarsia panels of **Santa Maria in Organo** in Verona (c. 1519) and Monte Oliveto Maggiore;
  - **Wenzel Jamnitzer** (*Perspectiva Corporum Regularium*, Nuremberg, 1568), **Lorenz Stoer** (*Geometria et Perspectiva*, 1567), and **Jean Cousin** placed these exact polyhedra atop classical columns, pedestals, and architectural ruins;
  - Tomb monuments and church finials throughout 16th- and 17th-century Italy (including Hawksmoor's and Wren's later obelisks and finials) adopted the regular and stellated polyhedra as crowning architectural ornaments.
- **Baron Mario Mellini and the Palazzo della Cancelleria in Rome**: :person:`**Mario Mellini** <mario-mellini>` (1456–1503, nephew of Cardinal Giambattista Mellini, resident at the Torre dei Mellini near Piazza Navona, where a famous collection of antiquities and manuscripts was kept) and Cardinal **Raffaele Riario** / Cardinal :person:`**Galeotto Franciotti della Rovere** <galeotto-della-rovere>` at the **Palazzo della Cancelleria** were :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` hosts in Rome (where :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` deposited a fourth set of his polyhedra models!).

Cap. XIX — How the Architect Must Govern Himself in Cramped and Irregular Sites, Why Continuous Geometric Proportion Is Wider Than Discrete Numerical Proportion, and Praise of Master Piero della Francesca.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- *:person:`*Piero della Francesca's * <piero-della-francesca>`Legend of the True Cross* at Arezzo and *:cite:`De Prospectiva Pingendi <piero:prospectiva>`* at Urbino**: This passage on Folio 0081 (``f. 33r``) is one of the earliest and most authoritative Renaissance tributes to *:person:`*Piero della Francesca* <piero-della-francesca>`* (c. 1415–1492), listing every major city where Piero worked (**Urbino, Bologna, Ferrara, Rimini, Ancona, Sansepolcro, and Arezzo**), noting his mastery of both **oil and tempera/gouache on wall and panel** (*in muro e taula a oglio e guazzo*), singling out the **Cappella Maggiore of San Francesco in Arezzo** (*la magna capella dela tribuna del'altar grande*) as *"vna dele dignissime opere de Italia e da tutti commendata"*, and citing Piero's manuscript treatise *:cite:`De Prospectiva Pingendi <piero:prospectiva>`* in the Ducal Library of Urbino!
