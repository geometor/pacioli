Cap. LVIII — On Oblong Bodies, That Is, Bodies Longer or Higher Than They Are Wide: And First on the Round Column (Cylinder, Plates XLIII–XLIV).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram & Plates**:
  - Folio 0052 (``f. 18v``) displays diagram ``0052.g1.png`` showing the axial rectangle :math:`dcef` of the cylinder with its axis :math:`ab` and diagonal :math:`de` intersecting at the center :math:`g`.
  - **Plate XLIII** (``0277.rst``) and **Plate XLIV** (``0279.rst``): Note that in the 1509 plates, Plates XLIII–XLIV show the round cone (*Cono Solido / Vacuo*) while the marginal text numerals shifted slightly between prisms and cones; ``Cap. LXX`` explains how to match the Roman numerals in the margins.
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: The earlier ``divina_en_2/58.txt`` draft omitted the entire Euclidean proof (:cite:`Euclid I.15 <euclid:elements:I.15>`, :cite:`Euclid I.26 <euclid:elements:I.26>`, :cite:`Euclid III.31 <euclid:elements:III.31>`) that the center :math:`g` bisects both the axis :math:`ab` and the diagonal :math:`de` so that every right cylinder is inscribable in a sphere.

Cap. LIX — On Lateral (Faceted) Columns, and First on the Three-Sided (Triangular) Column, Called the Serratile Body (Plates XLV–XLVI).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XLV** (``0281.rst``, *Laterato Solido* / Triangular Prism, solid) and **Plate XLVI** (``0283.rst``, *Laterato Vacuo* / Triangular Prism, skeletal).
- **Etymology of *:term:`Corpo Seratile <corpo-seratile>`***: In the medieval Gerard of Cremona and Campanus translations of Euclid Books XI–XII, a triangular prism (:g:`prisma`) is rendered *corpus serratile* ("sawed body").

Cap. LX — On Four-Sided (Quadrilateral) Lateral Columns and the Five Species of Quadrilaterals (Plates XLVII–XLVIII).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Arabic-Latin Geometric Terminology (*Elmuaym* and *:term:`Elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*)**: In Adelard of Bath's, Gerard of Cremona's, and Campanus's Latin editions of Euclid Euclid I.Def.22, the Arabic terms *al-mu'ayyan* (rhombus) and *al-munharif* (trapezium / irregular quadrilateral) were transliterated as *helmuaym*, *simile helmuaym* (rhomboid), and *helmuariphe* (*:term:`elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*). Pacioli preserves both the Greek/Italian terms (*rombo*, *romboide*, *irregulari*) and the Campanian Arabic-Latin terms (*elmuaym*, *:term:`elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*), illustrating each with lettered marginal woodcuts ``A, B, C, D, E`` on Folios 0053–0054.
- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XLVII** (``0285.rst``, Quadrilateral Prism, solid) and **Plate XLVIII** (``0287.rst``, Quadrilateral Prism, skeletal).
