Cap. II — On the Distance from the Profile to the Nape (*Cotozzo*) of the Said Head, on the Proportions of the Eye and Ear, and on the Painter's Perspective Net (*Rete*).

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- *:person:`*Alberti's * <alberti>`Velo*, Leonardo's *Pariete*, and Dürer's *Drahtgitter* (*La Rete*)**: Pacioli's detailed description on Folio 0067 (``f. 26r``) of the painter's **perspective net** (*rete*)—a wooden frame strung with lute-strings, silk threads, or sinews fixed between the artist's eye and the subject and matched to a squared drawing surface—links Leon Battista Alberti's *velo* (*De pictura*, II.31, 1435), Piero della Francesca's coordinate projections in *:cite:`De Prospectiva Pingendi <piero:prospectiva>`*, Leonardo da Vinci's *pariete di vetro* / net, and Albrecht Dürer's famous woodcuts of the draftsman's net in the *Underweysung der Messung* (1525, composed after Dürer's 1506–1507 visit to Venice and Bologna where he met :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` circle!).

Cap. III — On the Proportion of the Whole Well-Disposed Human Body to Its Head and Other Members According to Length and Breadth, and on the Perfect Numbers Six, Ten, and Sixteen.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0069 (``f. 27r``) includes the vertical graduated scale ``0069.g1.png`` marking the proportional divisions of the human body (:math:`\frac{1}{10}`, :math:`\frac{1}{8}`, :math:`\frac{1}{6}`, :math:`\frac{1}{4}`).
- **Euclid Euclid IX.36 and Algebraic *:term:`Censo de Censo <censo-de-censo>`***: Pacioli weaves together Vitruvius III.1.5–8 (on the numbers 10, 6, and 16), Euclid's theorem on even perfect numbers (Euclid IX.36: :math:`2^{n-1}(2^n - 1)` when :math:`2^n - 1` is a Mersenne prime, e.g. :math:`2 \times 3 = 6`, :math:`4 \times 7 = 28`), and Cossic algebra (*censo primo* :math:`= x^2 = 4`, *:term:`censo de censo <censo-de-censo>`* :math:`= x^4 = 16`).
