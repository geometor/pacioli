Cap. VIII — Where Columns Most Duly Made by the Ancients and Moderns Are Found Today in Italy, Critique of Leon Battista Alberti, and the Most Perfect Column in Venice at the Ca' Grande (Frari).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Pacioli Living with Leon Battista Alberti in Rome under Pope Paul II (1470–1471)**: This chapter contains the sole primary testimony that the young :person:`Luca Pacioli <pacioli>` (born c. 1447), upon arriving in Rome around **1470–1471** during the pontificate of the Venetian Pope **Paul II (Pietro Barbo, r. 1464–1471)**, lived **for many months as a guest in the Roman house of Leon Battista Alberti (1404–1472)** right up to the eve of :person:`Alberti's <alberti>` death!
- **Lorenzo de' Medici's Model for Poggio Reale in Naples**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` also gives an eyewitness report from his stay in Naples (c. 1489) that **Lorenzo the Magnificent** personally crafted an architectural model and sent it to **Giuliano da Maiano** (1432–1490) for :person:`King Ferrante <ferrante-of-naples>` and Duke Alfonso of Calabria's celebrated **Villa of Poggio Reale** (*Dogliuolo*).
- **Brunelleschi's Architecture in Florence and the Column at the Frari in Venice**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` singles out Brunelleschi's **Pazzi Chapel** at Santa Croce, **Santo Spirito**, and **San Lorenzo** as the supreme modern realizations of Vitruvian symmetry, and directs his pupils to measure the ancient column resting on an inverted capital in the second cloister of **Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari (*la Ca' Grande*)** in Venice.

Cap. IX — On Lateral (Prismatic) Columns in Architecture, and on the Quadrature of the Circle.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Aristotle on the Quadrature of the Circle (*Categories* 7b31)**: Pacioli quotes the classic Scholastic formulation of Aristotle, *Categories* 7 (7b30–33): *Quadratura circuli, si est scibilis, scientia quidem eius nondum est, ipsum vero scibile est* ("The squaring of the circle, if it is knowable, is an object of knowledge even though the knowledge of it does not yet exist"). Notice Pacioli's intriguing hint (*"Forse in questo dì è nato chi la darà"*, "Perhaps today the man has already been born who will give it")—likely an allusion to *:person:`*Leonardo da Vinci's* <leonardo>`* intense researches on lunules and the quadrature of curved surfaces (*De ludo geometrico*), which :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>` was pursuing during their years together!
