Cap. XIX — On Its Tenth Supreme Effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Universal Similarity of Golden-Divided Lines (Campanus Lemma to Euclid XIV.2)**: In :person:`Hypsicles <hypsicles>`' *Book XIV* of the *Elements*, Proposition 2 proves that the same circle circumscribes both the pentagon of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and the triangle of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` inscribed in the same sphere. In his commentary on XIV.2, :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>` inserted and proved a fundamental lemma (often cited in medieval and Renaissance mathematics simply as part of XIV.2): **if two straight lines are cut in extreme and mean ratio, they are cut similarly** ($AB : AC = DE : DF$ and $AC : CB = DF : FE$).
2. **The Six Operations of Book V**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` enumerates the six canonical Scholastic operations on proportions from Book V of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements*:
   - *Permutata* (*alternando*, :cite:`Euclid V.16 <euclid:elements:V.16>`): $a : b = c : d \implies a : c = b : d$
   - *Conversa* (*invertendo*, :cite:`Euclid V.7 <euclid:elements:V.7>`.p): $a : b = c : d \implies b : a = d : c$
   - *Congionta* (*componendo*, :cite:`Euclid V.18 <euclid:elements:V.18>`): $a : b = c : d \implies (a+b) : b = (c+d) : d$
   - *Disgionta* (*dividendo*, :cite:`Euclid V.17 <euclid:elements:V.17>`): $a : b = c : d \implies (a-b) : b = (c-d) : d$
   - *Eversa* (*convertendo*, :cite:`Euclid V.19 <euclid:elements:V.19>`.p): $a : b = c : d \implies a : (a-b) = c : (c-d)$
   - *Equa proportionalita* (*ex aequali*, :cite:`Euclid V.22 <euclid:elements:V.22>`).

Cap. XX — On Its Eleventh Most Excellent Effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Direct Calculation of the Decagon from the Radius ($R = 10$)**: Whereas Chapters XVI and XVII took $s_6 + s_{10} = 10$ (so that the radius $R = s_6 = \sqrt{125} - 5$), Chapter XX takes the hexagon side (the circle's radius $R$) itself as $10$ (so the circle's diameter is $20$). Since $(s_6 + s_{10}) : s_6 = s_6 : s_{10}$ (:cite:`Euclid XIII.9 <euclid:elements:XIII.9>`), $s_6$ is divided in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` when a segment equal to $s_{10}$ is marked off from it: thus the side of the inscribed decagon is simply the major golden segment of the radius ($s_{10} = \sqrt{125} - 5$). Note that :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` uses *circumscripto dal medesimo cerchio* in the sense of "surrounded/encompassed by the same circle" (i.e., inscribed within the circle).

Cap. XXI — On Its Twelfth Almost Incomprehensible Effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Mathematical Verification of the Twelfth Effect (Campanus XIV.9)**:
   - Let a sphere have diameter $D$. By :cite:`Euclid XIII.15 <euclid:elements:XIII.15>`, the edge of the inscribed cube satisfies $s_{\text{cube}}^2 = \frac{1}{3}D^2$.
   - By :cite:`Euclid XIII.16 <euclid:elements:XIII.16>`.p and :cite:`Euclid XIII.18 <euclid:elements:XIII.18>`, the edge of the inscribed :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` satisfies $s_{\text{icosa}}^2 = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{10}D^2$.
   - Meanwhile, in :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` formula with $a+b = 10$, $a = \sqrt{125}-5$, and $b = 15-\sqrt{125}$:
     - $(a+b)^2 + a^2 = 250 - \sqrt{12500} = 250 - 50\sqrt{5} = 50(5 - \sqrt{5})$.
     - By the Fifth Effect (Chapter XIV, :cite:`Euclid XIII.4 <euclid:elements:XIII.4>`), $(a+b)^2 + b^2 = 3a^2 = 450 - \sqrt{112500} = 3 \times 50(3 - \sqrt{5})$!
     - Wait: notice that $\frac{\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + b^2}}{\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + a^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{3a^2}}{\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + a^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} \cdot a}{\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + a^2}}$. Since $(a+b)^2 + a^2$ is the square of the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $a+b$ (radius of the pentagon's/:term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>`'s defining circle) and $a$ (the decagon side), by :cite:`Euclid XIII.10 <euclid:elements:XIII.10>` ($s_5^2 = s_6^2 + s_{10}^2$), $\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + a^2}$ is the **side of the pentagon** ($s_{\text{icosa}}$) inscribed in the circle of radius $a+b$, while $a$ is the decagon side!
   - In :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s Latin text of Book XIV, Proposition 9 proves this exact relation between the cube edge and :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` edge via the segments of a line cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`.
2. **Universal / Bound Roots (*Radici Universali / Radici Legate*)**: Pacioli introduces the Renaissance algebraic term *:term:`radice universale <radice-universale>`* or *:term:`radice legata <radice-universale>`* (written *R.V.* in the *Summa*, Distinctio VIII, Tractatus 3, f. 120r), where the radical sign governs an entire binomial or :term:`apotome <residuo>` ($\sqrt{250 - \sqrt{12500}}$).
