Cap. LXIX — On the Measure of All the Regular and Dependent Bodies, and the Panegyric to Duke Ludovico Maria Sforza and the 1498 Franciscan General Chapter in Milan.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Additivity and Subtractivity of Polyhedral Volumes**: Mathematically, ``Cap. LXIX`` formulates the exact modern decomposition method for computing the volumes of Archimedean truncations (:math:`V_{\text{trunc}} = V_{\text{parent}} - \sum V_{\text{vertex pyramids}}`) and Catalan/stellated cumulations (:math:`V_{\text{elev}} = V_{\text{core}} + \sum V_{\text{face pyramids}}`).
- **Historical Witness to the Milanese Court in 1498**:
  - **The Ducal Chapel Choir (*dignissimi cantori*)**: Under Ludovico Sforza, the *Cappella Musicale* of the Castello Sforzesco (directed by Franchino Gaffurio and having included Josquin des Prez and Loyset Compère) was one of the premier musical institutions of Europe.
  - :term:`**Santa Maria delle Grazie** <santa-maria-delle-grazie>`: :person:`Bramante <bramante>`'s monumental tribune (commissioned by :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>` as the Sforza dynastic mausoleum) and :person:`Leonardo da Vinci's <leonardo>` :term:`*Last Supper* <simulacro>` in its refectory were both being completed in 1497–1498.
  - **The Franciscan General Chapter of Pentecost 1498 in Milan**: Presided over by the famous Conventual Minister General **Francesco Sansone da Brescia** (1414–1499, General 1475–1499, also a patron of learning) and attended by **Cardinal Ippolito I d'Este** (1479–1520, Archbishop of Milan and brother of Duchess Beatrice d'Este). This passage confirms that :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` was writing the final chapters of the *Compendio* in Milan between June and December 1498.
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: The earlier ``divina_en_2/69.txt`` draft completely omitted the entire second half of ``Cap. LXIX`` (the panegyric to :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`, the Ducal Chapel choir, :term:`Santa Maria delle Grazie <santa-maria-delle-grazie>` compared to :person:`Augustus <augustus>`'s Temple of Peace, and the eyewitness account of the 1498 Franciscan General Chapter in Milan with :person:`Francesco Sansone <francesco-sansone>` and Cardinal :person:`Ippolito d'Este <ippolito-deste>`). Everything is restored in full here.

Cap. LXX — How to Locate in Order All the Bodies Drawn in Perspective in This Book, as Well as Their Physical Models According to Their Public Table.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Pacioli's Physical Polyhedra Models (*Forme Materiali*)**: ``Cap. LXX`` gives us a precious physical description of the sets of **60 three-dimensional models** that :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` constructed with his own hands (as he also notes in ``Architectura Cap. VI``, Folio 0072: one set of 60 for Duke :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`, one set for :person:`Galeazzo Sanseverino <galeazzo-sanseverino>`, and a third set in Florence for Gonfaloniere :person:`Piero Soderini <piero-soderini>`):
  - Each polyhedron was suspended from a cord (*cordiglio*);
  - Attached to the cord between **two black amber (jet) beads** (*fra doi ambre negre*) was a small parchment label (*vn breve*) bearing the Roman numeral (``I``–``LX``) and the body's name in **Greek and Latin** (identical to the cartouches printed on :person:`Leonardo's <leonardo>` woodcut plates!).

Cap. LXXI — What Is Understood by the Mathematical Terms Used in This Compendium—Namely Hypothesis, Hypotenuse, Corausto, Pyramidal Cone, Pentagonal Chord, Perpendicular, Cathetus, Diameter, Parallelogram, Diagonal, Center, and Sagitta—and Conclusion of Part I (December 14, 1498).

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0059 (``f. 22r``) contains diagram ``0059.g1.png`` illustrating the circular segment with its **arco** (arc/bow), **corda** (chord/bowstring), and :term:`**saetta** <saetta>` (:term:`sagitta <saetta>`/arrow).
- **Dating of the Colophon (December 14, 1498)**: The colophon on Folio 0061 (``f. 23r``) reads *"Finis a di 14. decembre in Milano nel nostro almo convento MCCCCXCVII. Sedente summo pontifice Alexandro VI. del suo pontificato anno VII."* Because Pope Alexander VI (Rodrigo Borgia) was elected on August 11, 1492, his **seventh pontifical year** (*anno VII*) ran from August 11, 1498 to August 10, 1499—and ``Cap. LXIX`` explicitly refers to the Franciscan General Chapter held in Milan in May–June **1498** (*de l'anno presente*). Thus ``MCCCCXCVII`` in the 1509 print dropped a final ``I`` for ``MCCCCXCVIII`` (**December 14, 1498**), the exact date recorded in the presentation manuscripts (Geneva MS l.e. 210 and Milan Biblioteca Ambrosiana MS 170 sup.).
