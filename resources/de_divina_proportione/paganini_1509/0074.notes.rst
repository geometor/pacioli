Cap. VII — Wherein the Three Species of Columns—Ionic, Doric, and Corinthian—Differ from One Another, and Vitruvius's Account of Their Origin and of Callimachus's Invention of the Corinthian Capital.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Vitruvius *De architectura* IV.1.1–10**: Pacioli quotes the entire famous Latin passage of Vitruvius IV.1 on the anthropometric origins of the Doric (:math:`1:6` then :math:`1:7`), Ionic (:math:`1:8` then :math:`1:9`), and Corinthian orders, including the story of *:person:`*Callimachus* <callimachus>`* (*katatexitechnos*) and the Corinthian maiden's tomb basket (*calathus*) covered by a square tile (*tegula*, which became the **abacus**) overgrown by an acanthus plant.

Cap. VIII — Where Columns Most Duly Made by the Ancients and Moderns Are Found Today in Italy, Critique of Leon Battista Alberti, and the Most Perfect Column in Venice at the Ca' Grande (Frari).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Pacioli Living with Leon Battista Alberti in Rome under Pope Paul II (1470–1471)**: This chapter contains the sole primary testimony that the young :person:`Luca Pacioli <pacioli>` (born c. 1447), upon arriving in Rome around **1470–1471** during the pontificate of the Venetian Pope **Paul II (Pietro Barbo, r. 1464–1471)**, lived **for many months as a guest in the Roman house of Leon Battista Alberti (1404–1472)** right up to the eve of Alberti's death!
- **Lorenzo de' Medici's Model for Poggio Reale in Naples**: Pacioli also gives an eyewitness report from his stay in Naples (c. 1489) that **Lorenzo the Magnificent** personally crafted an architectural model and sent it to **Giuliano da Maiano** (1432–1490) for King Ferrante and Duke Alfonso of Calabria's celebrated **Villa of Poggio Reale** (*Dogliuolo*).
- **Brunelleschi's Architecture in Florence and the Column at the Frari in Venice**: Pacioli singles out Brunelleschi's **Pazzi Chapel** at Santa Croce, **Santo Spirito**, and **San Lorenzo** as the supreme modern realizations of Vitruvian symmetry, and directs his pupils to measure the ancient column resting on an inverted capital in the second cloister of **Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari (*la Ca' Grande*)** in Venice.
