Cap. XXV — How It Is Not Possible for There to Be More Than Five Regular Bodies in Nature, and Why.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Source in Euclid and Campanus**: This famous classification proof comes from the concluding Scholium to :cite:`Euclid XIII.18 <euclid:elements:XIII.18>` (integrated into the proposition text in :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>`'s Latin edition as XIII.18 corollary), resting on :cite:`Euclid XI.21 <euclid:elements:XI.21>` (the sum of plane angles of any convex :term:`solid angle <angulo-solido>` is less than :math:`4R`) and :cite:`Euclid I.32 <euclid:elements:I.32>` (the interior angle sum of an :math:`n`-gon is :math:`(n-2)\cdot 2R`).
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: In ``divina_en_2/25.txt``, the angle calculations in fractions of a right angle (:math:`\frac{2}{3}R`, :math:`2\frac{2}{3}R`, :math:`3\frac{1}{3}R`, :math:`1\frac{1}{5}R`, :math:`3\frac{3}{5}R`, :math:`1\frac{1}{3}R`) were condensed into a brief table. Here the complete rhetorical and deductive structure of :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` 1509 text (``0031.rst``–``0032.rst``, ``f. 8r``–``f. 8v``) is translated in full.

Cap. XXVI — How the First Regular Body, Called the Tetracedron or Pyramid of Four Triangular Bases, Is Formed and Placed in the Sphere.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0032 (``f. 8v``) includes diagram ``0032.g1.png``, showing the semicircle :math:`adb` cut at :math:`c` (:math:`ac = 2\,cb`) alongside the base circle and triangle of the :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>`.
- **Mathematical Identity**: For a sphere of diameter :math:`D`, the edge :math:`a_{4}` of the inscribed regular :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` satisfies :math:`D^{2} : a_{4}^{2} = 3 : 2`, or :math:`a_{4} = D\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}`.
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: The earlier draft omitted Steps 5–7 (how the perpendicular :math:`hk = ac` from the center of the circumscribed circle of radius :math:`cd` proves :math:`ke = da` via :cite:`Euclid XIII.12 <euclid:elements:XIII.12>` and :cite:`Euclid XI.4 <euclid:elements:XI.4>`).

Cap. XXVII — How the Second Regular Body, Called the Exacedron or Cube, Is Formed and Placed in the Sphere.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Campanus vs. Heiberg Ordering**: In :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s Latin *Elements*, the Cube is Proposition 14 of Book XIII and the :term:`Octahedron <octahedron>` is Proposition 15 (whereas in Heiberg's Greek text the :term:`Octahedron <octahedron>` is :cite:`Euclid XIII.14 <euclid:elements:XIII.14>` and the Cube is :cite:`Euclid XIII.15 <euclid:elements:XIII.15>`). :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` follows :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s order here and explicitly explains the reason for this transposition at the end of ``Cap. XXXI``.
- **Relation to the Tetrahedron**: Using the same semicircle :math:`adb` cut at :math:`c` with :math:`ac = 2\,cb`, the longer chord :math:`da` is the edge of the inscribed :term:`**tetrahedron** <tetrahedron>` (:math:`da^{2} = \frac{2}{3}ab^{2}`), the shorter chord :math:`db` is the edge of the inscribed **cube** (:math:`db^{2} = \frac{1}{3}ab^{2}`), and therefore the edge of the :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` is the face diagonal of the cube (:math:`da^{2} = 2\,db^{2}`).
