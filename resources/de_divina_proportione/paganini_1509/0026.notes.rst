Cap. IX — What the Root of a Number and of Any Other Quantity Is
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Terminology of Roots (*Radix discreta* vs. *Radix surda*)**: Pacioli provides a concise glossary for courtly readers unfamiliar with abacus algebra. The Latin/Italian term *radice sorda* ("deaf" or "mute" root, rendering the Arabic *jadhr asamm*, which in turn translated the Greek $\alpha\lambda\omicron\gamma\omicron\varsigma$, "without ratio" or "inexpressible") denotes an irrational square root such as $\sqrt{10}$ or $\sqrt{125}$ (:cite:`Euclid X.def.3 <euclid:elements:X.def.3>`).

Cap. X — Sequel to the First Proposed Effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. ***Restorare e rempire* (*al-jabr*)**: Notice Pacioli's technical use of the verb *restorare* ("to restore"): adding $+5$ cancels the subtracted $-5$ in $\sqrt{125} - 5$, which is the literal meaning of the Arabic *al-jabr* (*restauratio*, restoring a deficient or subtracted quantity).
2. **Correlation with Euclid Euclid XIII.1**: Algebraically, since $a = \sqrt{5\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2} - \frac{a+b}{2}$, adding $\frac{a+b}{2}$ immediately isolates $\sqrt{5}\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)$, whose square is $5\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2$. Thus :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` illuminates why :person:`Euclid <euclid>` begins Book XIII with :cite:`Euclid XIII.1 <euclid:elements:XIII.1>`: it is the geometric equivalent of completing the square on the golden ratio quadratic equation.

Cap. XI — On Its Second Essential Effect
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Converse of the First Effect (Euclid XIII.2)**: In :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* Book XIII, Proposition 2 states: "If the square on a straight line be five times the square on a segment of it, then when the double of the said segment is cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`, the greater segment is the remaining part of the original straight line." :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` formulates this directly as the converse of Chapter VII/X: if $(a + c)^2 = 5c^2$ where $a + b = 2c$, then $c = \frac{a+b}{2}$ and $a$ is the major golden segment of $a+b$.
