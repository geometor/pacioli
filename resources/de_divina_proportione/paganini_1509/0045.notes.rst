Cap. L — On the Disposition of the Octocedron: Regular (Plates XIII–XIV), Truncated (Plates XV–XVI), and Elevated (Plates XVII–XVIII).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XIII** (``0217.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Planum Solidum* <octahedron>` (:term:`Octahedron <octahedron>`, solid).
  - **Plate XIV** (``0219.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Planum Vacuum* <octahedron>` (:term:`Octahedron <octahedron>`, skeletal).
  - **Plate XV** (``0221.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Abscisum Solidum* <octahedron>` (Truncated/rectified :term:`octahedron <octahedron>`, solid).
  - **Plate XVI** (``0223.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Abscisum Vacuum* <octahedron>` (Truncated/rectified :term:`octahedron <octahedron>`, skeletal).
  - **Plate XVII** (``0225.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Elevatum Solidum* <octahedron>` (:term:`**Stella Octangula** <octahedron-elevatum>`, compound of two dual tetrahedra, solid).
  - **Plate XVIII** (``0227.rst``): :term:`*Octocedron Elevatum Vacuum* <octahedron>` (:term:`Stella Octangula <octahedron-elevatum>`, skeletal).
- **First Depiction of the *:term:`Stella Octangula <octahedron-elevatum>`***: Plates XVII and XVIII (drawn by Leonardo da Vinci in 1497 and printed in 1509) are the earliest known perspective depictions of the **stellated :term:`octahedron <octahedron>`** (later rediscovered by Johannes Kepler in *:cite:`Harmonices Mundi <kepler:harmonices-mundi>`*, 1619, who named it the *:term:`Stella Octangula <octahedron-elevatum>`*). Because the dihedral angle of an octahedron (:math:`\arccos(-\frac{1}{3}) \approx 109.47^{\circ}`) and the dihedral angle of a regular tetrahedron (:math:`\arccos(\frac{1}{3}) \approx 70.53^{\circ}`) are **supplementary** (:math:`109.47^{\circ} + 70.53^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}`), erecting regular tetrahedra on the 8 faces of a regular :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` makes the faces coplanar across the octahedral edges, forming two interpenetrating large regular tetrahedra of double the edge length!
