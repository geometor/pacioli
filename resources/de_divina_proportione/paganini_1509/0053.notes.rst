Cap. LX — On Four-Sided (Quadrilateral) Lateral Columns and the Five Species of Quadrilaterals (Plates XLVII–XLVIII).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Arabic-Latin Geometric Terminology (*Elmuaym* and *:term:`Elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*)**: In Adelard of Bath's, Gerard of Cremona's, and Campanus's Latin editions of Euclid Euclid I.Def.22, the Arabic terms *al-mu'ayyan* (rhombus) and *al-munharif* (trapezium / irregular quadrilateral) were transliterated as *helmuaym*, *simile helmuaym* (rhomboid), and *helmuariphe* (*:term:`elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*). Pacioli preserves both the Greek/Italian terms (*rombo*, *romboide*, *irregulari*) and the Campanian Arabic-Latin terms (*elmuaym*, *:term:`elmuariffe <elmuariffe>`*), illustrating each with lettered marginal woodcuts ``A, B, C, D, E`` on Folios 0053–0054.
- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XLVII** (``0285.rst``, Quadrilateral Prism, solid) and **Plate XLVIII** (``0287.rst``, Quadrilateral Prism, skeletal).

Cap. LXI — On Five-Sided (Pentagonal) Lateral Columns and How the Species of Lateral Columns Proceed to Infinity (Plates XLIX–L).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XLIX** (``0289.rst``, Pentagonal Prism, solid) and **Plate L** (``0291.rst``, Pentagonal Prism, skeletal).
- **Invocation of Euclid XIII.7**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` deftly reminds the reader why in :ref:`Chapter XXI <compendio-cap_21-en>` (:cite:`Euclid XIII.7 <euclid:elements:XIII.7>`) he proved that an equilateral pentagon requires *three* equal angles to force equiangularity (since a non-regular equilateral pentagon—such as a house-shaped pentagon—can have five equal sides and two right angles without being regular).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagrams**: Folio 0054 (``f. 19v``) includes the unit cube diagram ``0054.g7.png`` and the circle inscribed in the square :math:`abcd` with diameter :math:`ef` and center :math:`g` (``0054.g8.png``), visually showing why the circle occupies :math:`\frac{11}{14}` of the circumscribed square and the four corner spandrels occupy :math:`\frac{3}{14}`.
- **Archimedes' *Measurement of a Circle***: Proposition 3 of Archimedes' *:cite:`Dimensio Circuli <archimedes:dimensio-circuli>`* proves :math:`3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}`, whence Proposition 2 states that the area of a circle to the square on its diameter is very nearly :math:`11 : 14`.
