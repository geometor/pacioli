Cap. XXIII — How, Out of Reverence for Our Salvation, the Said Effects Conclude
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Numerological Closure ($13 = 1 + 12$) and Leonardo's *:term:`Cenacolo <simulacro>`***: Pacioli brings Section 2 (Chapters VII–XXIII, the Thirteen Effects of the Divine Proportion) to a close by linking the number **13** to the 13 Articles of the Scholastic Apostles' Creed and to Christ surrounded by the Twelve Apostles ($1 + 12 = 13$). In a masterstroke of courtly rhetoric, he ties this theological-mathematical structure directly back to Ludovico Sforza's patronage of Leonardo da Vinci's *:term:`Last Supper <simulacro>`* in the refectory of :term:`Santa Maria delle Grazie <santa-maria-delle-grazie>` (already praised in Chapters I and III), where Christ and the twelve Apostles form a visible thirteenfold harmonic composition before the viewer's eyes.

Cap. XXIV — On the Tenth Effect of Our Divine Proportion, Which Introduces the Formation of the Five Regular Bodies.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Role as Bridge to Section 3**: ``Cap. XXIV`` concludes the chain of planar theorems from Book XIII (:cite:`Euclid XIII.1 <euclid:elements:XIII.1>`–:cite:`Euclid XIII.12 <euclid:elements:XIII.12>`) by pairing :cite:`Euclid XIII.10 <euclid:elements:XIII.10>` (:math:`s_{5}^{2} = s_{6}^{2} + s_{10}^{2}`) with :cite:`Euclid XIII.9 <euclid:elements:XIII.9>` (:math:`s_{6} : s_{10} = (s_{6}+s_{10}) : s_{6}`). This Pythagorean triad of regular polygon sides (:math:`s_5, s_6, s_{10}`) is the exact geometric engine :person:`Euclid <euclid>` uses in :cite:`Euclid XIII.16 <euclid:elements:XIII.16>` to construct the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` and in :cite:`Euclid XIII.17 <euclid:elements:XIII.17>` to construct the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`.
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: The earlier ``divina_en_2`` draft reduced this chapter to a three-line summary without the corollary on :cite:`Euclid XIII.9 <euclid:elements:XIII.9>` or the numerical illustration. The present translation restores the full 1509 Paganini text (``0031.rst``, ``f. 8r``).

Cap. XXV — How It Is Not Possible for There to Be More Than Five Regular Bodies in Nature, and Why.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Source in Euclid and Campanus**: This famous classification proof comes from the concluding Scholium to :cite:`Euclid XIII.18 <euclid:elements:XIII.18>` (integrated into the proposition text in :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>`'s Latin edition as XIII.18 corollary), resting on :cite:`Euclid XI.21 <euclid:elements:XI.21>` (the sum of plane angles of any convex :term:`solid angle <angulo-solido>` is less than :math:`4R`) and :cite:`Euclid I.32 <euclid:elements:I.32>` (the interior angle sum of an :math:`n`-gon is :math:`(n-2)\cdot 2R`).
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: In ``divina_en_2/25.txt``, the angle calculations in fractions of a right angle (:math:`\frac{2}{3}R`, :math:`2\frac{2}{3}R`, :math:`3\frac{1}{3}R`, :math:`1\frac{1}{5}R`, :math:`3\frac{3}{5}R`, :math:`1\frac{1}{3}R`) were condensed into a brief table. Here the complete rhetorical and deductive structure of :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` 1509 text (``0031.rst``–``0032.rst``, ``f. 8r``–``f. 8v``) is translated in full.
