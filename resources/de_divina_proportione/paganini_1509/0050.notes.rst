Cap. LVI — On the Spherical Body and How It Is Formed (Plates XLI–XLII).
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced**:
  - **Plate XLI** (``0273.rst``): *Sperico Solido* (Solid Sphere, labeled ``XL`` in the marginal roman numeral on Folio 0049).
  - **Plate XLII** (``0275.rst``): *Sperico Vacuo* (Hollow/Armillary Sphere with meridian and parallel ribs).
- **Euclid's Genetic Definition (Euclid XI.Def.14) vs. Theodosius's Locus Definition**: Pacioli contrasts the static locus definition of the sphere (attributed in medieval Latin manuals to :person:`*Dionysius* <dionysius>` or *Theodosius*, *Sphaerica* I.Def.1) with :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s kinematic definition (:person:`Euclid <euclid>` XI.Def.14) by the revolution of a semicircle around its fixed diameter—the exact kinematic method :person:`Euclid <euclid>` uses throughout Books XI–XIII to circumscribe a sphere around each of the five Platonic solids.

Cap. LVII — How All Five Regular Bodies Are Placed in the Sphere, How Stonemasons May Carve Them from a Spherical Stone, and the Anecdote of Count Girolamo Riario and Melozzo da Forlì in Rome.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Count Girolamo Riario, Melozzo da Forlì, and Pacioli in Rome (c. 1477–1480 or 1484–1488)**: This vivid autobiographical narrative is one of the richest historical documents of Renaissance workshop culture:
  - **Count Girolamo Riario** (1443–1488), Lord of Imola and Forlì and Captain General of the Church under his uncle Pope Sixtus IV, began constructing his Roman palace near Sant'Apollinare (incorporated into **Palazzo Altemps**) in the late 1470s / early 1480s.
  - :person:`**Melozzo da Forlì** <melozzo-da-forli>` (1438–1494), the celebrated *pictor papalis* and master of *sotto in su* perspective (who also came from Riario's city of Forlì and knew :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>`), joined Pacioli in setting the geometric trap for Riario's boastful master stonemason.
- **The Tau (:math:`\mathbf{T}`) Optical Illusion**: Pacioli's remark on the two equal rods placed in the shape of a ``T`` (*doi verghette equali in forma de tau, cioè così T, inanze ali occhi suoi, ora vna ora l'altra più longa giudicaua*, Folio 0050–0051) is the **earliest documented scientific description of the Vertical-Horizontal (T) Optical Illusion** in Western history (nearly four centuries before Fick, 1851, and Oppel, 1855)!
