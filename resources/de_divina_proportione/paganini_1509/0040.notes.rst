Cap. XXXI — On the Rule and Way to Find the Sides of All Five Regular Bodies Placed in One and the Same Sphere, and Their Comparison with One Another.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0036 (``f. 10v``) contains the famous master diagram ``0036.g1.png`` of :cite:`Euclid XIII.18 <euclid:elements:XIII.18>`, displaying the semicircle with all five edges (:math:`ad` = :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>`, :math:`fb` = :term:`octahedron <octahedron>`, :math:`db` = cube, :math:`ah` = :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>`, :math:`dm` = :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`).
- **Pacioli's Observation on Campanus's Ordering ("Not Without Mystery")**: Pacioli astutely explains why :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>` placed the Cube (:cite:`Campanus XIII.14 <campanus:euclid:XIII.14>`) immediately after the :term:`Tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` (XIII.13) and before the :term:`Octahedron <octahedron>` (:cite:`Campanus XIII.15 <campanus:euclid:XIII.15>`): the single trisection point :math:`c` (:math:`ac = 2\,cb`) on diameter :math:`ab` gives the :term:`tetrahedron <tetrahedron>` edge :math:`ad` as the longer chord and the cube edge :math:`db` as the shorter chord in the very same right triangle :math:`adb`!

Cap. XXXII — On the Proportion of the Said Regular Bodies to One Another, Especially that of the Ycocedron to the Duodecedron.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Hypsicles' Book XIV**: Book XIV of the *Elements* was written by :person:`Hypsicles <hypsicles>` of Alexandria (2nd century BCE), who cites the lost treatise *On the Comparison of the Five Figures* by Aristaeus the Elder and the treatise on the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` by Apollonius of Perga.
- **Mathematical Identity**: For a sphere of diameter :math:`D`:
  - The circumradius :math:`\rho` of a face of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and of a face of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` is identical: :math:`\rho^{2} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{30} D^{2}`? Wait: :math:`a_{20}^{2} = 3\rho^{2}` and :math:`a_{20}^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{10}D^{2}`, so :math:`\rho^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{30}D^{2} = \frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}\cdot\dots` Wait: for the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`, :math:`a_{12} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\frac{D}{\sqrt{3}}`, so :math:`a_{12}^{2} = \frac{6-2\sqrt{5}}{12}D^{2} = \frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}D^{2}`. The circumradius :math:`\rho_{12}` of a regular pentagon of side :math:`a_{12}` satisfies :math:`a_{12}^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}\rho_{12}^{2}`, so :math:`\rho_{12}^{2} = \frac{2}{5-\sqrt{5}}\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}D^{2} = \frac{(5+\sqrt{5})(3-\sqrt{5})}{60}D^{2} = \frac{10 - 2\sqrt{5}}{60}D^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{30}D^{2} = \rho_{20}^{2}`!
  - Because both solids have the exact same face circumradius :math:`\rho` and therefore the exact same insphere radius :math:`r_{\text{in}} = \sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{4} - \rho^{2}} = D\sqrt{\frac{5+2\sqrt{5}}{60}}`, the ratio of their volumes equals the ratio of their surface areas: :math:`\frac{V_{20}}{V_{12}} = \frac{A_{20}}{A_{12}} = \frac{s_{\text{cube}}}{s_{20}} = \sqrt{\frac{1/3}{(5-\sqrt{5})/10}} = \sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{6}} \approx 1.09818`.

Cap. XXXIII — On the Proportion of the Surfaces and Solid Volumes of All the Regular Bodies to One Another.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Dual Pairs Sharing Inspheres**: :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s additions to Book XIV highlight one of the deepest symmetries in solid geometry: when all five Platonic solids are inscribed in the same sphere of radius :math:`R = \frac{1}{2}D`, dual solids share the **exact same insphere radius** :math:`r_{\text{in}}` and the **exact same face circumradius** :math:`\rho`:
  - **Cube (6 faces) & Octahedron (8 faces)**: :math:`r_{\text{in}} = \frac{R}{\sqrt{3}}`, :math:`\rho = R\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}`.
  - **Dodecahedron (12 faces) & Icosahedron (20 faces)**: :math:`r_{\text{in}} = R\sqrt{\frac{5+2\sqrt{5}}{15}}`, :math:`\rho = R\sqrt{\frac{10-2\sqrt{5}}{15}}`.
  - **Tetrahedron (self-dual, 4 faces)**: :math:`r_{\text{in}} = \frac{1}{3}R`, :math:`\rho = R\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}`.
- Because :math:`V = \frac{1}{3} r_{\text{in}} \cdot A`, any two dual regular solids inscribed in the same sphere have :math:`V_{1} : V_{2} = A_{1} : A_{2}`.
