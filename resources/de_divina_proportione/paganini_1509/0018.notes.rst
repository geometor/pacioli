Cap. I — Epistle on the Divine Proportion to the Most Excellent Prince Ludovico Maria Sforza Anglo, Duke of Milan, Ornament of Peace and War, by Brother Luca Pacioli of Borgo Sansepolcro of the Order of Minorites, Professor of Sacred Theology
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **The Scientific Duel of February 9, 1498**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` opens Part I with a vivid eyewitness account of a *laudabile e scientifico duello* (an academic disputation) held on February 9, 1498 in the Castello Sforzesco of Milan before Duke :person:`Ludovico Maria Sforza <ludovico-sforza>` ("il Moro", 1452–1508). This courtly gathering brings together Franciscan Scholastic theologians, military commanders, court physicians, astrologers, jurists, and Renaissance artist-engineers.
2. **Franciscan & Courtly Prosopography**:
   - :person:`**Magister Gometius** <gometius>`: :person:`Gomez de Lisboa <gometius>` (d. 1513), Portuguese Franciscan theologian at Pavia and Milan, later Vicar General of the Conventual Franciscans.
   - :person:`**Domenico Ponzone** <domenico-ponzone>` and :person:`**Francesco Busti** <francesco-busti>`: Prominent Milanese Franciscan preachers and regents of the Convent of :person:`San Francesco <francis-of-assisi>` Grande.
   - :person:`**Galeazzo Sanseverino** <galeazzo-sanseverino>` (c. 1460–1525): Son-in-law of :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`, Captain General of the Milanese armies, and :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` personal patron in Milan (to whom one of the three manuscript copies of :cite:`*De Divina Proportione* <pacioli:divina-proportione>` was presented).
   - **Court Physicians & Astrologers**: :person:`Ambrogio Varese da Rosate <ambrogio-da-rosate>` (court astrologer and physician to :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>`), :person:`Aloisio Marliani <aloisio-marliani>`, :person:`Gabriele Pirovano <gabriele-pirovano>`, :person:`Nicolò Cusano <nicolo-cusano>` (a Milanese physician, not the Cardinal Nicholas of Cusa who died in 1464), and :person:`Andrea da Novara <andrea-da-novara>`.
3. **Leonardo da Vinci's Works in Milan**:
   - **The Sforza Equestrian Monument**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` records the exact dimensions of :person:`Leonardo's <leonardo>` colossal clay model for the monument to :person:`Francesco Sforza <francesco-sforza>`: 12 Milanese *braccia* from the horse'spoll (*cervice*, misprinted *cerulee* in 1509) to the ground, which :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` calibrates directly against a printed reference segment :g:`ab` on Folio 0017r ($37\frac{4}{5}$ times :g:`ab`), and an estimated bronze casting weight of 200,000 Milanese *libbre* (at 12 ounces per pound).
   - **The Last Supper (*:term:`Cenacolo <simulacro>`*)**: Described as *el ligiadro de l'ardente desiderio de nostra salute :term:`simulacro <simulacro>`* ("the graceful simulacrum of the ardent desire for our salvation", alluding to Luke 22:15, *Desiderio desideravi hoc pascha manducare vobiscum*) in the refectory of :term:`Santa Maria delle Grazie <santa-maria-delle-grazie>`.
   - **Leonardo's Treatises**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` provides priceless documentary testimony that by 1498 :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>` had already completed a treatise on painting and human movements (:cite:`*libro de pictura e movimenti humani* <leonardo:pittura>`) and was actively writing a treatise on mechanics: local motion, percussion, weights, and accidental forces (*del moto locale, dele percussioni e pesi e dele forze tutte*).
   - :person:`**Giacomo Andrea da Ferrara** <giacomo-andrea>`: Architect and commentator on :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`, closely bound to :person:`Leonardo <leonardo>` as a brother (*suo quanto fratello*, misprinted *quarto fratello* in the 1509 edition, as :person:`Winterberg <winterberg>` notes). He was executed by the French in May 1500 after the fall of Milan.
4. **Epistemological Primacy of Mathematics**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` cites :person:`Averroes <averroes>`'s commentary on :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`'s :cite:`*Metaphysics* <aristotle:met:II>` II (Book $\alpha$, comm. 14–16): mathematical demonstrations occupy the "first degree of certainty" (:cite:`*in primo gradu certitudinis* <averroes:met:II.16>`), whereas natural philosophy and medicine (exemplified by the classical controversy between :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`/:person:`Avicenna <avicenna>` on the heart, :person:`Galen <galen>` on the brain/liver, and Empedocles/Critias on the blood as the seat of life) remain subject to probable dispute.

Cap. II — Preface of the Reverend Father Magister Luca Pacioli of Borgo Sansepolcro, of the Order of Minorites and Professor of Sacred Theology, to the Compendium on the Divine Proportion drawn from the Mathematical Disciplines
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Dantean and Petrarchan Echoes**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` weaves vernacular literary classics directly into his Scholastic Latin-Italian prose:
   - *el maistro de color ch'sano* ("the Master of those who know", :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`) is a direct quotation of Dante, *Inferno* IV.131.
   - *l'uno pensier da l'altro scoppia* ("one thought bursts forth from another") quotes Dante, *Inferno* XXIII.10.
   - *la gola, sonno e otiose piume* ("gluttony, sleep, and idle feather-beds") quotes the opening line of Petrarch's famous sonnet *La gola e 'l sonno e l'oziose piume Hanno del mondo ogni vertù sbandita* (*Rerum vulgarium fragmenta* VII).
2. **Material Models of the Polyhedra**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` explicitly states that he fashioned with his own hands (*de mia propria mano materialmente*) three-dimensional models of the regular and dependent polyhedra to accompany the treatise in the Ducal Library at Pavia/Milan. In May 1506, the Venetian Republic recorded :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` presentation of three sets of sixty polyhedra fashioned in lightweight wood.
3. **Roberto Valturio and the Urbino Frieze**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` links classical military engineering (:person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` at Syracuse, Caesar's Rhine bridge, :person:`Vegetius <vegetius>`, :person:`Frontinus <frontinus>`) to :person:`Roberto Valturio <roberto-valturio>`'s *De re militari* (written c. 1455 for :person:`Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta <sigismondo-malatesta>` of Rimini, printed at Verona in 1472) and the celebrated stone bas-reliefs (*Formelle dell'Arte della Guerra*, carved by Ambrogio Barocci after designs by Francesco di Giorgio Martini) that adorned the bench-frieze around the facade of :person:`Federico da Montefeltro <federico-da-montefeltro>`'s Ducal Palace in Urbino.
4. **Duns Scotus, Bartolus of Sassoferrato, and Euclid**:
   - **John Duns Scotus** (*Ordinatio* / *Opus Oxoniense* II, dist. 2, p. 2, q. 5–6) used geometric arguments on the divisibility of the continuum from :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* to analyze angelic location (*ubi angelicum*) and motion.
   - :person:`**Bartolus of Sassoferrato** <bartolus>` (1313–1357) composed his treatise *De fluminibus seu Tyberiadis* (1355) while watching the bends of the Tiber near Perugia; as :person:`Bartolus <bartolus>` himself records in his prologue, he was assisted in the geometric division of alluvial islands and riverbeds by the Franciscan theologian and mathematician **Guido da Perpignano / Guido de Perusio**, citing :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements*.
5. **Pacioli's Public Chair of Mathematics in Milan**: Appointed by :person:`Ludovico Sforza <ludovico-sforza>` in 1496, :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` notes at the end of Chapter II that he has just completed lecturing publicly through Books I–X of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* (incorporating practical algebra and arithmetic alongside :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s theoretical propositions) and is now devoting the remainder of his term to Books XI–XV and :cite:`*De Divina Proportione* <pacioli:divina-proportione>`.
