Cap. VI.

Of its worthy recommendation.

This cur proportion, Exalted Duke, is worthy of such
preference and distinction, as one can generally only say considering
its infinite power, insofar as
without its knowledge very many things most worthy of admiration
could never come to light neither in philosophy nor in any other
science, which gift
certainly has been granted to it by the unchangeable nature of the higher basic
elements, as our great philosopher Cam-
panus, the highly famous Mathematician says on the tenth of the
fourteenth. Especially if one sees, that it is the one,
which brings so many varieties of solid bodies, regarding
size as well as quantity of their surfaces, as well as of figures
and forms, with a certain irrational symphony
into accord among themselves: as one in our progress
at the citation of the astonishing effccts will understand,
which (in a line divided according to it) must not be called natural,
but truly divine. Of these
