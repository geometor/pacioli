Cap. XLVIII. *)

Of the Shape and Arrangement of the plane, solid and hollow  and of the truncated solid, plane or hollow, and of the  elevated solid or hollow Tetrahedron.

I. II. The plane solid or hollow Tetrahedron is formed by six
equal lines, which contain twelve plane angles and four
corporeal corners, and form among each other four three-sided,
equilateral and equiangular base surfaces.

III. IV. Of the headless or truncated Tetrahedron:
The headless or, we will say truncated solid,
plane and hollow Tetrahedron is contained by 18 lines,
which generate 36 plane angles and twelve corners and eight
surfaces surround it, whereof four Hexagons and four equi-
lateral Triangles, i.e. of six sides, **) but the material
form makes it clear to our eye and it arises from the pre-
ceding thereby, that its sides are all cut off to a third.

V. VI. The elevated, or we will say pointed
solid and hollow Tetrahedron has likewise 18 edges, of
which six are common, and has 36 plane angles and
eight corners, of which four the peaks ***) of the surface
forming Pyramids, and four to the five pyramids com-
mon, namely to the inner, which the eye cannot see,
rather only the understanding perceives, and to the other four
outer, of which five pyramids named body is com-
posed, so far they are among themselves equilateral, triangular
and equiangular, as its presented material form
shows us. And of its surfaces, which clothe it, which
are not properly called Bases, are in Total of the
number twelve and all triangular. And of this one can
on no way determine the elevated truncated, from lack
of the Hexagons, which form no corporeal corners. +)
