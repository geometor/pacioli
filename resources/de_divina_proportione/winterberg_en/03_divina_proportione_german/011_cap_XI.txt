Cap. XI.

Of its second essential effect.

If a magnitude is divided into two parts and to the
one a magnitude is added, so that the square of this
sum is the fivefold of the square of the added magnitude,
so follows with necessity, that the said added
magnitude be the half of the first magnitude decomposed into the two parts,
and that the [part], to which it [was] added, [be] its
greater segment, and that it [be] the whole divided in them according to our
proportion. E.g. one take 15 - sqrt(125) and
sqrt(125) - 5 for the two complete segments of a magnitude and
add to the one of both namely sqrt(125) - 5 the number 5
as third magnitude, then the sum is = sqrt(125) whose
square 125 and the square of the added magnitude is 25.
Therefore 125 is the fivefold of 25, the square of the ad-
ded magnitude. I say, that sqrt(25) namely 5 [be] the half of the
first magnitude divided into those two segments and that to which
one added it, [be] the greater segment of said
first magnitude divided according to our proportion, which has a mean and two
extreme segments namely of 10.
And that is the conversion of the preceding effect, as
the second of the thirteenth concludes geometrically.
