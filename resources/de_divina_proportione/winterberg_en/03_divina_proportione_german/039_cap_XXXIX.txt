Cap. XXXIX.

Of the formation of the Dodecahedron in the Icosahedron.

The Icosahedron has, as said, twelve corporeal corners, each
contained in five plane angles of its five triangles. If one therefore
wants to describe the Dodecahedron in the same, one must
first according to that, what we have taught previously in this Tractate,
form the in question Icosahedron, and after it accordingly
ordinarily produced, one find according to the fif-
th of the fourth of each of its triangular surfaces the midpoint,
and these *) we connect then all among each other by thirty
straight lines such, that necessarily twelve pentagons
form themselves, whose each lies opposite to a corporeal corner of the said Icosahedron.
And each of the sides of the named Pentagons
stands crosswise opposite to each of the corresponding sides of named
Icosahedron. And just as in the named Icosahedron twelve
corners present, so there are in the Dodecahedron twelve Pentagons.
And as in those twenty triangular surfaces are found, so are in the
named Dodecahedron twenty corporeal corners present, which
were generated in said surfaces by means of the named lines.
And as in that thirty edges present, so there are in the
Dodecahedron likewise thirty to those crosswise opposite
edges, as said, as its whole form reveals,
and as also the sixth of the fifteenth concludes.

*) seil. midpoints.
