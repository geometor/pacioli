Cap. XIV.

Of its fifth wonderful effect.

If a magnitude is divided according to our said proportion,
so is always the sum of the square of the smaller
segment and of the square of the whole magnitude the triple
of the square of the greater segment. Let be e.g. ten the
divided magnitude as we have said, so that the one segment
15 - sqrt(125), i.e. the smaller, and the other sqrt(125) - 5 be,
i.e. the greater. I say, that, if one adds the square of
15 - sqrt(125) to the square of ten, the whole magnitude,
their sum will be triple, i.e. three times as large as the
square of the greater segment, i.e. sqrt(125) - 5.
Accordingly is the square of 15 - sqrt(125) = 350 - sqrt(112500)
and the square of ten is 100, which added to 350 - sqrt(112500)
yields 450 - sqrt(112500) for the said sum. And
the square of sqrt(125) - 5 is 150 - sqrt(112500), which makes 1/3 of the
said sum, as is evident. For if one multiplies
150 - sqrt(112500) with three, it makes exactly:
450 - sqrt(112500). Thus is the said sum the triple
of the said square, as we asserted. This effect
concludes the fifth of the 13th geometrically.
