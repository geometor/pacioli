Cap. XIII.

Of its fourth inexpressible effect.

If a magnitude is divided according to our divine proportion
and one adds to the whole magnitude its greater seg-
ment, so will said sum and said
greater segment be parts of another likewise divided magnitude. And the greater segment of this second so divided
magnitude will always be the whole first named magnitude. Let be
e.g. the magnitude divided according to our unique proportion
10 so that its greater segment will be sqrt(125) - 5, the smaller
15 - sqrt(125). If one therefore adds to the first magnitude
10 the greater segment, sqrt(125) - 5, so will result
a second, namely sqrt(125) + 5. And of this second
magnitude sqrt(125) + 5 I assert, that it is likewise divided in the
two said segments according to our proportion
namely in sqrt(125) - 5, the greater part of the first, and in
10, which was the first magnitude, and is the greater segment
of this second magnitude. And this becomes clear thus: because the pro-
duct of sqrt(125) - 5 (which is the greater segment of the first, and
now the smaller of this second is) with this whole second namely
with sqrt(125) + 5 amounts to as much as the square of the mean, or we
will say greater segment of this second, which is 10,
so that both make exactly 100 as it is required for
said proportion. This property proves to us
also geometrically the fourth of the 13th.
