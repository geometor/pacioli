Cap. XVII.

Of the reverse effect of the preceding.

If a line is divided according to the proportion, which
has a mean and two extreme segments, so is always
in the circle for which the greater segment is the side of the
hexagon inscribed in it, the smaller the corresponding
decagon side. If e.g. the divided line were ten, so
will the greater segment, which is sqrt(125) - 5, always be the side
of the hexagon of a circle, whose diameter will be the
double of sqrt(125) - 5, namely sqrt(500) - 10. I as-
sert, that in the same circle the smaller segment 15 - sqrt(125)
be the side of the equilateral decagon inscribed in the
same. And this conversion uses frequently Ptole-
my in the ninth Chapter of the first theorem of his Almagest,
to demonstrate the size of the chords of circle arcs. As
likewise in the aforementioned ninth of the 13th is clearly
proven geometrically.
