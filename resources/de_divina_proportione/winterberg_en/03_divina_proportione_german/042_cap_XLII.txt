Cap. XLII.

How the Octahedron be formed in the Dodecahedron.

If in the Dodecahedron first the Cube is described,
as in the Preceding has been said, so will in the
said Dodecahedron easily the Octahedron be formed. For
we will halve the six opposite sides of the Dodecahedron,
which lie opposite to the six surfaces of the Cube,
i.e. those sides, which form so to speak the summit of the
bolt-shaped [prism], of which are exactly six, and those their six
midpoints we connect all among each other by twelve
straight lines such, that they will generate six corporeal corners,
whose each is enclosed by four plane angles of the four triangles
of the Octahedron. And each meets one of the na-
med six edges of the Dodecahedron, and consequently shows itself,
that it be the demanded Enclosed, as contained in the ninth of the
fifteenth.
