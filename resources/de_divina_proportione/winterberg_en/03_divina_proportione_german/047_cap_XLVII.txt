Cap. XLVII.

How in each of the named regular bodies the sphere be formed.

Previously, Exalted Duke, we have, as seen, proven
of each of the named five regular bodies,
that it be inscribable into the presented sphere and circumscribable by this;
it remains now appropriately to show,
how also the said sphere let itself be inscribed in each of the same.
Therefore we will here in the Following present with evident clear-
ness, that also conversely the sphere can be inscribed in each of
them. This illuminates so: it may thus go out
from the Center of the sphere, which circumscribes every such body,
to all boundary surfaces of the same perpendiculars
or be felled. These will necessarily fall into the
Centers of the circles, which we exactly circumscribe to the said surfaces.
And so far all circles, which enclose the named
surfaces exactly, are equal, these perpen-
diculars will be equal. If we therefore, corresponding to the size of one of
them, describe a circle about the center of the sphere,
which comprises them, and lead its semicircle
around, until it returns to the place, from where it began
to move, so we will, since it necessari-
ly has to go through all endpoints of all perpendiculars,
in consequence of the Corollary of the fifteenth of the third convince us, that
the sphere described by the movement of this radius
graze or touch exactly all surfaces of the designated body in the meeting
point of the perpendiculars. For the sphere can
touch not more of the base surfaces of the body, as the
semicircle touched, when it moved. Therefore is clear, that
we have inscribed the designated body the sphere,
as it was proposed to do.
