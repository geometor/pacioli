Of the four-sided angular Columns.

XLIII. XLVI. Of the angular is the second sort four-sided,
and indeed are it those, which have two on said manner four-sided Bases,
and four other surfaces, which surround them, are like-
wise four-sided, equally far from each other removed, according as
they stand opposite to each other, and these are likewise soon equal,
soon inequilateral, corresponding to the nature of their Base surfaces.
For of the plane four-sided rectilinear figures
determine themselves four kinds: the one called Square, is that,
which has all sides equal, and as angles rights,
as here adjacent the figure A A) The other, called
elongated Tetragon, is that, which has equal opposite sides and of equal kind
right angles, but is longer than wide, as the adja-
cent figure B. The third sort is called Elmuaym,
which is an equilateral but not rectangular figure,
and is called with other name Rhombus, as here the
Figure C. The fourth figure is called to the Elmuaym similar or
Rhomboid with other name, where only its oppo-
site sides equal to one another and among themselves of equal distance,
and it has no right angles, as the Figure D reveals.
All other figures except these, which are four-sided, are
called Elmuariffe, i.e. irregular, as it are the recor-
ded Figures E. Now can, according to all these Diffe-
rences of the base surfaces, the said four-
sided columns change. But how one also wants, always must
one understand the equal distance of their base surfaces as height
thereby. And just these we can according to the kind
of their base surfaces call regular, and the remaining irregular
or Elmuariffe.
