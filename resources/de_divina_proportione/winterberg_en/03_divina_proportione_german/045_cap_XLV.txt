Cap. XLV.

Of the Art and Manner, to form the Tetrahedron in the Icosahedron.

If one forms the Cube in the said Icosahedron, as
we taught before, and then forms the Tetrahedron in just this Cube,
so is no doubt, that then necessarily the latter
will also be inscribed in the said Icosahedron. For since the
corporeal corners of the three-sided four-faced pyramid touch
those of the Cube, and those of the Cube those of the Icosahedron, so follows
from the First to the Last, that those of the Tetrahedron
likewise touch those of the Icosahedron. *) And therefire is in consequence of the twelfth
of the fifteenth our proposed body ended. And this
regarding that, which refers itself to their mutual inscription.

*) The corners of the cube must fall into the middle of the triangular surface of the Icosahedron (cfr. Cap. XLIV).
