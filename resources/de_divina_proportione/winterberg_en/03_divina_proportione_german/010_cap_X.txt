Cap. X.

Consequence of the first presented effect.

Let us return now, after these things well noted, to
its first stated effect, and make ourselves clear about the same
with distinct examples and let the same case of ten be resumed for its il-
lumination, which presented in that place, without struggling further
with other troublesome magnitudes, since with each always the same occurs, what is said in the
present. And for more complete insight of Your
Highness we will follow by means of Arithmetic all others,
presupposing however that the scientific
proofs of all that which our progress will contain, are produced
in the places, which we will cite from our Philosopher Euclid,
with all care geometrically according to the necessary
requirement of the conclusions. I say therefore,
that, if one divides ten according to our proportion, and the
greater segment is sqrt(125) - 5, if one adds to this, corresponding to the
said effect, five, i.e. the half of the
whole magnitude ten, that this makes exactly sqrt(125),
for this number, less five completes and perfects itself
by means of plus five, the half of ten. This sum, i.e.
sqrt(125), multiplied with itself which makes 125 for its square,
is five times as much as the square of the half of ten which [is]
five, and whose square is 25. Therefore 125 is exactly the fivefold
of the said 25, square of the said half of ten, as has been
said. And this effect takes place with every magnitude, of whatever
kind it be, as evidently the first of the 13th of our
guide shows.
