Cap. IX.

What the root of a number and other magnitude be.

And since it will often happen in the progress of our work,
to name roots, so it seems to me here in place, to explain briefly,
what it means, although in our work
has been spoken extensively about it in every way. Never-
theless I say, the root of a magnitude is itself a
magnitude, which multiplied with itself yields that magnitude,
as whose root it itself was designated. As we say
the root of nine is three, and of 16 is four, and of
25 five and so with the others, and 9, 16, 25 are
called Squares. And in reference hereto is to be known, that there
are some magnitudes, which have no roots such, that one
could state them determined by a number. As for ten
no number exists, which, multiplied with itself, yields exactly ten,
and likewise regarding 11, 12, 13, and other similar
numbers. And thus there result and arise roots of two
kinds, the one called discrete, or we will say rational and
is that, which can be expressed by a determined number,
as of nine the root is three. And the other is
called deaf [surd], and is that, which cannot be reproduced exactly
by a number, as we have said of the sqrt(10) and others.
And these are called with other names irrational,
insofar as all those magnitudes, which cannot be expressed by a
determined number, are called in science irra-
tional, and those, which let themselves be reproduced by a number,
are called rational. And this be enough for
our sentence regarding the root.
