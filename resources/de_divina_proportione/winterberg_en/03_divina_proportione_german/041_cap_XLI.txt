Cap. XLI.

Of the insertion of the Cube into the Dodecahedron.

The Cube further we will easily form in said Dode-
cahedron, in consideration of that, that this forms itself on the twelve
sides of the Cube, as it is contained in the seventeenth of the thir-
teenth. If one accordingly in each of its twelve
Pentagons according to the requirement of the Said draws twelve
chords, so will without doubt six quadratic equi-
lateral surfaces be formed and to each of the same will two
corners of the named Dodecahedron stand opposite, and it
will be formed from eight of the same eight corners of the inscribed
Cube, such, that over each of the Cube surfaces almost
the shape of the bolt-shaped body *) remains, what all
becomes clear through the eighth of the fifteenth.

*) Under this designation is in the Following always to be understood the three-sided Prism.
