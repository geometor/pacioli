Cap. XXXVI.

Of the inclusion of the Octahedron in the Cube.

If one further wants to form the Eight-surfaced-one, i.e. Octahedron in the
Hexahedron, so one must first have constructed in the Cube the equi-
lateral three-sided pyramid, whose sides, as
has been said, are the six diagonals of its surfaces.
If we therefore halve each of the named diagonals, and
will connect the midpoints by straight lines among each other,
so will be without doubt in the assumed Cube exactly
the Octahedron produced, and each of its corners will
find itself according to the third of the fifteenth exactly in the bases of the na-
med Cube.
