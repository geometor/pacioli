Cap. VIII.

We must know, that these well-noted things, to divide a
magnitude according to the proportion, which has a mean and
two extreme segments, wants to say, to make from it two unequal
parts such, that the product of the smaller into the
whole said undivided magnitude be just as large as the square of the
greater segment, as according to the third definition of the
sixth our Philosopher explains. And if he therefore ever
once in the case should use the naming: "Divide the said magnitude according to the
proportion, which has a mean and two extreme segments",
but should say only, the case,
to form two parts from it according to the condition, that the
product of the one into the whole said magnitude is equal
to the square of the other segment so must he, who understands it
well and is experienced in the art, lead the presented theorem back
to our said proportion, for otherwise it can
not be interpreted, e.g. if Someone said: "Make
me from ten two parts such, that the one, multiplied by 10,
yields just as much, as the other multiplied by itself.
This case and others similar are found if one proceeds according to
the instructions, which have been laid down by us in the speculative
practice, Algebra and Almucantabala, designated with other name
the Rule of the Thing, and in the work published by us,
solved that namely the one
part the smaller be 15 - sqrt(125) and the other greater
sqrt(125) - 5. The so described parts are irrational and
call themselves with technical term Residuals. Of their genera
our Philosopher states in the 79th of the tenth, that there are
six of them. And in general the said parts are brought forth
as follows the smaller if he says: 15 - sqrt(125),
That namely wants to say: After one has taken sqrt(125), which
is little more than 11, and subtracted from 15, that little more
than three remains, or we will say little less than 4. And
the greater results: sqrt(125) - 5. And that wants to say, after
one has taken the sqrt(125) which, as said, is little more
than 11 and subtracted five from this, that little more
than six, or we will say, little less than seven remains for
the said greater segment. But similar
acts of multiplying, adding, subtracting and dividing
binomial residuals and roots and all other rational
and irrational, whole and fractional magnitudes of every kind,
I do not care to repeat in this work, since
I have presented them completely in our aforementioned work,
and care is taken only, to say new things,
and not to repeat the already said. And
with that we have at the division of every magnitude always three expressions ordered
according to continuous proportionality such, that
the one represents the whole so divided magnitude, i.e. the greater seg-
ment, as here in the presented case ten, and the
other forms the greater part namely the mean segment,
as it is sqrt(125) - 5, the third smallest amounts to 15 - sqrt(125).
