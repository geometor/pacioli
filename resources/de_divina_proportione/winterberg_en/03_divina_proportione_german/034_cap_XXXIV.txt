Cap. XXXIV.

Of the mutual enclosure of the five regular bodies, and how many cases there be in total and why?

It follows now to explain, how the one of these five
essential, i.e. regular bodies be contained by the other,
and with which this is the case, and with which not; and
why. First now, to speak of the Tetrahedron, shows itself, that it
on no way can take up any other body in itself,
than the Octahedron, i.e. the body of eight three-sided surfaces
and six corners; because with them neither sides, nor surfaces, with
angles present, into which the sides of the Cube neither with
their corner points nor with their side surfaces in such a way could be
fitted, that they touch evenly, as their
real inscription requires and as its material
form shows us to the eye, and as it through true science
in the first of the fifteenth becomes evident. Nor also is valid
this for any one of the two others, namely Icosahedron and
Dodecahedron. If we now wanted to inscribe the named Octahedron in the na-
med Four-surfaced-one or Tetrahedron or form exactly,
we will do it on this way, namely: First
we will make the named Tetrahedron, as we have taught before.
And after we have so produced that, then we will
halve each of its edges, and connect the midpoints of these all
among each other by straight lines. If this happened,
so we have undoubtedly fitted the named body exactly
into that in such a way, that its six corners are supported by the
edges of the named Tetrahedron in equal way.
This will show the material experience, and the second
of the fifteenth manifests it.
