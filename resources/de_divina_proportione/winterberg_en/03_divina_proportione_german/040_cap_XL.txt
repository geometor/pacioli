Cap. XL.

Of the insertion of the Icosahedron into the Dodecahedron.

If one wants to inscribe the Icosahedron to the Dodecahedron,
so we will form beforehand the latter according to the previously in this
given instruction. And of its twelve Pentagons,
which enclose it, we will find the center, accor-
dingly, as the fourteenth of the fourth teaches. And these centers
we will connect among each other by means of thirty lines, so
that in the same twenty triangles and twelve corners will arise,
whose each is contained by five plane angles of named tri-
angles. The corners of the same lie in the twelve
midpoints of its twelve Pentagons. And in similar way
stand these its thirty lines to the thirty of the Dodecahedron
crosswise opposite, as it of those regarding these has been said
and further from the seventh of the said fifteenth
becomes clear.
