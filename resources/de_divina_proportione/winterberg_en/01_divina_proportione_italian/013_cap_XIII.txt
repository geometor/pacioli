Cap. XIII.

(Of its fourth ineffable effect.)

If a quantity is divided according to our divine proportion if to that whole quantity is added its greater part then said conjoint and said greater part will be parts of another quantity so divided. And the greater part of this second quantity so divided will always be the whole first quantity. Verbi gratia. Let the quantity divided according to our unique proportion be 10 that its greater part will be R 125 minus 5, and the smaller 15 minus R 125. Wherefore if above 10 first quantity is placed R 125 minus 5 greater part it will make a second that is R 125 plus 5. And this second quantity that is R 125 plus 5 I say to be similarly divided according to our proportion in the said two parts: that is in R 125 minus 5 greater [part] of the first and in 10 which was the first quantity and will be the greater part of this second quantity. And this appears so. Because the product of R 125 minus 5 (which was the greater part of the first and now will be the smaller of this second) in all this second, that is in R 125 plus 5 makes as much as the square of the mean or we want to say greater part of this second which is 10. that the one and the other make precisely 100. as is required to said proportion. Which force also the fourth of the thirteenth manifests to us geometrically.
