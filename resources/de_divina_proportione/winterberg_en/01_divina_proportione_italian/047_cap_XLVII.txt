Cap. XLVII.

(How in each of the said regulars the sphere is formed.)

Above as seen exalted Duke we have demonstrated each of said 5 regular bodies to be in the proposed sphere inscribable and by that circumscribable remains now conveniently to show how also the said sphere [in] each of them can be inscribed. Which here following we will adduce with evident clarity reciprocally the sphere in each of them to be able to be inscribed. The which thing so appears. Because from the center of the sphere which circumscribes each of these such bodies to all as many the bases of each of them issue or be drawn the perpendiculars. The which of necessity will fall inside the centers of the circles which we circumscribe exactly [to] said bases. And since that all the circles which exactly surround said bases be equal will be these perpendiculars equal. Wherefore if after the quantity of one of them we will circumscribe the circle above the center of the sphere that circumscribes them and its semicircle we will turn around it will be so much that it returns to the place whence it commenced to move. Because it will be necessary that it pass through all the extremities of all the perpendiculars we will convince by the corollary of the 15. of the 3. the sphere described by the motion of this semicircle to touch or exactly touch all the bases of the assigned body in the concourse of the perpendiculars. Because the sphere cannot more touch of the bases of the body than the semicircle touched when it moved. Wherefore will be manifest we to have inscribed the sphere to the signed body just as was proposed to do.
