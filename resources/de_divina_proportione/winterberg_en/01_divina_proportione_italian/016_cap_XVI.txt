Cap. XVI.

(Of its seventh inestimable effect.)

If the side of the equilateral hexagon be joined to the side of the equilateral decagon which both are understood described in a same circle. And their sum always will be a quantity divided according to said our proportion. And its greater part will be the side of the hexagon. Verbi gratia. Let the side of an equilateral hexagon in the marked circle be R. 125 minus 5. And the side of the equilateral decagon in the same circle be 15 minus R. 125. Of which circle the diameter will be R. 500 minus 10. I say that the sum of R. 125 minus 5. with 15 minus R. 125 which will be 10 to be divided according to our proportion. and its greater part will be R. 125 minus 5. and the smaller 15 minus R. 125. as several times was said to divide 10. And this will be manifest by the 9. of the 13. geometrically.
