Cap. L.

(Of the octahedron plane solid or true vacuum and abscinded solid or true vacuum and of the elevated solid or true vacuum.)

XV. XVI. The octahedron plane solid or true vacuum receives in itself 12 lines and 24 superficial angles and of solid has 6. and will be contained by 8 equilateral triangular bases and likewise equiangular as in its own material form to us presents itself.

XVII. XVIII. The octahedron abscinded or true cut plane solid or true vacuum has lines 36. that make 72 superficial angles that is 48 are of the hexagons and 24 of the squares and contains 24 solid and has 14 bases of which 8 are hexagonal that is of 6 sides and 6 of them are tetragonal that is square. But of said lines 24 of them are common that is to the squares and to the hexagons. And those such squares are formed from the hexagons when uniform all 8 touch themselves which of all the eye in the form its material clear to the intellect the truth makes known. And of this also is not possible [that] be formed its elevated that uniform presents itself for the defect similarly of the hexagons which as of the abscinded tetrahedron was said is not possible that they cause solid angle and form itself from the preceding in the third part of each its side uniform cut.

XIX. XX. The octahedron elevated solid or true vacuum has 36 lines of equal length and has 72 superficial angles and 8 solid pyramidal. And will be contained by 24 surfaces all equilateral trigonal and equiangular which exactly surround it. But of those lines 12 of them are common to all the triangles of the pyramids. And this such body composed of 8 laterated triangular equilateral pyramids of same height which all from outside appear, and also of the intrinsic octahedron by only imagination of the intellect perceptible of which octahedron the bases are bases of said 8 pyramids. As the form its material to us makes manifest.
