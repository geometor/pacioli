Cap. LXIII.

(Of the mode to know to measure all the laterated columns.)

XLV. XLVI. Shown the mode to the dimension of the round follows that of the laterated. For the which similarly this be general rule and with precision, that is that always be squared one of its bases which one wants and that which makes then be multiplied in the height or true length of said column. And this last product exactly will be its corporal mass or true capacity. And be they of how many one wants faces and never fails. As verbi gratia. be the laterated tetragonal column ab. the which be high 10. and its bases each be 6 for every verso. I say that be squared first one of said bases, that for being equilateral will multiply one of [the] sides in itself that is 6 in 6 makes 36. and this exactly will be the space of the base. Now I say that this be multiplied in the height or true length of all said column that is in 10 will make 360. And so many braccia or true feet exactly will be square [cubic?] said column, in mode that above of the round is said. And so if its bases were inequilateral or otherwise irregular pure according to the norms given by us in the said work always be squared and in their height the product be multiplied. And one will have the question infallibly in each. And for expedition of all the others this same rule must be observed or be they trigonal, or pentagonal or hexagonal or trueheptagonal. et sic de singulis. that is that according to the exigency of their bases those must first be measured. If they are triangular by the rule of the triangles, and if pentagonal by the rules of pentagons, and if hexagonal similarly Of the which forms and figures the rules diffuse in said our work are assigned. to the which for being easy the access for their copious multitude printed and by the universe already divulged here I do not care otherwise to adduce them and so to said columns we will place end and following we will say of their pyramids.
