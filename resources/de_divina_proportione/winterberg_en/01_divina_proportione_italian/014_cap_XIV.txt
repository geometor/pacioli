Cap. XIV.

(Of its fifth admirable effect.)

If a quantity be divided according to our said proportion always the conjoint [sum] of the square of the smaller part with the square of the whole integral quantity will be triple the square of the greater part. Verbi gratia. Let 10 be the divided quantity: as we have said that one part be 15 minus R. 125. that is the smaller and the other R. 125 minus 5. that is the greater. I say that the square of 15 minus R. 125. joined with the square of 10. whole quantity and their sum will be triple that is three times the square of the greater part that is of R. 125. minus 5. Wherefore the square of 15 minus R. 125. is 350 minus R. 112500. to the square of 10 is 100. which joined with 350 minus R. 112500 make 450 minus R. 112500 for said sum. And the square of R. 125 minus 5. is 150 minus R. 12500 which will be the 1/3 of said sum as appears. Because multiplying 150 minus R. 12500. by 3 will make precisely* 450 minus R. 112500. Therefore said sum will be triple to said square just as we said. Which effect geometrically concludes the fifth of the 13.
