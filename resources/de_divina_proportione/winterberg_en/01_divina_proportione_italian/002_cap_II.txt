Cap. II.

(Preface of the Reverend Father Master Luca Pacioli of Borgo San Sepolcro of the Order of Minors. And professor of sacred theology in the compendium on the divine proportion from the mathematical disciplines.)

Propter admirari ceperunt philosophari. Your Exalted Duke wants the proper authority of the master of those who know that from seeing knowing had its beginning. As the same affirms in another place saying. Quod nihil est in intellectu quin prius sit in sensu. That is that nothing be in the intellect which has not first offered itself in some way to the sense. Etc. [Of] our senses by the wise seeing is concluded [to be] more noble. Wherefore not unmeritedly also by the vulgar the eye is said to be the first door through which the intellect understands and tastes. As is contained in that place the priests of Egypt seeing the moon eclipse stood much admiring and seeking the cause to find that naturally happening by true science by the interposition of the earth between the sun and the moon of which they remained satisfied. And from then until now their successors refining themselves hand in hand with the light of the 5 intellectual windows filled for our utility an innumerable multitude of volumes of their profound sciences. Because just as one thought bursts from another so were born from that many others then. Which thing thinking to myself I elected to this most useful compendium of mathematical sciences...
