Cap. XLVI.

(Why said inscriptions cannot be more.)

Wherefore exalted Duke by the things discoursed is manifest that being 5 the regular bodies if each in each duly as is presupposed could be formed it would follow that everyone would receive 4. And by consequence among all would come to be 20 inscriptions. that is 4 times 5. But because everyone does not receive everyone as is adduced there are not if not 12 inscriptions. That is one only of the octahedron in the tetrahedron. And two in the cube that is of the tetrahedron and of the octahedron. And two also in the octahedron that is one of the cube. And one of the tetrahedron. And three are those of the icosahedron that is one of the dodecahedron and one of the cube and the other of the tetrahedron. And 4 are those of the dodecahedron that is one of the icosahedron the other of the cube the other of the octahedron. And the fourth of the tetrahedron. Which among all are 12 by number. Because in the pyramid [of] 4 bases are not sides nor angles nor surfaces in which can lean the angles of the three other regulars if not of the octahedron. And the cube also in itself can receive. The pyramid and the octahedron. And the octahedron only the cube and the pyramid and [in] none of these is not possible to place any of the other two that is icosahedron and dodecahedron, And although the icosahedron to the 3 gives reception only that to the octahedron has denied and this happens by respect of the glorious sign that makes all the demons tremble that is of the holy cross which the 3 lines that among them cut themselves square protracted from one angle to the other diametrically is not place in it that can duly to the disposition of the said tetrahedron be protracted. But the dodecahedron for being among the others of singular prerogative endowed to none has prohibited or true forbidden lodging as of all receptacle, And for this also the ancient Plato together with the others adduced attributed it to the universe.
