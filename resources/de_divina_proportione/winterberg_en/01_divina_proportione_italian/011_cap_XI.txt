Cap. XI.

(Of its second essential effect.)

If a quantity be divided in two parts. and above one [is] placed a quantity that the square of this conjoint be quintuple to the square of the added quantity follows of necessity said added quantity to be the half of the first quantity divided in said two parts. And that to which it was added to be its greater part and it [the first quantity] all to be divided in those [parts] according to our proportion. Verbi gratia. Let be taken 15 minus R 125 and R 125 minus 5 for the two integral parts of a quantity and above one that is R 125 minus 5 placed 5 for third quantity the conjoint will be R 125 the square of which is 125. and the square of the added quantity is 25. Wherefore 125 will be quintuple to 25 square of the added quantity. I say R 25 that is 5 to be half of the first quantity divided in those such two parts. And that to which it was added to be the greater part of said first quantity divided according to our proportion having the mean and two extremes. that is of 10. And this will be converse of the preceding effect. as concludes the second of the thirteenth geometrically.
