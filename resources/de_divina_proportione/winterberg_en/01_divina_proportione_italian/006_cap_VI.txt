Cap. VI.

(Of its worthy commendation.)

This our proportion, Exalted Duke, is of so much prerogative and worthy of excellence as could ever be said for respect of its infinite power. Since without its knowledge very many things most worthy of admiration neither in philosophy nor in any other science could ever come to light. Which gift certainly is conceded from the invariable nature of the superior principles. As says the great philosopher Campanus our most famous mathematician on the tenth of the 14th. Especially seeing it to be that which accords so many diversities of solids both of greatness and of multitude of bases and also of figures and forms with certain irrational symphony among them, as in our process will be understood placing the stupendous effects which (of a line divided according to it) are truly to be called not natural but divine. Of which the first in their enumeration will be this.
