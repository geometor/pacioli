Cap. LVI.

(Of the spherical body its formation.)

XL. By many the sphere has been defined what thing it be. especially by Dionysius worthy mathematician. Yet our author with sum brevity in his 11. describes it: and that such description by all posterity is adduced: where he says thus: Sphere will be that which contains the vestige of the arc of the circumference of the half circle every time: and in whatever mode is taken the semicircle stopping the line of the diameter one turns around the said arc, until as much that it returns to the place whence it commenced to move. That is made the semicircle above which you [will] line stopping that the said semicircle one leads around with all its revolution that such body which so will be described is called sphere. Of which the center will be the center of the said semicircle so conducted. As will be the semicircle c. made above the line ab. made center the point c. and all its arc be the part of the circumference adb. I say that stopping the said line ab. which will be diameter of said semicircle and that circumducing above it. commencing from the point d. going towards the inferior part and returning towards the superior with its arc [to] the said point d. whence first it moved, or true by the opposite. going towards the superior and returning towards the superior even with the arc to the said point d. that such round made by said semicircle in its revolution will be called spherical body: and sphere imagining as one must that said semicircle grace of example be a half plate material which otherwise would not form body, because only the arc circumducted does not make vestige being line without width and depth and this to its notice and causation be said.
