Cap. XXXIX.

(Of the formation of the dodecahedron in the icosahedron.)

The icosahedron as is said has 12 solid angles each contained by 5 superficial angles of its 5 triangles. And therefore wishing in it to make the dodecahedron is convenient first according as we have in this taught to make said icosahedron and when so duly it be disposed of each its triangular base let be found the center by the 5. of the 4. and those then we will continue by 30 straight lines all among them in mode that in it will be caused 12 pentagons each opposite to a solid angle of said icosahedron. And each of the sides of said pentagons will be opposite in cross to each of the sides of said icosahedron. And just as in said icosahedron are 12 solid angles so in the dodecahedron are 12 pentagons. And just as in it are 20 triangular bases so in said dodecahedron are 20 solid angles caused in said bases by means of said lines. And just as in it are 30 sides so in the dodecahedron are 30 sides to those opposite in cross as is said that all their material form manifests as also the 6. of the 15. concludes.
