Cap. XII.

(Of its third singular effect.)

If a quantity be divided according to our proportion if to its smaller part is added the half of the greater [part] then the square of the conjoint will be always quintuple to the square of the half of said greater. Verbi gratia. Let 10 be the quantity divided according to our divine proportion that the one part that is the greater will be R 125 minus 5 and the smaller 15 minus R 125. I say if above 15 minus R 125 which is the smaller is added the half of R 125 minus 5 which is the greater the conjoint then of the smaller and of said half multiplied in itself will be 5 times the square of the half of said greater and so appears. Because the half of R 125 minus 5 is R 31 1/4 minus 2 1/2 joined with 15 minus R 125 which is the smaller makes 12 1/2 minus R 31 1/4. Wherefore multiplying 12 1/2 minus R 31 1/4 times 12 1/2 minus R 31 1/4 makes 187 1/2 minus R 19531 1/4. And this will be called the square of the conjoint. Then square also the half of said greater that is multiply R 31 1/4 minus 2 1/2 times R 31 1/4 minus 2 1/2 will make 37 1/2 minus R 781 1/4. And this will be called the square of the half of the greater which precisely will be the 1/5 of the square of the conjoint. And consequently said square of the conjoint is quintuple to the square of the half of said greater part of 10 so divided. Which force much with the others is to be esteemed, as all geometrically is proved by the third of the 13. of our author.
