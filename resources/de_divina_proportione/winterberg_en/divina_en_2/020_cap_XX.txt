XX. Eleventh Effect

**Proposition**
If the side of an equilateral Hexagon is divided according to the Divine Proportion:
The greater segment is the side of the equilateral Decagon circumscribed about the same circle.
(Note: The text says "circumscribed by the same circle" (inscribed), but later says "side of the decagon from the same circle circumscribed... diameter would come to be 20". If Hexagon side = 10, then Radius = 10, Diameter = 20. If Greater Segment of 10 is $\sqrt{125} - 5$ ($\approx 6.18$), this is the side of the inscribed Decagon ($R(\sqrt{5}-1)/2 = 10(1.236)/2 = 6.18$).
So the proposition means: The greater segment of the Hexagon side (Radius) is the side of the INSCRIBED Decagon.)

**Example**
Hexagon Side (Radius) = 10.
Divided in Divine Proportion.
Greater Segment = $\sqrt{125} - 5$.
This equals the side of the Decagon inscribed in the circle of Radius 10.

**Reference**
(Euclid XIV.3 implies this relationship for inscribed figures).
