# Organization of "De Divina Proportione" (Book I)

This document outlines the logical structure and progression of Fra Luca Pacioli's *Divina Proportione* (Book I), as synthesized from the Winterberg edition.

## I. Foundations (Chapters I - IX)
These chapters establish the philosophical importance and mathematical definition of the Divine Proportion.
*   **I - IV**: Dedication, Utility of the Science, and Praise of Geometry.
*   **V**: The Supremacy of the Divine Proportion among the separate sciences.
*   **VI**: Construction of the Mean and Extreme Ratio.
*   **VII - IX**: Definitions, terminology, and key properties of the proportion.

## II. The Thirteen Effects (Chapters X - XXIII)
Pacioli details thirteen specific mathematical properties ("Effects") derived from the Divine Proportion.
*   **X - XXIII**: Each chapter is dedicated to one "Effect," demonstrating various geometric relationships, particularly those relating to the pentagon and hexagon inscribed in a circle.

## III. The Five Regular Bodies (Chapters XXIV - XXXV)
The application of the Divine Proportion to the construction of the Platonic Solids.
*   **XXIV - XXX**: The geometric necessity of the five regular bodies and their reliance on the Divine Proportion.
*   **XXXI**: Construction of the **Tetrahedron** (4 faces).
*   **XXXII**: Construction of the **Cube** (Hexahedron, 6 faces).
*   **XXXIII**: Construction of the **Octahedron** (8 faces).
*   **XXXIV**: Construction of the **Icosahedron** (20 faces).
*   **XXXV**: Construction of the **Dodecahedron** (12 faces).

## IV. Inscriptions and Relationships (Chapters XXXVI - L)
Detailed analysis of how the regular bodies fit within one another and their volumetric properties.
*   **XXXVI - XLV**: Inscribing bodies within bodies (e.g., Octahedron in Cube, Cube in Dodecahedron).
*   **XLVI - XLVII**: Summary of inscriptions and the Sphere containing them.
*   **XLVIII - L**: Specific variations and side calculations for the Tetrahedron, Cube, and Octahedron.

## V. Variations and Dependent Bodies (Chapters LI - LV)
Exploration of complex forms derived from the regular bodies.
*   **LI - LIII**: Variations of the Icosahedron, Dodecahedron, and the 26-faced body.
*   **LIV**: The 72-faced body.
*   **LV**: Celestial correspondences (Plato's Timaeus) and the infinite generation of forms.

## VI. Columns and Pyramids (Chapters LVI - LXIX)
Measurement and definition of other geometric solids.
*   **LVI - LVII**: The Sphere.
*   **LVIII - LXIII**: Round (Cylinder) and Angular (Prism) Columns.
*   **LXIV - LXVIII**: Round (Cone) and Angular Pyramids, including Truncated forms.
*   **LXIX**: Rules for measuring Abscinded and Elevated bodies.

## VII. Conclusion (Chapters LXX - LXXI)
Final guides and definitions.
*   **LXX**: Guide to the accompanying Illustrations (Perspective and Material models).
*   **LXXI**: Glossary of mathematical terms (Hypothesis, Cathetus, etc.) and colophon.
