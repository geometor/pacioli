XVI. Seventh Effect

**Proposition**
If the side of an equilateral hexagon and the side of an equilateral decagon inscribed in the same circle are added together, the sum is divided according to the Divine Proportion.
The greater segment is the side of the hexagon.

**Example**
Let the radius (Hexagon side) = $\sqrt{125} - 5$.
Let the Decagon side = $15 - \sqrt{125}$.
Sum (Radius + Decagon Side) = $(\sqrt{125} - 5) + (15 - \sqrt{125}) = 10$.
We know from previous examples that 10 divided in Divine Proportion has $\sqrt{125} - 5$ as the greater segment.

**Conclusion**
The sum of the Hexagon side and Decagon side is a line divided in Divine Proportion, where the Hexagon side is the greater segment.
(Euclid XIII.9)
