LXVI. Measurement of the Pyramid

**Rule**
Volume of Pyramid = $\frac{1}{3} \times$ (Volume of corresponding Column)
Volume = $\frac{1}{3} \times$ (Area of Base $\times$ Height)

**Example (Round Pyramid/Cone)**
*   Base Diameter ($D$) = 7.
*   Height ($h$) = 10.
*   Area of Base (from LXII) = 38.5.
*   Volume of Cylinder = 385.
*   Volume of Cone = $385 \div 3 = 128 \frac{1}{3}$.

**Proposition**
Every pyramid is the third part of the prism (or cylinder) having the same base and the same height.
(Euclid XII, Prop. 7 for Prisms; XII, Prop. 10 for Cones).
