VIII. Algebraic Calculation of the Divine Proportion

**Problem**
Divide a quantity of 10 according to the proportion having a mean and two extremes.
Let the whole quantity be 10.
Let the greater part be $x$ and the lesser part be $10 - x$.
By definition: $10 : x = x : (10 - x)$.
Therefore: $x^2 = 10(10 - x) = 100 - 10x$.
$x^2 + 10x - 100 = 0$.

**Solution**
Using Algebra (Rule of the Thing):
$x = \sqrt{125} - 5$ (The greater part).
The lesser part is $10 - (\sqrt{125} - 5) = 15 - \sqrt{125}$.

These parts are irrational "Residuals" (Apotomes, Euclid X.79).
$\sqrt{125} \approx 11.18$.
Greater part $\approx 6.18$.
Lesser part $\approx 3.82$.

**Verification**
$(15 - \sqrt{125}) \times 10 = 150 - \sqrt{12500}$.
$(\sqrt{125} - 5)^2 = 125 - 10\sqrt{125} + 25 = 150 - \sqrt{12500}$.
The product of the lesser and the whole equals the square of the greater.
