XI. Converse of the First Effect

**Proposition**
If a quantity is divided into two parts, and to one part a quantity is added such that the square of the sum is 5 times the square of the added quantity:
Then the added quantity is half of the original divided quantity.
And the part to which it was added is the greater segment of the original quantity divided according to the Divine Proportion.

**Example**
Let the two parts be $15 - \sqrt{125}$ (lesser) and $\sqrt{125} - 5$ (greater).
Add 5 to the greater part: $(\sqrt{125} - 5) + 5 = \sqrt{125}$.
Square of sum: $(\sqrt{125})^2 = 125$.
Square of added quantity: $5^2 = 25$.
$125 = 5 \times 25$.

**Conclusion**
The added quantity (5) is half of the original quantity (10).
The part to which it was added ($\sqrt{125} - 5$) is the greater segment of 10 divided in Divine Proportion.
(Euclid XIII.2)
