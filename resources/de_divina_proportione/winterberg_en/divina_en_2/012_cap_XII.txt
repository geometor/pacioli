XII. Third Effect

**Proposition**
If a quantity is divided according to the Divine Proportion, and to the smaller segment is added half of the greater segment:
The square of the sum is 5 times the square of half the greater segment.

**Example**
Whole quantity = 10.
Greater segment ($AC$) = $\sqrt{125} - 5$.
Smaller segment ($CB$) = $15 - \sqrt{125}$.
Half of greater segment ($AC/2$) = $\frac{\sqrt{125} - 5}{2} = \sqrt{31.25} - 2.5$.
Sum ($CB + AC/2$) = $(15 - \sqrt{125}) + (\sqrt{31.25} - 2.5)$
Note: $\sqrt{125} = 2\sqrt{31.25}$.
Sum = $15 - 2\sqrt{31.25} + \sqrt{31.25} - 2.5 = 12.5 - \sqrt{31.25}$.

Square of Sum = $(12.5 - \sqrt{31.25})^2 = 156.25 - 25\sqrt{31.25} + 31.25 = 187.5 - 25\sqrt{31.25}$.
Square of Half Greater = $(\sqrt{31.25} - 2.5)^2 = 31.25 - 5\sqrt{31.25} + 6.25 = 37.5 - 5\sqrt{31.25}$.
$5 \times (37.5 - 5\sqrt{31.25}) = 187.5 - 25\sqrt{31.25}$.

**Conclusion**
The square of the sum is exactly 5 times the square of half the greater segment.
(Euclid XIII.3)
