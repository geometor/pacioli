LVII. Inscribing Regular Bodies in the Sphere

**Proposition**
All five regular bodies can be inscribed within a sphere such that all their vertices touch the spherical surface.

**Construction (Analogy of the Lapicide)**
Imagine a spherical stone (e.g., a cannonball). By marking the appropriate number of equidistant points on its surface and cutting away the excess material along straight planes connecting these points, the regular bodies are formed:
1.  **Tetrahedron**: 4 equidistant points.
2.  **Cube**: 8 equidistant points.
3.  **Octahedron**: 6 equidistant points.
4.  **Icosahedron**: 12 equidistant points.
5.  **Dodecahedron**: 20 equidistant points.

**Geometric Impossibility**
It is impossible to construct a regular body with equal faces that is not one of these five. For example, a body with 4 equal faces must be a Tetrahedron (triangular faces); it cannot have 4 square faces. A body with 6 equal faces must be a Cube (square faces); it cannot have 6 triangular faces.
