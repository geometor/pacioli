XIV. Fifth Effect

**Proposition**
If a quantity is divided according to the Divine Proportion:
The sum of the square of the smaller segment and the square of the whole quantity is 3 times the square of the greater segment.
$CB^2 + AB^2 = 3 \cdot AC^2$.

**Example**
Whole ($AB$) = 10. Square = 100.
Greater ($AC$) = $\sqrt{125} - 5$. Square = $150 - \sqrt{12500}$.
Smaller ($CB$) = $15 - \sqrt{125}$. Square = $350 - \sqrt{112500}$. Note: $\sqrt{112500} = \sqrt{9 \times 12500} = 3\sqrt{12500}$.
So $CB^2 = 350 - 3\sqrt{12500}$.

Sum ($CB^2 + AB^2$) = $(350 - 3\sqrt{12500}) + 100 = 450 - 3\sqrt{12500}$.
Triple Greater Square ($3 \cdot AC^2$) = $3 \times (150 - \sqrt{12500}) = 450 - 3\sqrt{12500}$.

**Conclusion**
The sum is equal to the triple square of the greater segment.
(Euclid XIII.5)
