XVIII. Ninth Effect

**Proposition**
In a circle with an inscribed equilateral pentagon, sum chords (diagonals) subtending two adjacent angles intersect each other.
These two chords divide each other according to the Divine Proportion.
The greater segment of each chord is equal to the side of the pentagon.

**Example**
Pentagon $ABCDE$.
Chord $AC$ (subtending angle $B$) and Chord $BE$ (subtending angle $A$) intersect at $F$.
$AC$ is divided at $F$ such that $AF > FC$ (or vice versa depending on orientation, typically greater part is the segment connected to vertex).
Actually, the greater segment is equal to the side of the pentagon.
Side of Pentagon = Greater Segment of Chord.

If Chord = 10:
Greater Segment ($CF = EF =$ Side of Pentagon) = $\sqrt{125} - 5$.
Lesser Segment ($AF = BF$) = $15 - \sqrt{125}$.

**Reference**
(Euclid XIII.11; XIII.8; II.11; VI.29)
