CHAPTER XXXI — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

On the Rule and Way to Find the Sides of All Five Regular Bodies Placed in One and the Same Sphere, and Their Comparison with One Another.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram: Folio 0036 (f. 10v) contains the famous master diagram 0036.g1.png of Euclid XIII.18, displaying the semicircle with all five edges (\(ad\) = tetrahedron, \(fb\) = octahedron, \(db\) = cube, \(ah\) = icosahedron, \(dm\) = dodecahedron).

  • Pacioli’s Observation on Campanus’s Ordering (“Not Without Mystery”): Pacioli astutely explains why Campanus of Novara placed the Cube (Campanus XIII.14) immediately after the Tetrahedron (XIII.13) and before the Octahedron (Campanus XIII.15): the single trisection point \(c\) (\(ac = 2\,cb\)) on diameter \(ab\) gives the tetrahedron edge \(ad\) as the longer chord and the cube edge \(db\) as the shorter chord in the very same right triangle \(adb\)!
