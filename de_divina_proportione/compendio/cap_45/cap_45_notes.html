CHAPTER XLV — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

How the Octocedron Is Placed in the Duodecedron.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Geometric Insight of Campanus XV.11: In Euclid’s construction of the dodecahedron (Euclid XIII.17), 6 of the 30 edges of the dodecahedron are the roof ridges \(vx\) centered over the 6 faces of the inscribed cube. The midpoints of those 6 edges lie on the 3 coordinate axes of the cube at distance \(\frac{1}{2}s_{\text{cube}} + \text{lesser} = \frac{\varphi}{2}s_{\text{cube}} = \frac{\varphi^{2}}{2}a_{12}\) from the origin. Because the 3 coordinate axes are orthogonal and equal in length, those 6 edge-midpoints are the exact vertices of a regular octahedron of edge \(a_{8} = \frac{\varphi^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}a_{12}\)!
