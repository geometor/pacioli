CHAPTER XLIV — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

How the Ycocedron Is Placed in the Cube.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Two Ways to Inscribe the Icosahedron in the Cube:

    1. Edge-on-Face Inscription (Golden Rectangles): An icosahedron of edge \(a_{20}\) has 12 vertices that form three mutually perpendicular golden rectangles of dimensions \(a_{20} \times \varphi\,a_{20}\). Placing these three golden rectangles inside a cube of side \(s_{\text{cube}} = \varphi\,a_{20}\) puts 6 edges of the icosahedron (and all 12 of its vertices!) directly on the 6 square faces of the cube, with the cube edge and icosahedron edge in the Divine Proportion (\(s_{\text{cube}} : a_{20} = \varphi : 1\)).

    2. Campanus XV.10 Inscription: Campanus routes the construction through the octahedron inscribed in the cube (XV.3) and the circles of Euclid XIII.16.
