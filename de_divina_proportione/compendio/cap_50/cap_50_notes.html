CHAPTER L — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

On the Disposition of the Octocedron: Regular (Plates XIII–XIV), Truncated (Plates XV–XVI), and Elevated (Plates XVII–XVIII).

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Paganini 1509 Plates Referenced:

    • Plate XIII (0217.rst): Octocedron Planum Solidum (Octahedron, solid).

    • Plate XIV (0219.rst): Octocedron Planum Vacuum (Octahedron, skeletal).

    • Plate XV (0221.rst): Octocedron Abscisum Solidum (Truncated/rectified octahedron, solid).

    • Plate XVI (0223.rst): Octocedron Abscisum Vacuum (Truncated/rectified octahedron, skeletal).

    • Plate XVII (0225.rst): Octocedron Elevatum Solidum (Stella Octangula, compound of two dual tetrahedra, solid).

    • Plate XVIII (0227.rst): Octocedron Elevatum Vacuum (Stella Octangula, skeletal).

  • First Depiction of the *:term:`Stella Octangula <octahedron-elevatum>`*: Plates XVII and XVIII (drawn by Leonardo da Vinci in 1497 and printed in 1509) are the earliest known perspective depictions of the stellated :term:`octahedron <octahedron>` (later rediscovered by Johannes Kepler in Harmonices Mundi, 1619, who named it the :term:`Stella Octangula <octahedron-elevatum>`). Because the dihedral angle of an octahedron (\(\arccos(-\frac{1}{3}) \approx 109.47^{\circ}\)) and the dihedral angle of a regular tetrahedron (\(\arccos(\frac{1}{3}) \approx 70.53^{\circ}\)) are supplementary (\(109.47^{\circ} + 70.53^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}\)), erecting regular tetrahedra on the 8 faces of a regular octahedron makes the faces coplanar across the octahedral edges, forming two interpenetrating large regular tetrahedra of double the edge length!
