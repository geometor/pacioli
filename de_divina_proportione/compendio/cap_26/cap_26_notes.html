CHAPTER XXVI — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

How the First Regular Body, Called the Tetracedron or Pyramid of Four Triangular Bases, Is Formed and Placed in the Sphere.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram: Folio 0032 (f. 8v) includes diagram 0032.g1.png, showing the semicircle \(adb\) cut at \(c\) (\(ac = 2\,cb\)) alongside the base circle and triangle of the tetrahedron.

  • Mathematical Identity: For a sphere of diameter \(D\), the edge \(a_{4}\) of the inscribed regular tetrahedron satisfies \(D^{2} : a_{4}^{2} = 3 : 2\), or \(a_{4} = D\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\).

  • Comparison with Earlier Draft Track: The earlier draft omitted Steps 5–7 (how the perpendicular \(hk = ac\) from the center of the circumscribed circle of radius \(cd\) proves \(ke = da\) via Euclid XIII.12 and Euclid XI.4).

Constantin Winterberg’s Commentary (1889 Edition)¶

Note 10 (Cap. XXVI): Division of the Sphere Diameter by the Base of the Inscribed Tetrahedron (Euclid XIII.13) (Sachliche Anmerkungen #10, Cap. XXVI, p. 216, l. 12 from top)

In every regular tetrahedron, the four altitudes intersect at the center of the circumscribed sphere (of radius $r$) in the ratio $1 : 3$. Denoting the smaller segment (from the sphere center to the base plane) by $x = \frac{r}{3}$, and extending the altitude $ac = r + x$ beyond the base plane to meet the sphere at $b$ (so that $ab = 2r$ is the sphere diameter and $bc = r - x$), the ratio of the extension $bc$ to the tetrahedron altitude $ac$ is:

\[\frac{bc}{ac} = \frac{r - x}{r + x} = \frac{r - \frac{r}{3}}{r + \frac{r}{3}} = \frac{\frac{2}{3}r}{\frac{4}{3}r} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \text{whence } ab : ac = 3 : 2 \text{ and } ac : bc = 2 : 1.\]

Anmerkung 10 (Cap. XXVI, S. 216): Theilung des Kugeldurchmessers beim regulären Tetraeder (Euklid XIII.13)

Bekanntlich verhalten sich in jedem regulären Tetraeder die Abschnitte der Höhen, welche sich im Centrum der umschriebenen Kugel vom Radius $r$ schneiden, zu einander wie $1 : 3$. Bezeichnet man nun den kleinern Abschnitt mit $x$ und denkt sich ferner eine der Höhen über die Grundfläche hinaus bis zum Durchschnitt mit der Kugel verlängert, so ergibt sich das Verhältniss dieser Verlängerung $bc$ zu der Höhe $ac$ des Tetraeders:

\[\frac{bc}{ac} = \frac{r - x}{r + x}, \qquad \text{folglich, da } x = \frac{r}{3} \text{ ist:} \quad \frac{bc}{ac} = \frac{1}{2}.\]

Alles Uebrige ergibt sodann der Text von selber.
