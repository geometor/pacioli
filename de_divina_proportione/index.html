De Divina Proportione¶

On the Divine Proportion — Fra Luca Pacioli & Leonardo da Vinci (Venice 1509 / Vienna 1889)

Author: Fra Luca Pacioli, O.F.M. (c. 1447–1517)
Illustrator: Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519)
Critical Editor & Translator: Constantin Winterberg (Vienna, 1889)
Printer: Paganino de' Paganini (Venice, June 1, 1509)
GEOMETOR Edition: 318 archival folio scans · 482 woodcut plates & Leonardo polyhedra · Synchronized English, Italian, German, & Notes tracks

A folio-verified critical edition of Fra Luca Pacioli's De Divina Proportione (Venice, June 1, 1509), uniting the 318 high-resolution folio scans and 482 recalibrated woodcuts (214 initials, 180 marginal geometric diagrams, 23 Roman alphabet plates, 5 architectural and Arbor Proportionis plates, and Leonardo da Vinci's 60 polyhedra plates) across four synchronized tracks: English (GEOMETOR translation), Italiano (1509 Paganini diplomatic text), Deutsch (Winterberg 1889 critical translation), and Notes (historical & mathematical commentary).

Table of Contents¶

  • Frontmatter

    • Winterberg’s Critical Introduction (Vienna, 1889) — pp. III–V, 1–15 ([English · Deutsch])

  • Part I — Compendio de Divina Proportione (Cap. I–LXXI · Folios 0017–0061, f. 1r–23r)

    • I. Dedication, Preface & Nature of the Mathematical Sciences (Cap. I–VI) — Epistle to Ludovico Sforza, eulogy of the quadrivium, and the five divine attributes of the Divine Proportion

    • II. The Thirteen Marvelous Effects in Lines & Regular Polygons (Cap. VII–XXIII) — Extreme and mean ratio in segments, hexagons, decagons, and pentagons (Euclid XIII.1–12, X.73, X.76)

    • III. The Five Regular Bodies, Mutual Inscriptions & Dependent Solids (Cap. XXIV–LV · Plates I–XL) — Uniqueness (XI.21), constructions (XIII.13–18), proportions (Book XIV), twelve mutual inscriptions (Book XV), and truncated/elevated bodies

    • IV. Sphere, Columns, Pyramids, Plate Concordance & Glossary (Cap. LVI–LXXI · Plates XLI–LIX) — Stereometric mensuration, Milanese panegyric, order of Leonardo's perspective models, and mathematical terms

  • Part II — Tractato de l’Architectura (Prohemio & Cap. I–XX · Folios 0061–0082, f. 23r–33v)

    • I. Prohemio to the Stonemasons of Borgo Sansepolcro (f. 23r–25r) — Disciples of Borgo Sansepolcro and promise of Piero della Francesca's perspective

    • II. Vitruvian Human Proportions (Cap. I–III) — Head in the equilateral triangle (Euclid I.1), perspective net (rete), and perfect numbers 6, 10, and 16 (IX.36)

    • III. Columns, Classical Orders & Architectural Solids (Cap. IV–XI) — Corinthian, Ionic, and Doric orders, stylobates, columns across Italy, pyramids, and ancient letters

    • IV. Entablature, Porta Speciosa & Superimposed Orders (Cap. XII–XX) — Intercolumniations, triglyphs, epistyle, frieze, cornice, Porta Speciosa, and polyhedra in architecture

  • Appendices — Woodcut Plates (1509) & Mathematical Commentary (1889)

    • I. The Roman Geometric Alphabet (23 Plates · Folios 0139–0183)

    • II. Architectural Plates & Arbor Proportionis (5 Plates · Folios 0137, 0185, 0213, 0215, 0311)

    • III. Polyhedra Plates by Leonardo da Vinci (Plates I–LIX · Folios 0187–0309)

    • IV. Winterberg’s Substantive Mathematical Notes (Sachliche Anmerkungen 1–17 · 1889, pp. 338–352)
