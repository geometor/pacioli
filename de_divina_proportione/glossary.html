Glossary of Geometric, Architectural & Latin Terms¶

Multilingual Lexicon of Renaissance Mathematical & Architectural Terminology

Technical terms in Italian, Latin, Greek, and German across Fra Luca Pacioli’s De Divina Proportione (1509 / 1889).

abaco (abacus) (abaco)¶

Language: Greek / Latin / Italian | Translation: Abacus of a Capital

The flat slab forming the uppermost member of a column capital immediately beneath the architrave (Architectura Cap. VII, Folio 0213).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 4 (EN), Arch. Cap. 4 (IT), Arch. Cap. 7 (IT), Comp. Cap. 57 (IT), Comp. Cap. 70 (IT), Plates: Architectural (IT)

abscisum / absciso / scapezzo (ἀποτετμημένον) (abscisum)¶

Language: Latin / Italian / Greek | Translation: Truncated Polyhedron

A dependent (Archimedean) polyhedron derived from a regular body by cutting off its vertices at one-third or one-half of each edge (generating the truncated tetrahedron, cuboctahedron, truncated octahedron, truncated icosahedron, and icosidodecahedron).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 47 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 48 (EN), Comp. Cap. 48 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 49 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 50 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 51 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 52 (IT), Comp. Cap. 52 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 53 (IT), Comp. Cap. 68 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 69 (EN), Comp. Cap. 69 (IT), Winterberg’s Critical Introduction (1889), Plates: Anmerkungen (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

acroterio / regolo (acroterio)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Acroterium / Crowning Fillet

Pedestal at the apex and eaves of a pediment, or the uppermost crowning fillet (regolo) at the top of the cornice on Folio 0215 (Architectura Cap. XIV, XVI).

algebra e almucabala (regola de la cosa) (algebra-e-almucabala)¶

Language: Arabic / Italian | Translation: Algebra and Almucabala (The Rule of the Thing)

The speculative practice of restoration (al-jabr) and balancing (al-muqābala) of equations, termed in the Italian vernacular ‘la regola de la cosa’ (Chapter VIII).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 52 (IT)

ambliogonio (triangulo obtusangulo) (ambliogonio)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Obtuse-Angled Triangle (Amblygonion)

A triangle containing one obtuse angle greater than 90° (Compendio Cap. LX).

angulo acuto (angulo-acuto)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Acute Angle (< 90°)

A plane angle smaller than a right angle (Euclid I.def.12; Compendio Cap. LX).

angulo dela iustitia (angolo recto) (angulo-dela-iustitia)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Angle of Justice (Right Angle, 90°)

Pacioli’s name in Chapter LIV for the invariant 90° right angle determined by the plummet and square, without which good cannot be distinguished from bad in architecture and measurement.

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 54 (EN)

angulo obtuso (angulo-obtuso)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Obtuse Angle (> 90°)

A plane angle greater than a right angle (Euclid I.def.11; Compendio Cap. LX).

angulo recto (angulo-recto)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Right Angle (90°)

An angle formed when a straight line standing on another makes the adjacent angles equal (Euclid I.def.10); called by Pacioli the ‘Angle of Justice’ (Compendio Cap. LIV, LX).

angulo solido (angulo-solido)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Solid Angle (Polyhedral Vertex)

An angle formed by the meeting of three or more plane angles at a single point not lying in the same plane; the sum of its plane angles must be strictly less than four right angles (360°, Euclid XI.21).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 25 (EN), Comp. Cap. 25 (IT), Comp. Cap. 25 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 27 (EN), Comp. Cap. 28 (EN), Comp. Cap. 29 (EN), Comp. Cap. 30 (EN), Comp. Cap. 34 (EN), Comp. Cap. 35 (EN), Comp. Cap. 36 (IT), Comp. Cap. 37 (EN), Comp. Cap. 38 (EN), Comp. Cap. 40 (EN), Comp. Cap. 43 (EN), Comp. Cap. 45 (EN), Comp. Cap. 46 (EN), Comp. Cap. 48 (EN), Comp. Cap. 50 (IT), Comp. Cap. 53 (EN), Comp. Cap. 55 (EN), Comp. Cap. 57 (EN)

Arbor Proportio et Proportionalitas (arbor-proportionis)¶

Language: Latin | Translation: Tree of Ratio and Proportion

The full-page dichotomous classification diagram printed in red and black on Folio 0311, dividing continuous and discrete quantity into rational and irrational ratios and branching into the five species of commensurate ratios (multiplex, superparticularis, superpartiens, multiplex superparticularis, multiplex superpartiens).

Occurrences: Plates: Architectural (DE), Plates: Architectural (EN), Plates: Architectural (IT)

archipendolo / perpendiculo (piombo) (archipendolo)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Plumb-Level (Plummet and A-Frame Level)

Instrument using a weighted plumb-line (catheto) to determine true verticality and horizontal leveling in building (Compendio Cap. LIV, LXXI).

arco (arcus) (arco)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Circular Arc

A portion of the circumference of a circle cut off by a chord (Compendio Cap. LXII, LXXI).

arithmetica¶

Language: Greek / Latin / Italian | Translation: Arithmetic (Science of Discrete Quantity)

The mathematical discipline treating of discrete quantity (number) in itself and its ratios (Compendio Cap. III).

Occurrences: Arch. Prohemio (IT), Comp. Cap. 2 (IT), Comp. Cap. 3 (IT), Comp. Cap. 4 (IT), Comp. Cap. 10 (IT), Comp. Cap. 19 (IT), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT), Plates: Architectural (IT)

astragalo / tondino (astragalo)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Astragal / Bead Molding (Tondino)

Small convex semicircular molding with fillet at the top of a column shaft beneath the capital necking, or dividing fasciae of the architrave (Architectura Cap. VII, Folio 0213, 0215).

astronomia / astrologia (astronomia)¶

Language: Greek / Latin / Italian | Translation: Astronomy (Science of Celestial Motion)

The mathematical science of mobile continuous magnitude and the revolutions of the celestial spheres (Compendio Cap. III).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 1 (IT), Comp. Cap. 3 (IT)

binomio¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Binomial Irrational (a + √b)

The sum of two segments commensurable only in square (Euclid X.36, X.48–53), conjugate to the apotome (residuo).

capitello¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Column Capital

The crowning head of a column shaft, whose form distinguishes the Doric, Ionic (pulvinated), and Corinthian orders (Architectura Cap. IV, VII, Folio 0213).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 4 (IT), Arch. Cap. 5 (IT), Arch. Cap. 6 (IT), Arch. Cap. 7 (IT), Arch. Cap. 8 (IT), Arch. Cap. 12 (IT), Comp. Cap. 57 (IT), Plates: Architectural (IT)

catheto / perpendiculare (catheto)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Cathetus / Perpendicular Altitude (κάθετος)

Defined in Chapter LXXI: a plumbed line falling at right angles upon a plane or base line, or the altitude dropped from the apex of a triangle or pyramid to its base.

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 1 (EN), Arch. Cap. 1 (IT), Arch. Cap. 2 (IT), Arch. Cap. 20 (DE), Arch. Cap. 20 (EN), Arch. Cap. 20 (IT), Comp. Cap. 26 (IT), Comp. Cap. 27 (IT), Comp. Cap. 28 (IT), Comp. Cap. 29 (IT), Comp. Cap. 29 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 30 (IT), Comp. Cap. 31 (IT), Comp. Cap. 33 (IT), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT), Plates: Anmerkungen (EN)

censo (ce.) (censo)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: The Square of the Unknown (x²) in Cossic Algebra

Renaissance abacus term (translating Arabic māl / Latin census) for the square of the unknown quantity.

censo de censo (ce. ce.) (censo-de-censo)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Fourth Power of the Unknown (x⁴) in Cossic Algebra

Abacus algebraic term for the square of the square (x⁴), extensively used by Piero della Francesca and Pacioli in the biquadratic problems of the Libellus.

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 3 (EN), Arch. Cap. 3 (IT), Arch. Cap. 3 (Notes)

centro¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Center (κέντρον)

Defined in Chapter LXXI: the middle point inside a circle or sphere from which all straight lines drawn to the circumference are equal.

cimento e copella (cimento-e-copella)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Touchstone and Assaying-Cup (Cupel)

Metallurgical metaphor used by Pacioli in Chapters I–II for the mathematical disciplines as the infallible test of intellectual rigor (‘aurum probatur igni et ingenium mathematicis’).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 1 (EN), Comp. Cap. 1 (IT)

circino / sesto (compasso) (circino)¶

Language: Latin / Italian | Translation: Pair of Compasses (Dividers)

Along with the straightedge/ruler (regolo/norma), one of the two universal instruments of geometry and all mechanical arts, generating the circle and transferring proportions (Compendio Cap. LIV; Alphabet Folios 0139–0183).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 6 (EN), Arch. Cap. 9 (EN), Arch. Cap. 9 (IT), Arch. Cap. 11 (EN), Arch. Cap. 11 (IT), Arch. Prohemio (EN), Arch. Prohemio (IT)

circonferentia / peripheria (circonferentia)¶

Language: Latin / Italian / Greek | Translation: Circumference / Periphery of a Circle

The curved boundary line of a circle, whose ratio to its diameter was shown by Archimedes to lie between 3 10/71 and 3 1/7 (approx. 22:7; Compendio Cap. LXII).

Occurrences: Plates: Alphabet (IT)

circulo / cerchio (circulo)¶

Language: Latin / Italian | Translation: Circle

A plane figure bounded by a single curved line (circumference) such that all straight lines drawn to it from an interior center are equal (Euclid I.def.15).

colonna corinthia (colonna-corinthia)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Corinthian Column and Order

The most slender and ornate of the three classical orders, invented by Callimachus at Corinth in imitation of maidenly grace and an acanthus-wreathed basket over a maiden’s tomb, with a capital height equal to an entire lower shaft diameter (Architectura Cap. III–IV, VII; Folio 0215).

colonna dorica (colonna-dorica)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Doric Column and Order

The first and most virile of the three classical Greek orders, modeled on the naked proportion of a man (6:1 or 7:1 height-to-diameter), built without a base in ancient Greek temples and crowned by a plain echinus-and-abacus capital beneath a triglyph frieze (Architectura Cap. III–IV, VII, XII).

Columna laterata exagona (Κίων πλευρώδης ἑξάγωνος) (colonna-exagona)¶

Language: Latin / Greek / Italian | Translation: Hexagonal Prismatic Column (Hexagonal Prism)

Six-sided right prism with parallel regular hexagonal bases and six rectangular lateral faces (Compendio Cap. LXI, Plates XLVII–XLVIII).

Occurrences: Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

colonna ionica (pulvinata) (colonna-ionica)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Ionic Column and Order

The middle classical order, modeled on matronly slenderness (8:1 height-to-diameter), furnished with a base with scotia and tori, fluted shaft, and a voluted (‘pulvinated’) capital mimicking coiled hair (Architectura Cap. III, V, VII; Folio 0213).

colonna laterata (columna laterata / κίων πλευρώδης) (colonna-laterata)¶

Language: Italian / Latin / Greek | Translation: Prismatic (Polygonal) Column / Prism

An oblong solid whose two opposite bases are congruent rectilinear polygons (triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons) connected by rectangular lateral faces (Compendio Cap. LVIII–LXI, LXIII).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 9 (EN), Comp. Cap. 59 (EN), Comp. Cap. 63 (EN), Comp. Cap. 65 (IT), Comp. Cap. 67 (IT)

Columna laterata pentagona (Κίων πλευρώδης πεντάγωνος) (colonna-pentagona)¶

Language: Latin / Greek / Italian | Translation: Pentagonal Prismatic Column (Pentagonal Prism)

Five-sided right prism with parallel regular pentagonal bases and five rectangular lateral faces (Compendio Cap. LXI, Plates XLV–XLVI).

Occurrences: Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

Columna laterata quadrilatera (Κίων πλευρώδης τετράπλευρος) (colonna-quadrilatera)¶

Language: Latin / Greek / Italian | Translation: Quadrangular Prismatic Column (Square Prism)

Four-sided right prism with parallel square bases and four oblong rectangular lateral faces (Compendio Cap. LIX–LXI, Plates XLIII–XLIV).

colonna rotonda (columna rotunda / κίων στρογγύλος) (colonna-rotonda)¶

Language: Italian / Latin / Greek | Translation: Round Column / Right Circular Cylinder

A cylinder generated by the rotation of a rectangle about one of its stationary vertical sides, bounded by two parallel circular bases (Compendio Cap. LVIII, LXII; Plate LVII).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 3 (IT), Arch. Cap. 4 (EN), Arch. Cap. 7 (IT), Arch. Cap. 9 (IT), Arch. Cap. 20 (IT), Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

cono dela pyramide (cono-pyramidale)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Apex (Vertex) of a Pyramid or Cone

Defined in Chapter LXXI: the supreme summit point of a pyramid where all lateral edges ascending from its base concur.

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 65 (EN), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT)

corausto (coraustus) (corausto)¶

Language: Latin / Italian | Translation: Coraustus (Upper Horizontal Chord Joining Erected Perpendiculars)

Defined in Chapter LXXI (from Campanus on Euclid XIII.16): a straight line joining the upper extremities of two equal perpendiculars (catheti) erected upon a base plane, forming the upper horizontal ring of the icosahedron.

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 26 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 45 (EN), Comp. Cap. 48 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 71 (EN), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT), Comp. index

corda (chorda) (corda)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Chord of a Circle or Polygon

A straight line joining the endpoints of a circular arc or two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon (Compendio Cap. XVIII, LXII, LXXI).

corda pentagonica (corda-pentagonica)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Pentagonal Chord (Diagonal of a Regular Pentagon)

Defined in Chapters XVIII and LXXI: a straight line drawn inside a regular pentagon from any vertex to an opposite non-adjacent vertex; two intersecting pentagonal chords divide each other in the Divine Proportion (Euclid XIII.8).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 41 (EN), Comp. Cap. 46 (EN), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT)

cornice / cornicione (cornicione)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Cornice (Crowning Entablature Molding)

The projecting uppermost assembly of the entablature above the frieze, comprising dentils, corona (dripstone), echinus (ovolo), and acroterium/regolo (Architectura Cap. XIV, XVI, Folio 0215).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 16 (EN), Arch. Cap. 16 (IT), Arch. Cap. 20 (IT), Arch. index, Plates: Architectural (DE), Plates: Architectural (EN), Plates: Architectural (IT)

corona / gociolatoro (corona)¶

Language: Latin / Italian | Translation: Corona / Dripstone (Gocciolatoio)

The boldly projecting vertical-faced member of the cornice that throws rainwater clear of the frieze and architrave below (Architectura Cap. XVI, Folio 0215).

Occurrences: Plates: Architectural (IT)

corpi dependenti (corpi-dependenti)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Dependent (Archimedean Truncated and Stellated/Elevated) Bodies

Semi-regular and stellated polyhedra generated systematically from the five regular solids by uniform truncation of edges (‘abscisi / scapezzi’) or by erecting pyramids upon every face (‘elevati / pontuti’).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 5 (EN), Comp. Cap. 47 (Notes)

corpi regulari (quinque corpora regularia) (corpi-regulari)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: The Five Regular (Platonic) Bodies

The five convex polyhedra bounded by congruent equilateral and equiangular polygons meeting in congruent solid angles, each inscribable and circumscribable in a sphere (Euclid XIII.18): tetrahedron, hexahedron (cube), octahedron, icosahedron, and dodecahedron.

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 6 (IT), Arch. Cap. 9 (IT), Arch. Cap. 12 (IT), Comp. Cap. 5 (EN), Comp. Cap. 5 (IT), Comp. Cap. 19 (IT), Comp. Cap. 23 (IT), Comp. Cap. 24 (IT), Comp. Cap. 25 (IT), Comp. Cap. 26 (IT), Comp. Cap. 32 (IT), Comp. Cap. 46 (IT), Comp. Cap. 47 (IT), Comp. Cap. 55 (IT), Comp. Cap. 57 (IT), Comp. Cap. 69 (IT)

corpo seratile (columna laterata triangula / σχῆμα πριστόν) (corpo-seratile)¶

Language: Italian / Latin / Greek | Translation: Triangular Prism (Sawn Body)

A five-faced prism with two parallel triangular bases and three rectangular lateral faces, called ‘seratile’ (from Latin serratus, ‘sawn’) because it is obtained by sawing a parallelepiped diagonally in half (Euclid XI.def.13, XII.7; Compendio Cap. LIX, LXVII; Plates XLI–XLII).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 41 (IT), Comp. Cap. 59 (EN), Comp. Cap. 59 (IT), Comp. Cap. 59 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 67 (EN), Comp. Cap. 67 (IT), Comp. Cap. 67 (Notes), Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

corpo solido (solidum / στερεόν) (corpo-solido)¶

Language: Italian / Latin / Greek | Translation: Three-Dimensional Solid Body

That which has length, breadth, and depth (altitude), bounded by plane or curved surfaces (Euclid XI.def.1–2).

cosa (co.) (cosa)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: The Unknown Quantity (x) in Cossic Algebra

Renaissance abacus term (translating Arabic shay’ / Latin res) for the first power of the unknown in algebraic equations.

cosmographia¶

Language: Greek / Latin / Italian | Translation: Cosmography / Mathematical Geography

The mathematical description and projection of the terrestrial globe in correspondence with the celestial spheres, practiced by Ptolemy, Strabo, and Marinus (Compendio Cap. II–III).

cotozzo (occipite) (cotozzo)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Nape / Occiput of the Head

The rear occipital point of the human skull forming the apex of the equilateral triangle that governs the facial profile on Folio 0137 (Architectura Cap. I–II).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 2 (EN), Arch. Cap. 2 (IT), Arch. index

crosiera (intersecatione de li diametri) (crosiera)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Cross-Intersection of Diagonals and Circle

In Pacioli’s Roman geometric alphabet (Folios 0139–0183), the key construction points where the diagonals of the bounding square intersect one another or the inscribed circle.

Occurrences: Plates: Alphabet (IT)

cubo (cu.) (cubo-algebraico)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: The Cube of the Unknown (x³) in Cossic Algebra

Abacus algebraic term for the third power of the unknown quantity, produced by multiplying the square (censo) by the root (cosa) (Tabula Folio 0016, Libellus).

decagono equilatero (decagono)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Regular Decagon

A ten-sided equilateral polygon inscribed in a circle; its side is the greater segment when the radius (side of the inscribed hexagon) is divided according to the Divine Proportion (Euclid XIII.9–10; Compendio Cap. XV–XVI, XXIX).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 16 (IT), Comp. Cap. 17 (IT), Comp. Cap. 20 (IT), Comp. Cap. 29 (IT), Comp. Cap. 31 (IT), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT)

denticeli / rastro (denticeli)¶

Language: Latin / Italian | Translation: Dentils (‘Rake’ Molding)

Series of small rectangular tooth-like blocks beneath the corona of an Ionic or Corinthian cornice, called ‘rastro’ (rake) by Renaissance stonecutters (Folio 0215).

Occurrences: Plates: Architectural (IT)

diagonale¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Diagonal of a Quadrate or Parallelogram

Defined in Chapter LXXI: the straight line drawn from one angle to the opposite angle of a quadrilateral figure, dividing it into two triangles.

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 4 (DE), Arch. Cap. 4 (IT), Comp. Cap. 27 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 35 (DE), Comp. Cap. 67 (DE), Comp. Cap. 71 (DE), Plates: Anmerkungen (DE)

divina proportione (divina-proportione)¶

Language: Italian / Latin | Translation: Divine Proportion (Golden Ratio, φ = (1 + √5)/2)

Pacioli’s theological and mathematical name for extreme and mean ratio (Euclid VI.def.3, II.11, VI.30, XIII.1–18), possessing five analogies with God: unity, trinity of terms, irrationality/ineffability, self-similarity (invariability), and formal generation of the cosmic dodecahedron and the five Platonic solids.

Dodecahedron / duodecedron (corpo de dodeci pentagoni) (dodecahedron)¶

Language: Latin / Greek / Italian | Translation: Regular Dodecahedron (12-Base Pentagonal Body, Figure of the Cosmos)

The fifth and noblest regular solid, bounded by 12 regular pentagons, 30 edges, and 20 three-valent solid angles; constructed in Euclid XIII.17 via the Divine Proportion (Compendio Cap. XXX, Plates XXVII–XXXIV) and assigned by Plato to the Fifth Essence (Heaven).

Dodecahedron abscisum (Δωδεκάεδρον ἀποτετμημένον / Icosidodecahedron) (dodecahedron-abscisum)¶

Language: Latin / Greek | Translation: Icosidodecahedron (32-Base Truncated Dodecahedron)

Archimedean quasi-regular solid obtained by bisecting the thirty edges of a regular dodecahedron, bounded by 12 regular pentagons and 20 equilateral triangles, with 60 edges and 30 vertices (Compendio Cap. LII, Plates XXIX–XXX).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 46 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 52 (EN), Comp. Cap. 52 (Notes), Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

Dodecahedron abscisum elevatum (Δωδεκάεδρον ἀποτετμημένον ἐπηρμένον) (dodecahedron-abscisum-elevatum)¶

Language: Latin / Greek | Translation: Elevated Icosidodecahedron

Thirty-two-pointed stellated polyhedron formed by erecting 12 pentagonal pyramids and 20 triangular pyramids of equilateral triangles upon the 32 faces of a core icosidodecahedron, bounded externally by 120 equilateral triangles (Compendio Cap. LII, Plates XXXIII–XXXIV).

Occurrences: Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

Dodecahedron elevatum (Δωδεκάεδρον ἐπηρμένον) (dodecahedron-elevatum)¶

Language: Latin / Greek | Translation: Elevated (Stellated) Dodecahedron

Twelve-pointed stellated polyhedron formed by erecting 12 pentagonal pyramids of equilateral triangles upon the 12 pentagonal faces of a core regular dodecahedron, bounded externally by 60 equilateral triangles and requiring the Divine Proportion at every vertex (Compendio Cap. LII, Plates XXXI–XXXII).

Occurrences: Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

dyametro¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Diameter (διάμετρος)

Defined in Chapter LXXI: a straight line passing through the center of a circle or sphere and terminated at both ends by the circumference, bisecting the figure.

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 7 (IT), Comp. Cap. 26 (IT), Comp. Cap. 27 (IT), Comp. Cap. 31 (IT), Comp. Cap. 32 (IT), Comp. Cap. 56 (IT), Comp. Cap. 58 (IT), Comp. Cap. 62 (IT), Comp. Cap. 66 (IT), Comp. Cap. 67 (IT), Comp. Cap. 71 (IT)

echino / huovolo (echino)¶

Language: Greek / Italian | Translation: Echinus / Ovolo (Egg-and-Dart Molding)

Convex quarter-round classical molding, called ‘echino’ by the ancients and ‘huovolo’ (egg-molding) by Renaissance masons (Folio 0215).

Occurrences: Arch. Cap. 6 (EN), Arch. Cap. 6 (IT), Arch. Cap. 14 (EN), Arch. Cap. 14 (IT), Plates: Architectural (IT)

effecto (effetto)¶

Language: Italian | Translation: Miraculous Mathematical Effect / Theorem

Pacioli’s term in Chapters VII–XXIII for the thirteen passion-like mathematical properties (‘mirabili, ineffabili, inestimabili effecti’) of a line divided according to the Divine Proportion, derived from Euclid XIII.1–6 and XIV.2.

elevatum / elevato / pontuto (ἐπηρμένον) (elevatum)¶

Language: Latin / Italian / Greek | Translation: Elevated / Stellated (Cumulated) Polyhedron

A stellated polyhedron formed by erecting equilateral triangular pyramids upon every polygonal face of a core convex polyhedron (such as the elevated octahedron, later rediscovered by Kepler as the Stella Octangula).

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 48 (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (EN), Plates: Polyhedra (LA)

elmuariffe (trapezium) (elmuariffe)¶

Language: Arabic / Italian | Translation: Irregular Quadrilateral / Trapezium

Campanus’s Arabic-derived term (from al-munḥarif) used by Pacioli in Chapter LX for irregular four-sided plane figures that are neither squares, rectangles, rhombi, nor rhomboids.

Occurrences: Comp. Cap. 60 (DE), Comp. Cap. 60 (EN), Comp. Cap. 60 (IT), Comp. Cap. 60 (Notes), Comp. Cap. 63 (EN)

elmuhaym (rhombus) (elmuhaym)¶

Language: Arabic / Italian | Translation: Rhombus (Equilateral Non-Right-Angled Quadrilateral)

Campanus’s Arabic-derived term (from al-mu’ayyan) used by Pacioli in Chapter LX for an equilateral four-sided figure whose angles are not right angles.

epistilio / architrave (epistilio)¶

Language: Greek / Latin / Italian | Translation: Epistyle / Architrave
