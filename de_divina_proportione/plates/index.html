Appendices: Woodcut Plates & Mathematical Commentary¶

Roman Geometric Alphabet, Architectural Plates, Leonardo da Vinci’s Sixty Polyhedra & Winterberg’s Sachliche Anmerkungen

Appendix I: The Roman Geometric Alphabet

In Part II (Tractato de l’Architectura, Cap. VI and XI), Fra Luca Pacioli explains that the ancient imperial capitals can be constructed to mathematical perfection with only two Euclidean instruments—the straightedge (libella, straight line) and compass (circino, curved line)—within a master square subdivided into nine and ten parts. The twenty-three full-page woodcuts printed at Venice by Paganino de’ Paganini in 1509 are presented below as atomic units with synchronized English, Italian (1509), and German (Winterberg 1889) commentary.

Appendix II: Architectural Plates & Arbor Proportionis

Five full-page architectural woodcuts printed at Venice in 1509 illustrating the proportional canons of Vitruvius, the human profile in the equilateral triangle, the Temple Portal (Porta Speciosa), the classical orders and mouldings, and the Boethian Tree of Proportions (Arbor Proportionis et Proportionalitatis).

Appendix III: Polyhedra Plates by Leonardo da Vinci

Sixty full-page woodcut plates of the Five Regular Platonic Solids and their Truncated (abscisa), Elevated/Stellated (elevata), and Prismatic/Pyramidal/Spherical Dependent Bodies in Solid (planum/solidum) and Skeletal (vacuum) perspective views, drawn at Milan by Leonardo da Vinci (per mano del nostro Lionardo da Vinci Fiorentino). Opening any plate provides synchronized switching between Leonardo’s English descriptive commentary and the original 1509 Latin and Greek woodcut cartouches.

Appendix IV: Winterberg’s Mathematical Commentary (1889)

Constantin Winterberg appended seventeen substantive mathematical notes (Sachliche Anmerkungen, pp. 338–352) to his 1889 Vienna edition, reformulating Pacioli's numerical examples in modern algebraic notation, correcting Pacioli's claim in Cap. XLVI regarding the inscription of the octahedron in the icosahedron, and establishing a complete projective and polar-reciprocity theory of the twenty-one mutual inscriptions among the five Platonic solids.
