CHAPTER XII — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

On the Orders and Spacing (Intercolumniations / Tetrants) of Round Columns in Buildings, and How the Architect Must Adapt Symmetry to the Site and Load (Vitruvius Book V).

Historical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Vitruvius III.3 (Intercolumniations) and V.6.7 (Tempering *Symmetria* by *Eurythmia* and *Usus*): Pacioli pairs Vitruvius’s classification of intercolumniations (Pycnostyle \(1\frac{1}{2}D\), Systyle \(2D\), Eustyle \(2\frac{1}{4}D\), Diastyle \(3D\), Araeostyle \(>3D\)) with the famous passage from Book V Chapter 6 on theaters, explaining why human-scale functional elements (steps, balustrades, passages) cannot simply be scaled down linearly in smaller buildings.

Constantin Winterberg’s Commentary (1889 Edition)¶

  • Winterberg Footnotes 1–2 (Arch. Cap. XII, p. 318): (1) Pacioli writes tygraphi for Vitruvius’s triglyphi (triglyphs). (2) In the 1509 print, read tecto (‘roof’) instead of lecto (‘bed’). (Winterberg Fussnoten 1–2 (Arch. Cap. XII, S. 318): 1) Tygraphen = Triglyphen. 2) Text l. tecto st. lecto.)
