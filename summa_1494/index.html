Summary of Arithmetic, Geometry, Proportions and Proportionality¶

Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita — Fra Luca Pacioli (Venice, 1494)

Author: Fra Luca Pacioli, O.F.M. (c. 1447–1517)
Printer: Paganino de' Paganini (Venice, November 1494)
Patrons: Guidobaldo da Montefeltro, Duke of Urbino & Marco Sanudo

Table of Contents¶

DEDICATIONS, CONTINENTIA & SPECULATIVE ARITHMETIC

Quantity (quantità), Magnanimous Duke, is immediately twofold—namely continuous (continua, divisible without end: line, surface, body, place, time) and discrete (discreta, proceeding from the indivisible unit: number and speech)—establishing the fourfold mathematical Quadrivium of Arithmetic, Music, Geometry, and Astronomy (Boethius, De Institutione Arithmetica I.1).

ALGORISMUS, EUCLIDEAN SURDS & COSSIC ALGEBRA (REGOLA DELA COSA)

  1. First Compound Canon (*Primi canonis versus*, `fol. 145r`) — Censo e cose equale a numero ($x^2 + b x = c$): “Si res et census numero coaequantur, a rebus dimidio sumpto censum producere debes, addere quem numero, radicem tollere toti, dimidiumque rei de radice abjicere.” Halve the cose ($b/2$), square that half ($(b/2)^2$), add it to the numero ($c$), extract the square root, and subtract half the cose:

    $$x = \sqrt{\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 + c} - \frac{b}{2}$$

  2. Second Compound Canon (*Secundi canonis versus*) — Censo e numero equale a cose ($x^2 + c = b x$): Valid whenever $(b/2)^2 \ge c$, yielding two positive roots $x = \frac{b}{2} \mp \sqrt{(b/2)^2 - c}$ (proved via [II.5]).

  3. Third Compound Canon (*Tertii canonis versus*) — Cose e numero equale a censo ($b x + c = x^2$): Yielding $x = \sqrt{(b/2)^2 + c} + \frac{b}{2}$ (proved via [II.6]).

TRACTATUS DE PROPORTIONIBUS ET PROPORTIONALITATIBUS

Between any two unequal positive magnitudes $a = AP$ and $b = PB$ set end-to-end on diameter AB of semicircle (MB) with midpoint M, a single Euclidean figure exhibits the Arithmetic ($A$), Geometric ($G$), and Harmonic ($H$) means and proves their geometric proportionality $G^2 = A \cdot H$.

PARTICULARIS DE COMPUTIS ET SCRIPTURIS & THE PROBLEM OF POINTS

Fundamental Conservation Law of the Quaderno (`Cap. 14, fol. 202r`): “Never must anything be placed in **Dare* that is not also placed in the same amount in Avere, and conversely never must anything be placed in Avere that is not placed in the same amount in Dare; whence at the closing balance of the book, the grand total of Dare must equal the grand total of Avere.”*

TRACTATUS GEOMETRIAE: HERON, SURFACE DIVISION & REGULAR SOLIDS

Opening the separately foliated second tome (Tractatus Geometriae, fols. 1r–2r), Fra Luca Pacioli condenses the foundational definitions and constructions of Euclid Books I, II, and VI before turning in Distinctio I, Cap. 8 (fol. 9v) to the crown jewel of practical surveyor’s mensuration inherited from Heron of Alexandria and Leonardo Pisano's Practica Geometriae: determining the exact area, perpendicular cathetus (catetto), and incircle of a scalene triangle from its three sides alone.
