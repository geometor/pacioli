PARTICULARIS DE COMPUTIS ET SCRIPTURIS & THE PROBLEM OF POINTS¶

Part I, Distinctio IX, Tractatus X–XI: Interrupted Games of Chance (fol. 197r–197v) & Venetian Double-Entry Bookkeeping (fols. 198v–210v)

f. Part I, fols. 197r–197v

The Game of Ball (Giuoco della Balla) & Problem 51 (A Balestrare)

  • Game of Ball (`fol. 197r`): A company plays at ball (a balla) to $60$, and each winning chase (caccia) is worth $10$; they lay down a stake of $10$ ducats. By certain accidents they cannot finish: one side finds itself with $50$ and the other with $20$ (or $30$). It is asked what share belongs to each side out of the said $10$ ducats.

  • Problem 51 — Three Crossbowmen (`fol. 197v`): Three companions set themselves to shoot with the crossbow (a balestrare) upon this agreement: that whoever first makes $6$ best shots shall win $10$ ducats. It happens that when the first has made $4$ best shots, the second $3$, and the third $2$, they wish to shoot no more and by mutual consent wish to divide the stake: I ask what each ought to have.

Dividing the stake in the manner of a commercial partnership (compagnia) proportionally to the rounds won yields $\left(\frac{5}{8}, \frac{3}{8}\right) \times 10 = \left(6\frac{1}{4}, 3\frac{3}{4}\right)$ ducats for $(5, 3)$, and $\left(\frac{4}{9}, \frac{3}{9}, \frac{2}{9}\right) \times 10 = \left(4\frac{4}{9}, 3\frac{1}{3}, 2\frac{2}{9}\right)$ ducats for $(4, 3, 2)$.

f. Part I, fols. 198v–210v

Distinctio Nona — Tractatus XI: Particularis de Computis et Scripturis (Cap. 1–14)

Since, as has been said, three things above all are needful to everyone who intends to attend to trade with due order (Cap. 1, fol. 198v):

  1. The first and most principal is ready counted cash (la pecunia numerata) and every other substantial faculty.

  2. The second thing required for due commerce is that one be a good reckoner and prompt accountant (buon ragionieri e pronto computista).

  3. The third and final needful thing is that one arrange all one’s affairs in fair and due order, so that with brevity one may have knowledge of each item as to both its debit (debito) and its credit (credito). And herein we shall observe the manner of Venice (el modo de Vinegia), which certainly above all others is greatly to be commended.

Before trading, draw up the Opening Inventory on a single day (Cap. 2–3, fols. 198v–199r, exemplified on November 8, 1493), and maintain the Three Principal Books (Cap. 5–14, fols. 199v–202r), presented first to the Venetian Consoli de’ Mercanti (Cap. 7):

  • Memoriale (Squarzo / Vacchetta): Day-book recording all transactions hour-by-hour in whatever coin they occur.

  • Giornale: Private chronological journal wherein two terms are used (Cap. 11–12, fol. 201r): Per always denotes the debtor, and A denotes the creditor. And note that Cassa is always debtor or even, and can never be creditor—for if in the balance Cash should remain creditor, it would denote error in the book, since no one can spend what he did not have in the chest. By Cavedal is understood your entire present capital stock. Thus runs the first entry of the Journal (fol. 201v): M.cccc.xciii, on the 8th day of November in Venice. Per Cash in coin // A Capital of myself, etc.

  • Quaderno Grande: General ledger with alphabetical Repertorio, setting Dare on the left hand and Avere on the right hand across four columns of Venetian money of account: know that in Venice $24$ grossi make $1$ ducat, $32$ piccoli make $1$ grosso, $12$ grossi make $1$ soldo di grossi, and $20$ soldi make $1$ lira di grossi (worth $10$ gold ducats), so the four columns signify lire, soldi, grossi, piccoli.

Fundamental Law of Double-Entry (`Cap. 14, fol. 202r`): Know that of every single entry you have placed in the Journal you must make two in the Quaderno: namely one in Dare and the other in Avere. For never must anything be placed in Dare that is not likewise placed with the same quantity in Avere; and thus never must anything be placed in Avere that is not likewise placed with its amount in Dare. Hence arises at the closing balance of the book that the one sum must equal the other, namely the summa summarum of Dare and of Avere.
[Euclid I.cn.2 & Ledger Equilibrium]

In the directed ledger circuit on circle (OK), each Giornale posting Per u // A v connects accounts K (Cavedal), S (Cassa), M (Zenzer), D (Martino), P (Pro e Danno), and B (Bilancio)—opening cash KS, ginger purchase SM, credit sale MD and gain DP, collection DS, and profit transfer PK closing the cycle KSMDP. Because every posting enters equal weight in Dare and in Avere, the grand equilibrium holds unconditionally:

$$\sum_{u} (\text{Dare}_u - \text{Avere}_u) = 0 \iff \text{Summa}(\text{Dare}) = \text{Summa}(\text{Avere})$$

Observing that Cassa can never show a net credit balance ($\text{Dare}_{\text{Cassa}} - \text{Avere}_{\text{Cassa}} \ge 0$, Cap. 12), sweeping all gain and loss through Pro e Danno into Cavedal (Cap. 27), and closing open accounts via SB and KB into Bilancio del libro (Cap. 34) yields the exact Summa summarum of the entire ledger.
