TRACTATUS GEOMETRIAE: HERON, SURFACE DIVISION & REGULAR SOLIDS¶

Part II, Distinctiones I–VIII: Plane Mensuration, Archimedes’s Circle, De Divisione Superficierum, Inscribed Polyhedra & the 1494 Colophon (fols. 1r–76r)

f. Part II, fols. 1r–9v

Distinctio Prima, Capitulum Octavum — Rule of Heron on the (13, 14, 15) Triangle (Part II, fol. 9v)

Having first set down the foundational definitions and constructions of Euclid Books I, II, and VI (fols. 1r–2r), we turn to measuring the area, perpendicular cathetus (catetto), and inscribed circle of a scalene triangle from its three sides alone, following Heron and Leonardo Pisano's Practica Geometriae.

[Heron's Formula & Euclid II.13 / IV.4]

Let the triangle GBA be such that along side GA it is \(13\), along side BA it is \(15\), and along base GB it is \(14\) braccia ([I.22]). Add all three sides together: they make \(42\); take the half thereof, which is \(21\). Subtract each side separately: there remain \(6\), \(7\), and \(8\):

\[21 - 15 = 6, \qquad 21 - 14 = 7, \qquad 21 - 13 = 8\]

Multiply \(6\) by \(7\), which makes \(42\), and this by \(8\), which makes \(336\); and multiply this by the aforesaid half, namely \(21\): it will make \(7056\), whose square root is precisely \(84\) square braccia for the entire area of the said triangle:

\[\text{Area}(\triangle GBA) = \sqrt{7056} = 84 \text{ square braccia}\]

And dividing double the area (\(168\)) by base GB (\(14\)) yields \(12\) for the cathetus AD, falling at D with segments GD \(= 5\) (in right triangle GDA) and DB \(= 9\) (in right triangle DBA). Dividing area \(84\) by semiperimeter \(21\) gives the inscribed circle (IT) centered at I of radius IT \(= 4\) ([IV.4]).

f. Part II, fol. 31r & fols. 34r–43v

Measurement of the Circle (fol. 31r), Division of Surfaces (Dist. IV) & Three Circles in a Triangle (fol. 57r)

By Archimedes of Syracuse in Dimensio circuli (fol. 31r), by means of the \(96\)-sided polygon within and without the circle, the circumference to the diameter is proved to be less than tripla sesquisettima (\(3\frac{1}{7}\)) and greater than \(3\frac{10}{71}\). And in Distinctio IV (De divisione superficierum), we divide triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles in prescribed ratios \(p : q\) both through vertices ([VI.1]) and parallel to a side ([VI.13], [VI.19]).

[Euclid VI.13 & VI.19]To bisect the area (\(84\)) of GBA by a cut UV parallel to BA: erect perpendicular MS at midpoint M of semicircle (MB) to meet the arc at S, and transfer chord GS \(= \sqrt{14 \cdot 7} = \sqrt{98} = 7\sqrt{2}\) via arc (GS) onto GB at U (equal). Drawing UV parallel to BA cuts off similar sub-triangle GUV of exact area \(\frac{98}{196} \cdot 84 = 42\).

In Distinctio V–VI we gauge wine casks (botte) and solve circle packings (fol. 57r): given a triangle whose two equal sides are each \(10\) and whose base is \(12\), I wish to place inside it \(3\) equal circles, the largest that can fit therein. Operating by the Rule of the Thing, you will arrive at \(5\frac{3}{4}\) censi and \(16\) cose equal to \(48\), whence the radius \(r = \frac{8\sqrt{85} - 32}{23}\) is made manifest.

f. Part II, fols. 68v–70r & 76r

Distinctio Octava — De Corporibus Regularibus & Venetian Colophon (November 10, 1494)

In Distinctio VIII (fols. 68v–70v), we inscribe the five regular bodies ([XIII.13]–[XIII.17]) and their dependent bodies (following Piero della Francesca, Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus) inside a sphere (OP) of diameter \(D = 6\) (radius \(R = 3\)):

  • Tetrahedron (`fol. 69r`): Possanza \(e^2 = \frac{2}{3} D^2 = 24\) (\(e = \sqrt{24}\)); Cube: \(e^2 = \frac{1}{3} D^2 = 12\) (\(e = \sqrt{12}\)); Octahedron (`fol. 68v`): \(e^2 = \frac{1}{2} D^2 = 18\) (\(e = \sqrt{18}\)).

  • Truncated Tetrahedron (`fol. 69v, prob. 15–16`): Let there be a sphere whose diameter is \(6\); I wish to place within it the body of \(4\) triangular and \(4\) hexagonal bases. You will find its side to be \(\mathcal{R}.6\frac{6}{11}\) (namely \(\sqrt{72/11}\)).

  • Cuboctahedron (`fol. 69v–70r, prob. 17–19`): Wishing to place in the same sphere of diameter \(6\) the body of \(14\) bases, namely \(6\) square and \(8\) triangular, I say its side is precisely half the diameter of the sphere, namely \(3\) braccia, forming the equatorial regular hexagon PQRSTU across diameters PS, QT, and RU, with side PQ equal to the sphere radius \(R = 3\).

  • Hydrostatic Mensuration & Dodecahedron (`fol. 70r, prob. 20–21`): Just as Archimedes found the measure of a statue or irregular body by water overflowing from a filled vessel (Prob. 20), let there be a sphere whose axis has possanza \(48\) (\(\text{axis} = \mathcal{R}.48\), Prob. 21): I seek the side of the dodecahedron inscribed therein (spanned with the icosahedron by golden rectangle WXYZ, WX : XY \(= \phi : 1\)). Take one third of \(48\), which is \(16\), whose root \(4\) is the side of the inscribed cube ([XIII.15]); divide \(4\) according to the proportion having extreme and mean ratio, whose greater part \(\mathcal{R}.20 \;\bar{m}\; 2\) is the side of the dodecahedron, and the lesser part is \(6 \;\bar{m}\; \mathcal{R}.20\).

Colophon (`Part II, fol. 76r`): At the expense, diligence, and workmanship of the prudent man Paganino de' Paganini of Brescia, in the exalted city of Venice, by grace of its exalted Dominion for ten years that no other may reprint it, under the most felicitous government of Our Most Serene Prince Agostino Barbarigo, Brother Luca of Borgo Sansepolcro of the Order of Minorites and humble professor of Sacred Theology has shown forth his small judgment and strength—in the year of our Salvation M.cccc.lxliiii, on the 10th day of November.
