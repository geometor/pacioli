CHAPTER XVI
===========

.. rubric:: On the Composition and Mouldings of the Cornice (*Cornicione*).

.. folio:: 0079 (f. 32r)
   :initial: 0079.c2.png

Above the frieze (:term:`*zophoro* <zophoro>`, :math:`V`) is assembled the crowning upper block called the **cornice** (*cornice* by the Ancients, :term:`*cornicione* <cornicione>` by the Moderns, depicted with its letters :math:`k, l, m, o` in :ref:`Plate 0215 <plates-architectural-en>`).

.. assertion:: Sequence of Mouldings in the Cornice from Bottom to Top (:ref:`Plate 0215 <plates-architectural-en>`)

   Starting immediately above the frieze :math:`V` and ascending in order to the summit:

   1. **The Frieze Cymatium and Five Stepped Fillets (*Gradetti / Tenie*, marked :math:`k`)**:
      Immediately upon the frieze is placed a small rectangular fillet or reglet (*regolo ouer gradetto*, called *tenia* by the Ancients), projecting outward by an amount equal to its own height; in richly moulded cornices (as seen in :ref:`Plate 0215 <plates-architectural-en>`), up to **five** such small stepped fillets and mouldings (:math:`k`) are placed for ornamental articulation between the frieze and the dentils.
   2. **The Dentils (*Denticoli / Denticelli / Rastro*, marked :math:`l`)**:
      Above the taenia :math:`k` rests the broader band called **denticoli** by :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`, **denticelli** by modern masons, and sometimes **rastro** from its likeness to the teeth of a rake (*rastrello facto a denti*).
   3. **Taenia and Astragal (*Paternostri / Fusaroli*)**:
      Above the dentils :math:`l` is placed another narrow fillet (*tenia*), then a rounded bead-and-reel moulding (*bastone detto paternostri ouero fusaroli*), and above that another fillet (*quadra ouer tenia*).
   4. **The Corona or Dripstone (*Corona / Gocciolatoio*, marked :math:`m`)**:
      Next is set the projecting **corona** (:math:`m`), so called by the Ancients and named the **gocciolatoio** (dripstone) by our modern masons because its broad overhang throws rainwater clear of the frieze, :term:`architrave <epistilio>`, and columns below.
   5. **Upper Taenia, Bead-and-Reel (*Paternostri*), and Fillet**:
      Above the corona :math:`m` follow in order another fillet (*tenia*), another course of bead-and-reel (*paternostri e fusaroli*), and another small fillet (*quadretta*).
   6. **The Sima or Cyma Recta (*Sima / Gola dela Cornice*, marked :math:`o`)**:
      Penultimately rises the curved **sima** (:math:`o`), which our modern masons call the **throat of the cornice** (*gola dela cornice*).
   7. **The Crowning Acroterium or Fillet (*Acrotherio / Tenia*)**:
      Finally, at the very top above the sima :math:`o`, is placed the crowning fillet or **acroterium** (*acrotherio*).

   And throughout the entire cornice—just as we enjoined for the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` (:ref:`Chapter VI <architectura-cap_06-en>`) and the epistyle (:ref:`Chapter XIV <architectura-cap_14-en>`)—**every single step and moulding must project outward (*sporti in fore*) to the right and left by an amount equal to its own vertical height** (:math:`1 : 1` projection), and all blocks must be securely cramped and leaded together (*ben incatenato con ferri e piombi*) so that the whole edifice stands unshakable and harmonious to the eye!
