CHAPTER XVIII — Critical Commentary & Notes
===========================================

.. rubric:: On Its Ninth Effect, Surpassing All the Others

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Intersecting Diagonals of the Regular Pentagon (Euclid XIII.8 and Euclid XIII.11)**: Proposition 8 of Book XIII proves that if two diagonals :g:`ac` and :g:`be` of an equilateral and equiangular pentagon :g:`abcde` intersect at :g:`f`, both diagonals cut one another in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` ($ac : cf = cf : af$ and $be : ef = ef : bf$), and their greater segments equal the pentagon's side ($cf = ef = ab$). :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` also cites :cite:`Euclid XIII.11 <euclid:elements:XIII.11>` (which proves that the side of the regular pentagon inscribed in a circle with rational diameter is the irrational line called a *minor*, $\dot{\varepsilon}\lambda\acute{\alpha}\sigma\sigma\omega\nu$, constructed from a fourth binomial and fourth :term:`apotome <residuo>`) because his *Libellus* (Tractatus I) and *Summa* extensively use :cite:`Euclid XIII.8 <euclid:elements:XIII.8>` and :cite:`Euclid XIII.11 <euclid:elements:XIII.11>` together to calculate all dimensions of the pentagon and :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`.

Constantin Winterberg's Commentary (1889 Edition)
-------------------------------------------------

.. rubric:: Note 7 (Cap. XVIII): Ninth Effect — Diagonals of the Regular Pentagon (Euclid XIII.8) (:ref:`Sachliche Anmerkungen #7 <plates-anmerkungen-en>`, Cap. XVIII, p. 206, l. 11 from bottom)

The proof follows immediately from the similarity of the isosceles triangles $\triangle bca \sim \triangle baf$ formed by the two intersecting chords $ac$ and $be$ (meeting at $f$) of the regular pentagon:

.. math::

   \frac{ca}{ba} = \frac{ba}{af} \implies ba^2 = ca \cdot af,

and since $ba = bc = cf$, we have $cf^2 = ca \cdot af$, so the chord $ca$ is cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` at $f$.

.. rubric:: Anmerkung 7 (Cap. XVIII, S. 206): Neunter Effect — Diagonalen des regulären Fünfecks (Euklid XIII.8)

Der Beweis folgt aus der Aehnlichkeit der gleichschenkligen Dreiecke $\triangle bca$ und $\triangle baf$, wonach sich unmittelbar ergibt:

.. math::

   \frac{ca}{ba} = \frac{ba}{af}, \quad \text{d. h.} \quad ba^2 = ca \cdot af,

oder, da $ba = bc = cf$ ist: $cf^2 = ca \cdot af$.
