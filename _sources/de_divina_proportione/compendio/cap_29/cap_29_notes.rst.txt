CHAPTER XXIX — Critical Commentary & Notes
==========================================

.. rubric:: How the Fourth Regular Body, Called the Ycocedron of Twenty Triangular Bases, Is Formed in the Sphere.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Paganini 1509 Marginal Diagram**: Folio 0034 (``f. 9v``) includes diagram ``0034.g1.png``, showing the sphere diameter divided in :math:`4:1` ratio (:math:`ac = 4\,cb`) and the circle containing the pentagon and decagon.
- **Role of the Divine Proportion**: This construction brings together nearly every theorem proved in Section 2: :cite:`Euclid XIII.9 <euclid:elements:XIII.9>` (sum of hexagon and decagon sides divided in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`), :cite:`Euclid XIII.10 <euclid:elements:XIII.10>` (:math:`s_{5}^{2} = s_{6}^{2} + s_{10}^{2}`), and :cite:`Euclid XIII.11 <euclid:elements:XIII.11>` (the side of the pentagon is a *Minor*, :cite:`Euclid X.76 <euclid:elements:X.76>`).
- **Exact Formula**: For a sphere of diameter :math:`D`, the radius of the circumcircle of the two parallel pentagonal cross-sections is :math:`R = \frac{D}{\sqrt{5}}`, and the edge of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` is :math:`a_{20} = R\sqrt{\frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{2}} = D\sqrt{\frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{10}}`. For :math:`D = 10`, :math:`a_{20} = \sqrt{50 - 10\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{50 - \sqrt{500}}`.

Constantin Winterberg's Commentary (1889 Edition)
-------------------------------------------------

.. rubric:: Note 12 (Cap. XXIX): Construction of the Regular Icosahedron in the Sphere (Euclid XIII.16) (:ref:`Sachliche Anmerkungen #12 <plates-anmerkungen-en>`, Cap. XXIX, p. 220, l. 15 from top)

The edge $a$ of the regular :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` in terms of the circumradius $r$ of the sphere is:

.. math::

   a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{10 + 2\sqrt{5}}}.

Passing a plane through the sphere diameter $ab = 2r$ and a polar cap edge $ad = a$ (which cuts the sphere in a semicircle $adb$), the projection $ac$ of $ad$ on the diameter $ab$ satisfies:

.. math::

   ac = \frac{ad^2}{ab} = \frac{a^2}{2r} = r\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right), \qquad \text{whence } cm = r - ac = \frac{r}{\sqrt{5}},

and therefore the middle axial segment between the two parallel pentagon planes is $cc' = 2\,cm = \frac{2r}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{5}}$, where $d = 2r$ is the sphere diameter.

Now in the auxiliary semicircle of Chapter XXIX with $ac_{\text{aux}} = 2\,bc_{\text{aux}}$, we have $bd = \frac{d}{\sqrt{5}} = cc'$—that is, $bd$ equals the height of the ten-triangle antiprism belt (the segments called :term:`*catheti* <catheto>` in the text) erected perpendicular to the two pentagon planes at $c$ and $c'$. Moreover, the circumradius $r'$ of these two parallel pentagons (and of the inscribed decagon) with pentagon side $a$ is:

.. math::

   r' = a\sqrt{\frac{5 + \sqrt{5}}{10}} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{5}} = bd,

so the circumradius $r'$ of the pentagon circle equals both the line $bd$ of the text and the axial height $cc'$ of the perpendicular :term:`*catheti* <catheto>`, exactly as constructed in :person:`Euclid <euclid>` :cite:`Euclid XIII.16 <euclid:elements:XIII.16>`!

.. rubric:: Anmerkung 12 (Cap. XXIX, S. 220): Construction des regulären Ikosaeders in der Kugel (Euklid XIII.16)

Bekanntlich ist die Seite $a$ des regulären :term:`Ikosaeders <icosahedron>`, durch den Halbmesser $r$ der umschriebenen Kugel ausgedrückt:

.. math::

   a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{10 + 2\sqrt{5}}}.

Denkt man sich nun durch die Axe $ab = 2r$ und eine Kante $ad$ des :term:`Ikosaeders <icosahedron>` eine Ebene gelegt, welche zugleich die Kugel im Halbkreise $adb$ schneidet, so ist nach bekannten Sätzen:

.. math::

   ac = \frac{ad^2}{ab} = \frac{a^2}{2r} = r\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right),

woraus hervorgeht, dass $cm = r - ac = \frac{r}{\sqrt{5}}$, somit das Doppelte $cc' = \frac{2r}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{5}}$, wenn der Kugeldurchmesser mit $d = 2r$ bezeichnet wird. Nun ist dem Text zufolge $\frac{bd}{d} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$, sonach $bd = cc'$, d. h. gleich der Länge der im Text als Katheten bezeichneten Höhen, welche man in den Ecken der Fünfecke zu errichten hat. Der Radius $r'$ des diesen Fünfecken umschriebenen Kreises ist aber, wenn $a$ zugleich Fünfecksseite sein soll:

.. math::

   r' = a\sqrt{\frac{5 + \sqrt{5}}{10}} = \frac{d}{\sqrt{5}} = bd,

d. h. es muss $r'$ genau gleich der Linie $bd$ des Textes und zugleich gleich den als Katheten bezeichneten Höhen sein, wie solches der dort angegebenen Construction zufolge auch wirklich der Fall ist.
