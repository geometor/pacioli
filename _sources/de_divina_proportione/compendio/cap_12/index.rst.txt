Chapter XII: On Its Third Singular Effect
=========================================

.. rubric:: On Its Third Singular Effect

.. folio:: 0027 (f. 6r)
   :initial: 0027.c1.png

.. enunciation::

   If a quantity be divided according to our proportion, and to its **lesser part** be added the **half of the greater part**, the **square of their sum** will always be **quintuple** the **square of the half of the said greater part** (:just:`XIII.3`).

.. rubric:: Numerical Demonstration

.. assertion::

   For example, let :math:`10` be the quantity divided according to our :term:`Divine Proportion <divina-proportione>`, so that one part—namely the greater (:math:`a`)—is :math:`\sqrt{125} - 5`, and the lesser (:math:`b`) is :math:`15 - \sqrt{125}`.

   I say that if to :math:`15 - \sqrt{125}`, which is the lesser part, we add the half of :math:`\sqrt{125} - 5`, which is the greater part, the sum of the lesser part and the said half, when multiplied by itself, will be **five times** the square of the half of the said greater part. And this appears as follows:

   1. The half of the greater part (:math:`\sqrt{125} - 5`) is:

      .. math::

         \frac{a}{2} = \sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}} - 2\frac{1}{2}

   2. Adding this to :math:`15 - \sqrt{125}`, which is the lesser part (:math:`b`), yields:

      .. math::

         b + \frac{a}{2} = (15 - \sqrt{125}) + \left(\sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}} - 2\frac{1}{2}\right) = 12\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}}

   3. Multiplying :math:`12\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}}` by :math:`12\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}}` produces:

      .. math::

         \left(b + \frac{a}{2}\right)^2 = 187\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{19531\frac{1}{4}}

      and this is called the **square of the sum**.

   4. Next, let the half of the said greater part also be squared—that is, multiply :math:`\sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}} - 2\frac{1}{2}` by :math:`\sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}} - 2\frac{1}{2}`—which will make:

      .. math::

         \left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 = 37\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{781\frac{1}{4}}

      and this is called the **square of the half of the greater part**, which is converterly one-fifth (:math:`\frac{1}{5}`) of the square of the sum:

      .. math::

         5 \times \left(37\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{781\frac{1}{4}}\right) = 187\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{25 \times 781\frac{1}{4}} = 187\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{19531\frac{1}{4}}

.. qed::

   Consequently, the said square of the sum is **quintuple** the square of the half of the said greater part of :math:`10` thus divided. And this power is to be highly esteemed along with the others, as is proved geometrically in full by the third proposition of the Thirteenth Book (:just:`XIII.3`) of our author.
