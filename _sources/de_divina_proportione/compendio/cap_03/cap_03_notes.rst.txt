CHAPTER III — Critical Commentary & Notes
=========================================

.. rubric:: The Preface Being Ended, There Follows an Explanation of What Is Meant by This Name 'Mathematical'

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **The Lacuna for the Greek Word in the 1509 Printing**: In the 1509 Paganini edition (Folio 0021r), the compositor left a blank space after *derivato da* for the Greek word $\mu\alpha\theta\eta\mu\alpha\tau\iota\kappa\acute{o}\varsigma$ (or $\mu\alpha\nu\theta\acute{\alpha}\nu\omega$ / $\mu\acute{\alpha}\theta\eta\mu\alpha$), which :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` regularly glosses in his *Summa* and his 1509 edition of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` as *disciplinabile*.
2. **Reforming the Quadrivium — Perspective as a Liberal Art**: Chapter III is one of the defining documents of the Italian Renaissance elevation of the visual arts from *artes mechanicae* to *artes liberales*. Challenging :person:`Boethius <boethius>`'s canonical fourfold :term:`*Quadrivium* <quadrivio>` (Arithmetic, Music, Geometry, Astronomy), :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` argues on strictly parallel epistemological and mathematical grounds that :term:`*Prospectiva* <perspectiva>` (linear perspective and the science of visual rays, pioneered by :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>`, :person:`Leon Battista Alberti <alberti>`, and :person:`Leonardo da Vinci <leonardo>`) stands on an equal footing with Music:

   - **Sense Hierarchy**: Sight (*el vedere*, the *prima porta al intellecto*) is nobler than hearing (*l'udito*).
   - **Mathematical Object**: Measure of the visual ray (*linea visuale*) and natural number vs. sonorous number (*numero sonoro*) and musical time (*tempo de sue prolationi*).
   - **Proportional Scope**: Arithmetic and geometric proportions of foreshortening vs. harmonic proportions.

3. **Second Ekphrasis of Leonardo's *:term:`Last Supper <simulacro>`***: Pacioli specifies the exact dramatic moment depicted by Leonardo in the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie: Christ's words *Unus vestrum me traditurus est* (Matthew 26:21), capturing the Apostles' *acti e gesti* ("actions and gestures", corresponding directly to Leonardo's theory of *moti mentali* expressed through bodily movements).
