CHAPTER XLI
===========

.. rubric:: How the Exacedron or Cube Is Placed in the Ycocedron.

.. folio:: 0042 (f. 13v)

The seventh proposition of the fifteenth book (:cite:`Campanus XV.7 <campanus:euclid:XV.7>`) shows how to inscribe the **cube** (6 square bases) inside the :term:`**ycocedron** <icosahedron>` (20 triangular bases) by means of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` first inscribed in the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`.

.. enunciation:: Inscribing the Cube in the Icosahedron (Campanus XV.7)

   In a given regular body of twenty triangular bases (:term:`*ycocedron* <icosahedron>`), to inscribe the cube (:term:`*exacedron* <exahedron>`) so that the eight vertices of the cube stand at eight of the twenty centers of the triangular faces of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`.

.. rubric:: Construction and Proof (via Campanus XV.6 and :just:`XIII.17`)

.. assertion::

   1. First, in the given :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` inscribe the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>` by the method of :ref:`Chapter XL <compendio-cap_40-en>` (:cite:`Campanus XV.6 <campanus:euclid:XV.6>`), so that the twenty vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` stand at the centers of the twenty triangular faces of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` (see the marginal diagram ``0040.g1.png`` on Folio 0040).
   2. Recall from :ref:`Chapter XXX <compendio-cap_30-en>` (:just:`XIII.17`) how the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` was originally constructed upon the **six square faces of a cube**: twelve of the twenty vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` are the roof-ridge vertices, while the remaining **eight vertices** of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` are the eight corners of the underlying cube, and the **twelve edges of that cube** are twelve diagonals (*corde pentagoniche*) drawn across the twelve pentagonal faces of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`!
   3. Accordingly, in the twelve pentagonal faces of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` inscribed in our :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`, draw the twelve pentagonal chords (*corde pentagoniche*) connecting those eight corresponding vertices.
   4. By :just:`XIII.17` and :just:`XIII.8`, these twelve pentagonal chords are mutually equal, meet at right angles in six square faces, and have their eight vertices among the twenty vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`—which are themselves the centers of eight of the twenty triangular faces of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`!

Therefore the cube is inscribed in the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`, and its edge is the **pentagonal chord** (:term:`*corda pentagonica* <corda-pentagonica>`) of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` inscribed in the same :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`, so that the edge of this cube and the edge of the inscribed :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` stand to one another in our :term:`**Divine Proportion** <divina-proportione>`!

.. qed::
