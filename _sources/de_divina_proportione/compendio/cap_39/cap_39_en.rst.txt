CHAPTER XXXIX
=============

.. rubric:: How the Tetracedron Is Formed in the Octocedron.

.. folio:: 0042 (f. 13v)

The fifth proposition of the fifteenth book (:cite:`Campanus XV.5 <campanus:euclid:XV.5>`) shows how to inscribe the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` (4 triangular bases) inside the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>` (8 triangular bases) by means of the cube already inscribed in the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`.

.. enunciation:: Inscribing the Tetrahedron in the Octahedron (Campanus XV.5)

   In a given regular body of eight triangular bases (:term:`*octocedron* <octahedron>`), to inscribe the regular body of four triangular bases (:term:`*tetracedron* <tetrahedron>`) so that the four vertices of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` stand at four alternating centers of the eight faces of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`.

.. rubric:: Construction and Proof (via Campanus XV.4 and XV.2)

.. assertion::

   1. First, in the given :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` inscribe the **cube** :math:`efghklmn` by the method of :ref:`Chapter XXXVIII <compendio-cap_38-en>` (:cite:`Campanus XV.4 <campanus:euclid:XV.4>`), so that the eight vertices of the cube stand at the eight centers of the triangular faces of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` (see the marginal diagram ``0039.g2.png`` on Folio 0039).
   2. Second, in this inscribed cube :math:`efghklmn`, inscribe the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` by the method of :ref:`Chapter XXXVI <compendio-cap_36-en>` (:cite:`Campanus XV.2 <campanus:euclid:XV.2>`), drawing the six face diagonals across the six square faces of the cube to join four non-adjacent vertices of the cube (for instance :math:`e, g, l, n`).
   3. Because the eight vertices of the cube lie at the centers of the eight faces of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`, the four vertices :math:`e, g, l, n` of this :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` likewise stand at four alternating face-centers of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`!
   4. Moreover, since by :ref:`Chapter XXXVIII <compendio-cap_38-en>` the square on the edge of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` has to the square on the edge of its inscribed cube the ratio :math:`9 : 2`, and by :ref:`Chapter XXXVI <compendio-cap_36-en>` the square on the edge of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` inscribed in that cube is **double** (:math:`2 : 1`) the square on the edge of the cube, by *ex aequali* (:just:`V.22`) the square on the edge of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` has to the square on the edge of its inscribed :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` the ratio :math:`9 : 4`:

      .. math::

         s_{\text{octa}}^{2} : s_{\text{tetra}}^{2} = 9 : 4 \implies s_{\text{tetra}} = \frac{2}{3}\,s_{\text{octa}}

Thus the edge of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` inscribed in the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` is **commensurable in length** with the edge of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`, being exactly **two-thirds** (:math:`\frac{2}{3}`) of it!

.. qed::
