CHAPTER LVII — Critical Commentary & Notes
==========================================

.. rubric:: How All Five Regular Bodies Are Placed in the Sphere, How Stonemasons May Carve Them from a Spherical Stone, and the Anecdote of Count Girolamo Riario and Melozzo da Forlì in Rome.

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Count Girolamo Riario, Melozzo da Forlì, and Pacioli in Rome (c. 1477–1480 or 1484–1488)**: This vivid autobiographical narrative is one of the richest historical documents of Renaissance workshop culture:

  - **Count Girolamo Riario** (1443–1488), Lord of Imola and Forlì and Captain General of the Church under his uncle Pope Sixtus IV, began constructing his Roman palace near Sant'Apollinare (incorporated into **Palazzo Altemps**) in the late 1470s / early 1480s.
  - :person:`**Melozzo da Forlì** <melozzo-da-forli>` (1438–1494), the celebrated *pictor papalis* and master of *sotto in su* perspective (who also came from Riario's city of Forlì and knew :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>`), joined :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` in setting the geometric trap for Riario's boastful master stonemason.

- **The Tau (:math:`\mathbf{T}`) Optical Illusion**: :person:`Pacioli's <pacioli>` remark on the two equal rods placed in the shape of a ``T`` (*doi verghette equali in forma de tau, cioè così T, inanze ali occhi suoi, ora vna ora l'altra più longa giudicaua*, Folio 0050–0051) is the **earliest documented scientific description of the Vertical-Horizontal (T) Optical Illusion** in Western history (nearly four centuries before Fick, 1851, and Oppel, 1855)!
- **Comparison with Earlier Draft Track**: The earlier ``divina_en_2/57.txt`` draft cut out the entire second half of this chapter—omitting the three carpenter jokes, the Tau optical illusion, the entire story of Count :person:`Girolamo Riario <girolamo-riario>`, :person:`Melozzo da Forlì <melozzo-da-forli>`, and the Roman stonemason's wager and gift of a cloak, and :person:`Cicero <cicero>`'s story of :person:`Hiero <hiero>` and :person:`Simonides <simonides>`! Here the complete text of Folios ``0050.rst``–``0051.rst`` (``f. 17v``–``f. 18r``) is translated in full.
