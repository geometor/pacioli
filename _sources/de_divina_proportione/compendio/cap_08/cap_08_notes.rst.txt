CHAPTER VIII — Critical Commentary & Notes
==========================================

.. rubric:: How a Quantity Divided According to the Proportion Having a Mean and Two Extremes Is to Be Understood

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Pacioli's Canonical Numerical Exemplar ($a + b = 10$)**: Throughout Chapters VIII–XXII (and later in the polyhedral measurements), :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` adopts $10$ as the standard rational whole divided in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`. Setting the greater part $a = x$ (*una cosa*) and the lesser part $b = 10 - x$, the defining equation $10(10 - x) = x^2$ gives the quadratic equation $x^2 + 10x = 100$ (*censo e cose equale a numero*). By al-Khwarizmi's rule in *Algebra et Almucabala*:
   $$a = \sqrt{\left(\frac{10}{2}\right)^2 + 100} - \frac{10}{2} = \sqrt{125} - 5$$
   $$b = 10 - (\sqrt{125} - 5) = 15 - \sqrt{125}$$
2. **Arithmetic of Apotomes (*Residui*, Euclid X.73)**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` writes radicals in pure single-radical form so that $10 \cdot \sqrt{125} = \sqrt{100 \cdot 125} = \sqrt{12500}$. Thus:

   - Product of extremes: $10 \cdot (15 - \sqrt{125}) = 150 - \sqrt{12500}$.
   - Square of the mean: $(\sqrt{125} - 5)^2 = 125 + 25 - 2 \cdot 5\sqrt{125} = 150 - \sqrt{12500}$.

   Notice also that in line 24 of the 1509 print (and :person:`Winterberg's <winterberg>` transcription), `15 m. R. 125` was twice misprinted as `o. m. R. 125`; our translation restores the mathematically exact `15 - \sqrt{125}`.

3. **Campanus X.79 = Heiberg Euclid X.73**: In :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s Book X, an :term:`apotome <residuo>` ($\dot{\alpha}\pi\omicron\tau\omicron\mu\acute{\eta}$, rendered *residuum* by Gerard of Cremona and :person:`Campanus <campanus>`) is defined in :cite:`Euclid X.73 <euclid:elements:X.73>` (which is Proposition 79 in :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s numbering because :person:`Campanus <campanus>` counted several lemmas as numbered propositions), followed by the six species of apotomes in X.79–84 (:cite:`Campanus X.85–90 <campanus:euclid:X.85–90>`). Specifically, $\sqrt{125} - 5$ is a *fourth apotome* (*residuum quartum*), and $15 - \sqrt{125}$ is a *first apotome* (*residuum primum*).
