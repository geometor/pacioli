Fra Luca Pacioli, O.F.M.
========================

*Franciscan Mathematician, Humanist & Professor of Sacred Theology*

Born in Borgo Sansepolcro in Tuscany, Fra Luca Bartolomeo de Pacioli was the foremost mathematical encyclopedist of the Italian Renaissance. Educated in the abacus school tradition and mentored by his fellow townsman :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>`, Pacioli joined the Franciscan Order (Order of Friars Minor) around 1472. Over five decades he taught mathematics and theology across the major centers of Renaissance Italy—including Perugia, Venice, Rome (under the patronage of Leon Battista Alberti), Naples, Urbino, Milan, and Florence.

In November 1494 in Venice, Pacioli published his monumental *Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita*, a 616-folio vernacular encyclopedia synthesizing medieval Hindu–Arabic algorism, Cossic algebra, Euclidean proportion theory, practical mensuration, and the first published treatise on double-entry bookkeeping (*Particularis de computis et scripturis*). Called to Milan in 1496 to the court of Duke Ludovico Maria Sforza (*Il Moro*), Pacioli formed an extraordinary intellectual partnership with :person:`Leonardo da Vinci <leonardo>`, teaching Leonardo Euclidean geometry while collaborating on *De Divina Proportione* (completed in manuscript in 1498 and printed at Venice by Paganino de' Paganini in 1509). His work stands as the foundational bridge between ancient Greek geometry, medieval Arabic algebra, and early modern scientific method.
