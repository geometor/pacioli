HISTORICAL, PHILOLOGICAL & DEDUCTIVE NOTES
==========================================

.. rubric:: Commentary on Venetian Double-Entry Symmetry, Benedetto Cotrugli & the Birth of Probability

1. Why Double-Entry Appears Inside a Treatise on Proportion
-----------------------------------------------------------

Modern readers sometimes treat *Particularis de computis et scripturis* (`fols. 198v–210v`) as an isolated commercial manual accidentally bound inside a mathematical encyclopedia. Viewed within Pacioli's architectural plan, however, the Venetian :term:`Quaderno <quaderno>` is the **algebraic mirror of Euclidean balance**: just as Cossic *restauratio* (*al-jabr*, adding equal terms to both sides of an equation under :just:`I.cn.2`) preserves equality between unknown and known magnitudes, posting every commercial event simultaneously to left-hand *Dare* and right-hand *Avere* preserves the global conservation law :math:`\sum \text{Dare} = \sum \text{Avere}` across hundreds of heterogeneous accounts. Although the Ragusan diplomat Benedetto Cotrugli had drafted a brief manuscript chapter on double-entry in 1458 (*Libro de l'arte de la mercatura*, unprinted until 1573), Pacioli's 1494 *Summa* was the **first printed codification**, instantaneously translated and adapted across Antwerp (Jan Ympyn, 1543), Nuremberg (Wolfgang Schweicker, 1549), London (Hugh Oldcastle, 1543), and Amsterdam (Simon Stevin, 1608).

2. Internal Auditing Controls on `fols. 200v–202r`
--------------------------------------------------

Three operational details on scans `n0419`–`n0422` reveal Pacioli's intimate familiarity with Rialto counting-houses (where he tutored the sons of the merchant Antonio Rompiasi at San Bartolomeo in the 1470s):

- **Civic Authentication (`Cap. 7, fol. 200v`)**: Before a single line is written, the merchant presents his bound books to the *Consoli de' Mercanti* at Rialto to certify page count and currency standard, preventing fraudulent leaf substitution.
- **Diagonal Cancellation Lines (`Cap. 12, fol. 201v–202r`)**: Two oblique strokes are drawn across every *Giornale* item—the left stroke as soon as the *Dare* entry is posted to the *Quaderno*, the right stroke only when the matching *Avere* entry is posted.
- **Zero-Erasure Error Correction (`Cap. 31`, *Storno*)**: Scraping or blotting ink in the *Quaderno* is strictly forbidden; mistakes are reversed algebraically by posting a contra-entry on the opposite side!

3. The 160-Year Trajectory of `fol. 197r–197v` (1494 :math:`\to` 1654)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Pacioli's proportional-to-past-points division of the *balla* and *balestrare* stakes drew sharp critiques from :person:`Niccolò Tartaglia <tartaglia>` (*General Trattato*, 1556—who noticed that if a race to :math:`6` stops at :math:`1 : 0`, giving the entire :math:`10`-ducat pot to a player who won only a single round is manifestly unjust), :person:`Gerolamo Cardano <cardano>` (*Practica Arithmetice*, 1539, who rightly insisted that only the *deficit to the goal* :math:`n - s_i` matters), and Lorenzo Forestani (1603), before :person:`Blaise Pascal <pascal>` and :person:`Pierre de Fermat <fermat>` solved the general problem in 1654 by enumerating all :math:`m^{\sum (n - s_i) - (m - 1)}` equiprobable completion sequences. For Pacioli's three-crossbowmen problem `(4, 3, 2)` to `6` on `fol. 197v`, at most :math:`(6-4) + (6-3) + (6-2) - 2 = 7` imaginary shots (:math:`3^7 = 2187` equiprobable outcomes, reducing by 3 to denominator :math:`729`) decide the winner without ambiguity, awarding exact shares :math:`\frac{4510}{729} \approx 6.187`, :math:`\frac{1950}{729} \approx 2.675`, and :math:`\frac{830}{729} \approx 1.139` ducats.
