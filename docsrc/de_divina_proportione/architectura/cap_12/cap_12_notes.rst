CHAPTER XII — Critical Commentary & Notes
=========================================

.. rubric:: On the Orders and Spacing (Intercolumniations / Tetrants) of Round Columns in Buildings, and How the Architect Must Adapt Symmetry to the Site and Load (Vitruvius Book V).

Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Vitruvius III.3 (Intercolumniations) and V.6.7 (Tempering *Symmetria* by *Eurythmia* and *Usus*)**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` pairs :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`'s classification of intercolumniations (Pycnostyle :math:`1\frac{1}{2}D`, Systyle :math:`2D`, Eustyle :math:`2\frac{1}{4}D`, Diastyle :math:`3D`, Araeostyle :math:`>3D`) with the famous passage from Book V Chapter 6 on theaters, explaining why human-scale functional elements (steps, balustrades, passages) cannot simply be scaled down linearly in smaller buildings.

Constantin Winterberg's Commentary (1889 Edition)
-------------------------------------------------

- :person:`Winterberg <winterberg>` Footnotes 1–2 (Arch. Cap. XII, p. 318): (1) :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` writes :term:`*tygraphi* <tygraphi>` for :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`'s :term:`*triglyphi* <tygraphi>` (:term:`triglyphs <tygraphi>`). (2) In the 1509 print, read *tecto* ('roof') instead of *lecto* ('bed').
  (:person:`Winterberg <winterberg>` Fussnoten 1–2 (Arch. Cap. XII, S. 318): *1) Tygraphen = Triglyphen. 2) Text l. tecto st. lecto.*)
