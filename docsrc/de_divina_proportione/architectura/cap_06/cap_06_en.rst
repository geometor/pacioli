CHAPTER VI
==========

.. rubric:: On the Order and Construction of the Stylobate (Pedestal or Base-Pillar) of the Column, the Stereobate Foundation, the Ancient Roman Alphabet, and the Three Sets of Sixty Polyhedra Models Made by Pacioli alongside Leonardo da Vinci.

.. folio:: 0071–0072 (f. 28r–f. 28v)
   :initial: 0071.c2.png

The :term:`**stylobate** <stilobata>` (:term:`*stilobata* <stilobata>`, which we call the :term:`*pilastrello* <stilobata>` or :term:`*basamento* <stilobata>` of the column, marked :math:`cdef` in :ref:`Plate 0213 <plates-architectural-en>`) is the rectangular pedestal that supports the column and its base.

.. rubric:: Proportions and Mouldings of the Stylobate and Stereobate (:ref:`Plate 0213 <plates-architectural-en>`, :ref:`Plate 0185 <plates-architectural-en>`)

.. assertion::

   1. **Width and Height of the Stylobate Die (:math:`cdef`)**:

      - The width :math:`ef = cd` of the vertical die (shaft) of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` must be **exactly equal to the width of the plinth (:math:`hg`) of the column base** standing upon it (:math:`1\frac{1}{2}` column diameters), aligned plumb (*a livello*) so that the vertical lines :math:`ce` and :math:`df` carry the load directly without overhang or obliquity.
      - The **height** of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` must be **twice its width** (:math:`2 : 1` ratio, i.e., equal to two plinth lengths), so that it combines structural strength with visual grace, as seen in the :term:`**Porta Speciosa** <porta-speciosa>` (:ref:`Plate 0185 <plates-architectural-en>`).

   2. **Universal Law of Square Projections (*Proiecture / Sportafore*)**:

      - Both the **base** (:math:`abcd`) and the **capital or cornice** (*cimasa*, :math:`efmn`) of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` are adorned with stepped mouldings (plinth, tori, scotias, fillets).
      - In all these mouldings—and likewise in the :term:`architrave <epistilio>` and cornice above the column—observe this universal rule: **every projecting moulding (*sportafore / proiectura*) on the right and left must project outward by the exact amount of its own vertical height**, so that its profile forms a **square** (*respondino quadrate*) even if there were 10,000 mouldings!
      - And the projection of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>`'s base (:math:`abcd`) must be at least equal to (or slightly wider than, **never** narrower than) the projection of its upper cornice (:math:`efmn`).

   3. **The Subterranean Foundation (*:term:`Steriobata <steriobata>`*)**:

      - Beneath the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` base :math:`ab` must be laid a solid masonry foundation carried down underground to firm, level ground (*fin al vivo piano a livello de bon muraro*), equal in width at least to the full spread of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` base :math:`ab`; this foundation is called the :term:`**stereobate** <steriobata>` (:term:`*steriobata* <steriobata>`) by the Ancients.

   4. **Nomenclature of the Seven Mouldings of the Stylobate Cornice**:
      Counting downward from the top of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` capital (:math:`efmn`):

      - 1st (highest): **Acrotherio** (ancient) / top fillet;
      - 2nd: **Cimatio** (ancient) / **Intaulato** (modern);
      - 3rd: :term:`**Fastigio** <fastigio>` (fascia/corona);
      - 4th: :term:`**Echino** <echino>` (ancient) / **Uovolo** (modern egg-and-dart / ovolo);
      - 5th: **Baltheo** or :term:`**Trochilo** <scothia>` (ancient) / **Regolo** (modern);
      - 6th & 7th: **Tenia** (ancient) / **Intaulatura** (modern), resting immediately upon the die of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>`.

      (And if you carve foliage, trophies, or animals upon the flat faces of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>` die :math:`cdef`, keep the relief within the plane so as not to weaken the vertical corner lines :math:`ce` and :math:`df`.)

.. rubric:: Inscriptions in the Ancient Roman Alphabet, Leonardo da Vinci in Milan (1496–1499), and the Three Sets of Sixty Material Polyhedra

Upon the flat die of the :term:`stylobate <stilobata>`—as well as on friezes, pediments, and sepulchral monuments—it is customary to carve inscriptions and epitaphs in **ancient Roman capital letters** (*lettere belle antiche con tutta proportione*), which lend supreme beauty to the work. For this very reason I have placed in this volume (:ref:`Appendix I: The Roman Geometric Alphabet, Folios 0139–0183 <plates-alphabet-en>`) the exact geometric construction of the entire **Ancient Alphabet** with all its proportions, so that scribes and illuminators (*scriptori e miniatori*)—who are often so stingy in sharing their secrets—may see clearly that even without their pen and brush, **the two mathematical lines, curved and straight (*:term:`circino <circino>` e libella*), bring all letters and all other things to perfection**!

And this same truth shines forth in the perspective figures of all the regular and dependent bodies above in this book (:ref:`Appendix II: Plates I–LIX <plates-polyhedra-en>`), which were drawn by that most worthy painter, perspectivist, architect, and musician, endowed with every virtue, **Leonardo da Vinci of Florence** (:person:`*Lionardo da Vinci Fiorentino* <leonardo>`), in the city of **Milan** when we were both in the service and stipend of the Most Excellent Duke :person:`**Ludovico Maria Sforza Anglo** <ludovico-sforza>` in the years of our salvation **1496 to 1499**—whence afterward, on account of diverse events in those parts [the French invasion of October 1499], **we departed together and took up our dwelling together in Florence** (*donde poi da siemi per diversi successi in quelle parti ci partemmo e a Firenze pur insiemi trahemmo domicilio*)!

And in Milan, with **my own hands** (*de mie proprie mani*), I constructed, colored, and adorned **sixty material models** (*forme de dicti corpi materiali bellissime... numero 60*) of those regular and dependent bodies for the said Duke; and I made **another set of sixty** in Milan for my patron **Messer Galeazzo Sanseverino**; and afterward in **Florence** I made **a third set of sixty** for our Lord the Perpetual Gonfaloniere :person:`**Piero Soderini** <piero-soderini>` (:person:`*Petro Soderino* <piero-soderini>`), **which are presently kept in his palace [the Palazzo della Signoria / Palazzo Vecchio] in Florence**!
