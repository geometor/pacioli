CHAPTER VII — Critical Commentary & Notes
=========================================

.. rubric:: On the First Effect of a Line Divided According to Our Proportion

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Proposition Citations in Chapter VII**:

   - **First Effect (Euclid XIII.1)**: If a line :math:`AB = a+b` is cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` at :math:`C` with greater segment :math:`AC = a`, and :math:`AD = \frac{1}{2}AB` is added to :math:`AC`, then :math:`CD^2 = 5 \cdot AD^2`, i.e., :math:`(a + \frac{a+b}{2})^2 = 5(\frac{a+b}{2})^2`.
   - **Campanus VI.19 = Heiberg Euclid VI.30**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` cites *la 19. del 6.* ("the 19th of the 6th"), which is the numbering of *Elements* VI.30 ("To cut a given finite straight line in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`") in the medieval Latin edition of :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>`, preceded by :cite:`Euclid VI.def.3 <euclid:elements:VI.def.3>`.
   - :cite:`**Euclid II.11** <euclid:elements:II.11>`: :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s geometric construction in Book II ("To cut a given straight line so that the rectangle contained by the whole and one of the segments is equal to the square on the remaining segment"), which constructs the :term:`golden section <goldener-schnitt>` by areas prior to the theory of proportions in Book V.
   - **Campanus Euclid IX.16**: In :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s commentary on *Elements* IX.16 ("If two numbers be prime to one another, the second will not be to any other number as the first is to the second"), :person:`Campanus <campanus>` proves as a corollary that no three integers can stand in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` (since if :math:`a+b : a = a : b` in lowest terms, :math:`a` and :math:`b` would be coprime, yet :math:`a+b` and :math:`a` would also be coprime, contradicting IX.16).

2. **Christological and Boethian Analogies**: :person:`Pacioli <pacioli>` extends the theological analogies of Chapter V with a Christological comparison (Christ fulfilling the Mosaic Law, Matthew 5:17, and submitting to Mary and Joseph, Luke 2:51) and quotes the famous Neoplatonic hymn *O qui perpetua mundum ratione gubernas* from :person:`Boethius <boethius>`'s *Consolation of Philosophy* (Book III, metrum 9, line 3): *stabilisque manens dat cuncta moveri* ("remaining unmoved, He gives all things to move").

.. rubric:: Note 1 (Cap. VII): Equivalence of Pacioli's Initial Characterization with Euclid's Definition of Extreme and Mean Ratio (:ref:`Sachliche Anmerkungen #1 <plates-anmerkungen-en>`, Cap. VII, p. 198, l. 4 from bottom)

Since the definition of continuous division (:term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>`) according to :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (:cite:`Euclid VI.def.3 <euclid:elements:VI.def.3>`, :cite:`Euclid II.11 <euclid:elements:II.11>`) is stated at the close of Chapter VII—namely, when a given line :math:`ac` is divided at :math:`b` into two segments such that the square on the greater segment :math:`ab^2` equals the rectangle :math:`ac \cdot bc` contained by the whole line and the lesser segment—we demonstrate its identity with the numerical property stated at the beginning of Chapter VII (:cite:`Euclid XIII.1 <euclid:elements:XIII.1>`):

- **Hypothesis**: Let the line :math:`ac` be divided in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` at :math:`b`, so that :math:`ab^2 = ac \cdot bc`.
- **Claim**: :math:`\left(ac + \frac{bc}{2}\right)^2 = 5\left(\frac{bc}{2}\right)^2`, or equivalently :math:`\left(ab + \frac{ac}{2}\right)^2 = 5\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2`.
- **Proof**: By hypothesis, :math:`ab^2 = ac(ac - ab)`, whence

  .. math::

     ac^2 = ab^2 + ab \cdot ac \quad \text{and} \quad ab^2 + ab \cdot ac = ac^2.

  Adding :math:`\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2` to both sides yields

  .. math::

     \left(ab + \frac{ac}{2}\right)^2 = ab^2 + ab \cdot ac + \left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2 = ac^2 + \frac{ac^2}{4} = 5\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2. \quad \text{Q.E.D.}

.. rubric:: Anmerkung 1 (Cap. VII, S. 198): Identität der zu Anfang gegebenen Definition der stetigen Theilung mit der Euklidischen Definition

Da die Definition der stetigen Theilung nach :person:`Euklid <euclid>` gewöhnlich so angegeben wird, wie am Schluss des Capitels gesagt, d. h. wenn die gegebene Linie :math:`ac` im Punkte :math:`b` in zwei Abschnitte zerfällt, wovon das Quadrat des grössern :math:`ab^2` gleich dem Product :math:`ac \cdot bc` aus der ganzen Länge mal dem kleinern Abschnitte ist, so sei zum Nachweise der Identität der letztern mit der zu Anfang dieses Capitels gegebenen Eigenschaft Folgendes bemerkt:

- **Voraussetzung**: Es sei nach der bekannten :person:`Euklidischen <euclid>` Definition die Linie :math:`ac` im Punkte :math:`b` stetig getheilt, so dass :math:`ab^2 = ac \cdot bc`.
- **Behauptung**: :math:`\left(ab + \frac{ac}{2}\right)^2 = 5\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2`.
- **Beweis**: Nach Voraussetzung ist :math:`ab^2 = ac(ac - ab)` oder :math:`ac^2 = ab^2 + ab \cdot ac`. Fügt man rechts und links :math:`\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2` hinzu, so folgt unmittelbar:

  .. math::

     \left(ab + \frac{ac}{2}\right)^2 = 5\left(\frac{ac}{2}\right)^2. \quad \text{w. z. b. w.}

.. rubric:: Note 2 (Cap. VII): Bisection of the Whole Line and Continuous Division of the Half (:ref:`Sachliche Anmerkungen #2 <plates-anmerkungen-en>`, Cap. VII, p. 198, l. 14 from bottom)

This corollary (:cite:`Euclid XIII.1 <euclid:elements:XIII.1>` / :cite:`Euclid XIII.2 <euclid:elements:XIII.2>`) follows immediately if we bisect the line :math:`ab` at :math:`c` (:math:`ac = bc = \frac{1}{2}ab`) and divide :math:`bc` in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` at :math:`d`; considering the three parts of the line :math:`ab`, the greater segment of the half-line together with the other half satisfies the fivefold square relation :math:`ad^2 = 5\,ac^2`.

.. rubric:: Anmerkung 2 (Cap. VII, S. 198): Halbirung der Linie und stetige Theilung der Hälfte

Diese Folgerung ergibt sich, indem man sich die Linie :math:`ab` in :math:`c` halbirt, und sodann :math:`bc` im Punkte :math:`d` auf die gewöhnliche Art stetig getheilt denkt. Betrachtet man dann die drei Theile, aus denen die Linie :math:`ab` besteht, so ist der zusammengesetzte Abschnitt :math:`ad = ac + cd`.
