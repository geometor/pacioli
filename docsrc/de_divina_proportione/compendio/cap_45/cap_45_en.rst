CHAPTER XLV
===========

.. rubric:: How the Octocedron Is Placed in the Duodecedron.

.. folio:: 0043 (f. 14r)

Returning now to the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>`, the eleventh proposition of the fifteenth book (:cite:`Campanus XV.11 <campanus:euclid:XV.11>`) shows how to inscribe the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>` (8 triangular bases) inside the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>` (12 pentagonal bases)—a construction of singular elegance because the six vertices of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` do not touch the faces or vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, but rather the **midpoints of six of its thirty edges**!

.. enunciation:: Inscribing the Octahedron in the Dodecahedron (Campanus XV.11)

   In a given regular body of twelve pentagonal bases (:term:`*duodecedron* <dodecahedron>`), to inscribe the regular body of eight equilateral triangular bases (:term:`*octocedron* <octahedron>`) so that the six :term:`solid angles <angulo-solido>` of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` stand upon the midpoints of six opposite edges of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`.

.. assertion:: Construction and Proof (Campanus XV.11, via :just:`XIII.17` and :just:`XIII.14`)

   1. Recall from :ref:`Chapter XXX <compendio-cap_30-en>` (:just:`XIII.17`) how the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` was built by erecting six hipped roofs over the **six square faces of an inscribed cube**: over each of the six square faces of that underlying cube there stands one **ridge edge** (:term:`*corausto* <corausto>` or roof-crest, such as :math:`vx` in ``0035.g1.png``) of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, parallel to the median of that square face of the cube and lying directly above its center!
   2. Thus among the thirty edges of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, there is a set of **six privileged edges**—three pairs of opposite, parallel roof-ridge edges—whose **six midpoints** lie directly on the three mutually perpendicular axes passing through the center of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (and of the underlying cube) and perpendicular to the six faces of that cube (see the marginal diagram ``0041.g2.png`` on Folio 0041).
   3. Mark the midpoints :math:`f, g, h, k, l, m` of these six opposite edges of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`. The three straight lines joining the three opposite pairs of midpoints pass straight through the center of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, are all equal in length (each being equal to the diameter of the sphere that touches those six edge-midpoints), and intersect one another at **right angles** at the center!
   4. Join the six midpoints :math:`f, g, h, k, l, m` to one another by twelve straight lines. By the exact same proof as in :ref:`Chapter XXVIII <compendio-cap_28-en>` (:just:`XIII.14`), because the three axes are mutually equal and bisect one another at right angles at the center, all twelve connecting edges are mutually equal (each having its square equal to half the square on the axis joining two opposite midpoints), and all eight faces are equilateral triangles!

Therefore the regular :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` is inscribed in the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, with its six vertices standing at the midpoints of six opposite edges of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`.

.. qed::
