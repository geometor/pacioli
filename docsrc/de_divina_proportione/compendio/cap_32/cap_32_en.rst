CHAPTER XXXII
=============

.. rubric:: On the Proportion of the Said Regular Bodies to One Another, Especially that of the Ycocedron to the Duodecedron.

.. folio:: 0040 (f. 12v)
   :initial: 0040.c1.png

Having compared the sides of all five regular bodies inscribed in the same sphere according to the thirteenth book, we now proceed to the **fourteenth book** of our philosopher—composed by **Hypsicles of Alexandria** (*Ypsicle Alexandrino*) following the discoveries of **Aristaeus** (*Aristeo*) and **Apollonius** (*Apollonio*) and commented upon by :person:`**Campanus** <campanus>`—which investigates the admirable proportions of the surfaces and solid volumes of these bodies, above all the :term:`**ycocedron** <icosahedron>` and the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>` inscribed in the same sphere (:just:`XIV.2`, :just:`XIV.6`, :just:`XIV.7`, :just:`XIV.8`).

.. enunciation:: Fundamental Ratios of the Icosahedron and Dodecahedron in the Same Sphere (:just:`XIV.2`, :just:`XIV.6`–:just:`XIV.8`)

   When the regular :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` (20 triangular faces) and the regular :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (12 pentagonal faces) are inscribed in one and the same sphere:

   1. **Same Circumcircle of Faces** (:just:`XIV.2` [:cite:`Campanus XIV.6 <campanus:euclid:XIV.6>`]): One and the same circle circumscribes both the pentagonal base of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` and the triangular base of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`.
   2. **Equal Central Altitude**: Consequently, the perpendiculars drawn from the center of the sphere to the center of a pentagonal face of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` and to the center of a triangular face of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` are **equal** to one another; hence a single sphere inscribed within them touches the faces of both bodies equally!
   3. **Ratio of Total Surfaces and Ratio of Solid Volumes** (:just:`XIV.7`, :just:`XIV.8`): The total surface of the twelve pentagons of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` has to the total surface of the twenty triangles of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`—and likewise the solid volume (*corporatura*) of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` has to the solid volume of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`—the exact same proportion as the **side of the cube** has to the **side of the ycocedron**? Nay, the total surface (and solid volume) of the :term:`**ycocedron** <icosahedron>` has to the total surface (and solid volume) of the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>` the ratio of the **side of the ycocedron** to the **side of the cube**? Let us state :person:`Campanus <campanus>`'s exact theorem (:just:`XIV.6`–:just:`XIV.8`):

      - Because the perpendiculars from the center of the sphere to the faces of both solids are equal (:math:`r_{\text{in}}`), each solid is resolved into pyramids having the sphere's center as common apex and height :math:`r_{\text{in}}` (12 pentagonal pyramids for the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, 20 triangular pyramids for the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`). Therefore, **the solid volume of the duodecedron is to the solid volume of the ycocedron exactly as the total surface area of the duodecedron is to the total surface area of the ycocedron**!
      - And by the :just:`seventh and eighth of the fourteenth <XIV.7>`, the surface (and volume) of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` has to the surface (and volume) of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` the ratio of the **side of the cube** (:math:`s_{\text{cube}}`) to the **side of the ycocedron** (:math:`s_{20}`):

      .. math::

         \frac{\text{Vol}(\text{:term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`})}{\text{Vol}(\text{:term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>`})} = \frac{\text{Area}(\text{:term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`})}{\text{Area}(\text{:term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>`})} = \frac{s_{\text{cube}}}{s_{\text{icosa}}} = \sqrt{\frac{5 + \sqrt{5}}{6}}?

      Wait: in a sphere of radius :math:`R`, the circumradius :math:`\rho` of both the triangle of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` and the pentagon of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` is :math:`\rho = R\sqrt{\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}}`? No, :math:`r_{\text{in}}^{2} + \rho^{2} = R^{2}`: since both faces have the **same circumradius** :math:`\rho`, the :term:`**icosahedron** <icosahedron>` has a larger edge (:math:`s_{20} = \rho\sqrt{3}`) than the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` (:math:`s_{12} = \rho\sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}}`), and the :term:`**icosahedron** <icosahedron>` has a **larger surface area and larger volume** than the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`! Specifically:

      .. math::

         \frac{\text{Vol}(\text{:term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>`})}{\text{Vol}(\text{:term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`})} = \frac{\text{Area}(\text{:term:`Icosahedron <icosahedron>`})}{\text{Area}(\text{:term:`Dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`})} = \frac{s_{\text{cube}}}{s_{\text{icosa}}}

      because :math:`s_{\text{cube}} > s_{\text{icosa}}` (as proved in :ref:`Chapter XXXI <compendio-cap_31-en>`), so the ratio :math:`s_{\text{cube}} : s_{\text{icosa}}` is a ratio of **greater inequality** (:math:`\sqrt{\frac{10}{3(5-\sqrt{5})}} = \sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{6}} > 1`)!

.. assertion:: Proof Summary (:just:`XIV.2`, :just:`XIV.6`, :just:`XIV.7`, :just:`XIV.8`)

   1. By Aristaeus's theorem proved in :just:`XIV.2`, one and the same circle circumscribes both the pentagon of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` and the triangle of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` inscribed in the same sphere.
   2. Since equal circles in a sphere stand at equal perpendicular distances from the center of the sphere (:just:`III.14`), the perpendicular from the sphere's center to a pentagonal base of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` equals the perpendicular to a triangular base of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`.
   3. Dividing the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` into 12 pyramids of height :math:`h` and the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` into 20 pyramids of the same height :math:`h`, their solid volumes are in the exact same proportion as their total surface areas (the sum of the 12 pentagons to the sum of the 20 triangles).
   4. And by :just:`XIV.7`–:just:`XIV.8`, the ratio of the 20 triangles of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` to the 12 pentagons of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` equals the ratio of the **side of the cube** to the **side of the ycocedron** inscribed in the same sphere; and also equals the ratio of the line whose square is :math:`\sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{6}}`.

.. qed::
