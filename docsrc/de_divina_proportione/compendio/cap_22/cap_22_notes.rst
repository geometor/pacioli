CHAPTER XXII — Critical Commentary & Notes
==========================================

.. rubric:: On Its Thirteenth Most Worthy Effect

Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **The Golden Triangle (:cite:`Euclid IV.10 <euclid:elements:IV.10>`) and the Pentagon (:cite:`Euclid IV.11 <euclid:elements:IV.11>`–:cite:`Euclid IV.12 <euclid:elements:IV.12>`)**: In :cite:`Euclid IV.10 <euclid:elements:IV.10>`, :person:`Euclid <euclid>` constructs the $72^\circ\text{–}72^\circ\text{–}36^\circ$ isosceles triangle by dividing the leg $AB$ at $C$ via :cite:`Euclid II.11 <euclid:elements:II.11>` so that $AB \cdot BC = AC^2$, and placing $BD = AC$ as the base. In :cite:`Euclid IV.11 <euclid:elements:IV.11>` he inscribes this golden triangle into a circle and bisects the two $72^\circ$ base angles to obtain the five vertices of the regular pentagon.
2. **Pacioli's Insight into Euclid's Axiomatic Method**: Chapter XXII contains a remarkably lucid analysis of the architecture of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements*—one that directly anticipates modern historiography of Greek mathematics (and connects directly to the GEOMETOR G-Index dependency graph!). Pacioli explains why :person:`Euclid <euclid>` proves the golden section twice—first by geometric areas in :cite:`Euclid II.11 <euclid:elements:II.11>` ($AB \cdot BC = AC^2$) and again by proportions in :cite:`Euclid VI.30 <euclid:elements:VI.30>` ($AB : AC = AC : BC$): because :cite:`Euclid IV.10 <euclid:elements:IV.10>`–:cite:`Euclid IV.11 <euclid:elements:IV.11>` (the regular pentagon) stands in Book IV *prior* to Eudoxus's general theory of proportions in Book V, :person:`Euclid <euclid>` could not legally invoke ratios in Book IV without violating his strict deductive rule of using only *antecedenti* (previously proved propositions) and never *sequenti*!
