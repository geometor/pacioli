CHAPTER XXXIII
==============

.. rubric:: On the Proportion of the Surfaces and Solid Volumes of All the Regular Bodies to One Another.

.. folio:: 0040–0041 (f. 12v–f. 13r)
   :initial: 0040.c2.png

Continuing with the fourteenth book of our author (with the additions and commentaries of :person:`Campanus <campanus>`), we summarize how the surfaces and volumes of the other regular bodies—the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`, the cube, and the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`—are compared when inscribed in the same sphere (:just:`XIV.9`, :just:`XIV.10`).

.. rubric:: Proportions of the Tetrahedron, Cube, and Octahedron in the Same Sphere

.. assertion::

   1. **Edges in the Same Sphere** (recapitulating :just:`XIII.18`):

      - If the diameter of the sphere is :math:`D`, then:

        .. math::

           s_{\text{tetra}}^{2} = \frac{2}{3}D^{2}, \qquad s_{\text{octa}}^{2} = \frac{1}{2}D^{2}, \qquad s_{\text{cube}}^{2} = \frac{1}{3}D^{2}

   2. **Inscribed Spheres (Perpendiculars from the Center to the Faces)**:

      - In the **cube**, the distance from the center of the sphere to each of its six square faces is half the edge of the cube (:math:`r_{6} = \frac{1}{2}s_{\text{cube}}`), so :math:`r_{6}^{2} = \frac{1}{12}D^{2}`.
      - In the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>`, each of the eight faces is an equilateral triangle of side :math:`s_{\text{octa}}` whose circumcircle has radius :math:`\rho_{8}` satisfying :math:`s_{\text{octa}}^{2} = 3\,\rho_{8}^{2}` (:just:`XIII.12`), so :math:`\rho_{8}^{2} = \frac{1}{6}D^{2}`. Therefore, the perpendicular from the center of the sphere to a face of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` satisfies :math:`r_{8}^{2} = \left(\frac{1}{2}D\right)^{2} - \rho_{8}^{2} = \frac{1}{4}D^{2} - \frac{1}{6}D^{2} = \frac{1}{12}D^{2}`!
      - **Marvelous Equality**: Therefore, **the perpendicular from the center of the sphere to a face of the cube is exactly equal to the perpendicular from the center of the sphere to a face of the octocedron** (:math:`r_{6} = r_{8} = \frac{D}{\sqrt{12}}`)! Just as one and the same inner sphere touches the faces of both the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` and the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` (:ref:`Chapter XXXII <compendio-cap_32-en>`), so another single inner sphere touches the faces of both the **cube** and the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>`!
      - In the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>`, the circumcircle of each triangular face of side :math:`s_{\text{tetra}}` has :math:`\rho_{4}^{2} = \frac{1}{3}s_{\text{tetra}}^{2} = \frac{2}{9}D^{2}`, so the perpendicular from the center of the sphere to a face of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` satisfies :math:`r_{4}^{2} = \frac{1}{4}D^{2} - \frac{2}{9}D^{2} = \frac{1}{36}D^{2}`, whence :math:`r_{4} = \frac{1}{6}D` (one-sixth of the sphere's diameter, or one-third of its radius, and one-fourth of the altitude of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`!).

   3. **Volume and Surface Proportions of Cube and Octahedron**:

      - Because the cube and the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` have the **exact same insphere radius** :math:`r_{6} = r_{8}`, the ratio of the solid volume of the cube to the solid volume of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` is identical to the ratio of the total surface area of the six squares of the cube to the total surface area of the eight triangles of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>`:

        .. math::

           \frac{\text{Vol}(\text{Cube})}{\text{Vol}(\text{:term:`Octahedron <octahedron>`})} = \frac{\text{Area}(\text{Cube})}{\text{Area}(\text{:term:`Octahedron <octahedron>`})} = \frac{6 \cdot \frac{1}{3}D^{2}}{8 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2}D^{2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}

   4. **Volume Proportion of Tetrahedron and Octahedron**:

      - Comparing their bases and altitudes, the solid volume of the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` to that of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` inscribed in the same sphere is :math:`\frac{\frac{1}{6}D^{3}}{\frac{2}{27}\sqrt{3}\,D^{3}} = \frac{9}{4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}`.

As for the detailed numerical rules for finding the exact surface areas and volumes of all five bodies when a given measure in *braccia* or *piedi* is assigned to their edges or to the sphere's diameter, we have treated them at full length in our great work (*Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni e Proportionalita*, Venice, 1494) and in the particular treatise dedicated to your illustrious kinsman :person:`Guidobaldo <guidobaldo>`, Duke of Urbino (*Tractato de' Corpi Regulari*).
