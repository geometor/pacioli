Authors, Illustrators & Editors
-------------------------------

.. _person-pacioli:

Fra Luca Pacioli, O.F.M. (c. 1447 – 1517)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Franciscan Mathematician, Humanist & Professor of Sacred Theology*

Born in Borgo Sansepolcro in Tuscany, Fra Luca Bartolomeo de Pacioli was the foremost mathematical encyclopedist of the Italian Renaissance. Educated in the abacus school tradition and mentored by his fellow townsman :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>`, Pacioli joined the Franciscan Order (Order of Friars Minor) around 1472. Over five decades he taught mathematics and theology across the major centers of Renaissance Italy—including Perugia, Venice, Rome (under the patronage of Leon Battista Alberti), Naples, Urbino, Milan, and Florence.

In November 1494 in Venice, Pacioli published his monumental *Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita*, a 616-folio vernacular encyclopedia synthesizing medieval Hindu–Arabic algorism, Cossic algebra, Euclidean proportion theory, practical mensuration, and the first published treatise on double-entry bookkeeping (*Particularis de computis et scripturis*). Called to Milan in 1496 to the court of Duke Ludovico Maria Sforza (*Il Moro*), Pacioli formed an extraordinary intellectual partnership with :person:`Leonardo da Vinci <leonardo>`, teaching Leonardo Euclidean geometry while collaborating on *De Divina Proportione* (completed in manuscript in 1498 and printed at Venice by Paganino de' Paganini in 1509). His work stands as the foundational bridge between ancient Greek geometry, medieval Arabic algebra, and early modern scientific method.

.. _person-leonardo:

Leonardo da Vinci (1452 – 1519)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Florentine Painter, Sculptor, Polymath, Architect & Engineer*

One of the greatest creative minds in human history. During his tenure as court artist and engineer to Ludovico Sforza in Milan (1482–1499), Leonardo forged a deep friendship and mathematical partnership with Fra Luca Pacioli, who arrived in Milan in 1496. Leonardo enthusiastically studied Euclid's *Elements* and Pacioli's *Summa*, noting in his journals to purchase a copy of the *Summa* and consult Master Luca on geometric propositions.

For Pacioli's *De Divina Proportione*, Leonardo drew the sixty exquisite perspective polyhedra plates (*schemata Vincii nostri Leonardi manibus scalpta*), depicting both solid (*vacui*) and hollowed-out skeletal (*vacui / pertusati*) regular and semi-regular Archimedean bodies suspended in dramatic perspective. These illustrations—cut in wood for the 1509 Venice edition—represent the earliest published depictions of Archimedean and elevated polyhedra in print and constitute Leonardo's only published book illustrations during his lifetime.

* **Primary Contribution**: Sixty Polyhedra Perspective Plates (Tabulae I–LIX) in :doc:`De Divina Proportione <de_divina_proportione/index>`
* **Apparatus Details**: :doc:`De Divina Proportione Person Index <de_divina_proportione/persons>`

.. _person-piero-della-francesca:

Piero della Francesca (c. 1415 – 1492)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Early Renaissance Master Painter, Geometer & Perspective Theorist of Borgo Sansepolcro*

Celebrated as the "monarch of painting and architecture in our times" (*monarca a li tempi nostri de la pictura e architectura*) by Pacioli, Piero was Pacioli's elder compatriot and foundational mathematical master in Borgo Sansepolcro. Beyond his celebrated frescoes—including *The Legend of the True Cross* in Arezzo and the *Resurrection* in Sansepolcro—Piero was an accomplished geometer who authored three rigorous mathematical treatises: *Trattato d'Abaco*, *De Prospectiva Pingendi* (the first comprehensive manual on linear perspective), and *Libellus de Quinque Corporibus Regularibus*.

Pacioli translated and incorporated large sections of Piero's Latin *Libellus* on regular polyhedra into Part III of *De Divina Proportione*, preserving Piero's stereometric theorems and techniques for calculating the edge-lengths and volumes of inscribed polyhedra.

* **Primary Contribution**: Mathematical theory of polyhedra and perspective underlying :doc:`De Divina Proportione <de_divina_proportione/index>` and :doc:`Summa <summa_1494/index>`
* **Apparatus Details**: :doc:`De Divina Proportione Person Index <de_divina_proportione/persons>` · :doc:`Summa Person Index <summa_1494/persons>`

.. _person-winterberg:

Constantin Winterberg (fl. 1887 – 1889)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*German Classical Art Historian, Mathematician & Critical Translator*

Scholar of Renaissance perspective and geometry who produced the landmark 1889 Vienna critical edition and German translation of Pacioli's work: *Fra Luca Pacioli: Divina Proportione — Die Lehre vom Goldenen Schnitt* (published as Volume II of Rudolf Eitelberger von Edelberg's prestigious *Quellenschriften für Kunstgeschichte und Kunsttechnik des Mittelalters und der Neuzeit*, Vienna: Carl Graeser, 1889).

Winterberg provided an extensive scholarly introduction (*Einleitung*), detailed chapter-by-chapter philological annotations, collation with the 1498 Milan manuscript (Codex Ambrosianus), and seventeen rigorous substantive mathematical commentaries (*Sachliche Anmerkungen*) analyzing Leonardo's polyhedra constructions and Pacioli's proportion theorems. His 1889 text and notes provide the primary German critical track throughout the GEOMETOR edition.

* **Primary Contribution**: Critical German translation, introduction, and mathematical *Sachliche Anmerkungen* in :doc:`De Divina Proportione <de_divina_proportione/index>`
* **Apparatus Details**: :doc:`De Divina Proportione Person Index <de_divina_proportione/persons>`
