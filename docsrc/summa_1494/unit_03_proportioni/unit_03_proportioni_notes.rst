HISTORICAL, PHILOLOGICAL & DEDUCTIVE NOTES
==========================================

.. rubric:: Commentary on *Proportione* vs. *Proportionalità*, Piero della Francesca & the 1509 *Compendio*

1. Renaissance Terminological Precision: *Proportione* vs. *Proportionalità*
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Throughout *Distinctio VI* (and in the title *Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita* itself), Pacioli adheres strictly to :person:`Campanus of Novara's <campanus>` Latin rendering of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` Book V:

- **Proportione** translates Greek *logos* / Latin *habitudo*—a single **ratio** $a : b$ between two homogeneous magnitudes.
- **Proportionalità** translates Greek *analogia* / Latin *proportio*—a **similitude or equality of ratios** $a : b :: c : d$.

Only in the seventeenth century did *proportio* shift in French and English usage to denote the four-term equality while *ratio* replaced *proportio* for the two-term relation. Keeping Pacioli's distinction clear illuminates why `fol. 74r` calls Euclid's six rules *modi arguendi proportionalitatum* (rules of inference transforming one valid proportion into five equivalent proportions).

2. Tribute to Piero della Francesca on `fol. 68v`
-------------------------------------------------

Scan `n0155` (`Part I, fol. 68v`) refutes the legend that Pacioli suppressed his master's name: in one of the warmest tributes of the Italian Quattrocento, Pacioli hails :person:`Maestro Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>` as *"el monarca a li tempi nostri de la pictura"* and explicitly commends his treatise :cite:`*De prospectiva pingendi* <piero-della-francesca:de-prospectiva-pingendi>`, which Pacioli notes Duke Guidobaldo already possessed in the ducal library at Urbino.

3. Bridge from *Summa* Dist. VI (1494) to *De Divina Proportione* (1509)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every mathematical ingredient of Pacioli's 1509 Milanese–Venetian treatise :ref:`*De Divina Proportione* <de-divina-proportione-index>` is forged in *Distinctio VI* of the 1494 *Summa*:

- The genealogical tree of rational and irrational ratios printed on `fol. 82r`–`83r` directly prefigures the full-page *Arbor Proportionis et Proportionalitatis* woodcut (`Folio 0311`) closing the 1509 edition.
- The canonical numerical archetype dividing $10$ into the :term:`apotomes <residuo>` $\sqrt{125}-5$ and $15-\sqrt{125}$ recurs verbatim in :ref:`*Compendio* Chapters VIII–XXII <compendio-cap_08-en>`, elevated in Milan (1496–1498) to a theological and cosmological contemplation illustrated by Leonardo da Vinci.
