DEDICATIONS, CONTINENTIA & SPECULATIVE ARITHMETIC
=================================================

.. rubric:: Preliminaries (fols. 1r–8v) & Part I, Distinctio I: Continuous and Discrete Quantity, Figurate & Perfect Numbers (fols. 1r–18v)

.. folio:: [prelim. fol. 1r–1v]

.. rubric:: General Contents (*Continentia de tutta lopera*) & Venetian Preliminaries

**Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita** (printed in Venice by :person:`Paganino de' Paganini <paganino-de-paganini>`, November 10–20, 1494).

*General Contents of the Entire Work:*

1. **Numbers and Measures** (*De numeri e misure*): All modes occurring according to every theoretical and practical faculty—speculative properties of integers, algorism, fractions, and :term:`proportions <proportione>` (*Distinctiones I–VI*).
2. **The Great Art** (*De l'Arte Maggiore*, called by the common people the :term:`Rule of the Thing <cosa>`, or *Algebra e Almucabala*): Operations on :term:`radicals <radice-universale>`, :term:`binomials <binomio>`, :term:`apotomes <residuo>`, and the :term:`six canonical equations <capitoli-composti>` (*Distinctio VIII*).
3. **Mercantile Arithmetic** (*De compagnie, soccite, baratti, cambi, meriti, sconti, leghe de monete e pesi*): Partnerships, barter, bills of exchange, interest, discounts, coinage alloys, weights, and games of chance (*Distinctio IX, Tractatus I–X*).
4. **All Modes of Keeping Mercantile Accounts and Writings** (*De computis et scripturis*): The :term:`Inventory <inventario>`, :term:`Memorandum <memoriale>`, :term:`Journal <giornale>`, and :term:`Great Ledger <quaderno>` according to the custom of Venice and other places (*Distinctio IX, Tractatus XI*).
5. **Geometry in All Practical and Theoretical Cases** (*Tractatus Geometriae*): Triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, divisions of surfaces, wine casks, and all five regular bodies and their dependents inscribed in the sphere (*Part II, Distinctiones I–VIII*).

*To the Magnificent Venetian Patrician* :person:`Marco Sanudo <marco-sanudo>`, *most exquisite cultivator of Astrology, Geometry, and Arithmetic, Brother Luca of Borgo Sansepolcro of the Order of Minorites and humble professor of Sacred Theology sends warmest greeting.* Together with the Latin elegiac epigram of :person:`Paolo Pompilio <pompilio>` and the Italian sonnet of :person:`Giorgio Sommaripa <giorgio-sommaripa>`.

.. rubric:: Dedicatory Epistle to Guidobaldo da Montefeltro, Duke of Urbino

*To the Most Illustrious Prince* :person:`Guidobaldo <guidobaldo-da-montefeltro>`, *Duke of Urbino: Epistle of Brother Luca of Borgo Sansepolcro of the Order of Minorites and humble professor of Sacred Theology.*

Magnanimous Duke: as is clearly made manifest to everyone by the ancient and modern philosophers, all the sciences and the mechanical and liberal arts—without number, weight, and measure (as Wisdom declares: *omnia in mensura, numero et pondere disposuisti*)—would be vain and confused; for military art, perspective, music, cosmography, and architecture all stand or fall with exact measurement. Therefore, for the common utility of all the subjects of Your Ducal Lordship and of every other Italian scholar, I have resolved to reduce into a single volume in our mother vernacular tongue everything required for the practice and theory of Arithmetic and Geometry, calling to mind the wondrous and stupendous edifice of the Ducal Palace of Urbino ordered by the blessed memory of the most unconquered Prince :person:`Federico <federico-da-montefeltro>`, Your father.

.. rubric:: Distinctio Prima, Tractatus Primus — Division of Quantity & Authorities

.. enunciation::

   **Quantity** (*quantità*), Magnanimous Duke, is immediately twofold—namely **continuous** (*continua*) and **discrete** (*discreta*). Continuous quantity is that whose parts are joined at a certain common boundary, such as wood, iron, stone, line, surface, and body (divisible without end). Discrete or divided quantity is that whose parts are not joined at a common boundary, such as $1, 2, 3, \dots$ proceeding from the indivisible unit—establishing the fourfold mathematical *Quadrivium* of Arithmetic, Music, Geometry, and Astronomy (:cite:`Boethius, *De Institutione Arithmetica* I.1 <boethius:de-institutione-arithmetica>`).

From :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, :person:`Severinus Boethius <boethius>`, :person:`Leonardo Pisano <leonardo-pisano>`, :person:`Jordanus Nemorarius <jordanus-nemorarius>`, :person:`Biagio Pelacani of Parma <biagio-pelacani>`, :person:`Johannes de Sacrobosco <sacrobosco>`, and :person:`Prosdocimo de' Beldomandi <prosdocimo-de-beldomandi>` we have drawn the greater part of this work: **and wherever in the said work no author is set down, understand that passage to be from Leonardo Pisano.**

.. rubric:: Figurate Polygonal Numbers & Euclid's Theorem on Perfect Numbers (:just:`IX.36`)

.. picture:: fig_01_figurate_perfect.svg
   :alt: Triangular tetractys T4 = 10 and Euclid IX.36 dyadic perfect-number rectangle 4 x 7 = 28

Following :person:`Boethius <boethius>` and :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (:just:`VII.def.22`, :just:`IX.36`), we consider numbers both geometrically as **figurate polygonal numbers**—such as the fourth triangular number (*tetractys*) $T_4 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10$ bounded by the equilateral array :g:`ABC <summa.I.1.figurate:< A B C >>` where :g:`AB <summa.I.1.figurate:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <summa.I.1.figurate:/ A B / = summa.I.1.figurate:/ B C / = summa.I.1.figurate:/ C A />` to :g:`BC <summa.I.1.figurate:/ B C />` and :g:`CA <summa.I.1.figurate:/ C A />`—and by their aliquot parts into **abundant** (*superflui*), **deficient** (*diminuti*), and **perfect** (:term:`numeri perfetti <numero-perfetto>`).

A **perfect number** is one that is precisely equal to all its aliquot parts added together: such is $6$, whose parts are $1, 2, 3$, making exactly $6$; the second is $28$, made of $1, 2, 4, 7, 14$; the third is $496$; the fourth is $8128$.

.. assertion:: [Euclid IX.36] <IX.36>

   All perfect numbers arise in the order given by :person:`Euclid <euclid>` in the final proposition of Book IX (:just:`IX.36`), setting out continuous double numbers beginning from the unit:

   $$1, \; 2, \; 4, \; 8, \; 16, \; \dots, \; 2^{n-1}$$

   Whenever their sum $S_n = 1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^{n-1} = 2^n - 1$ makes a **prime incomposite number** (*numero primo incomposto*), multiplying that sum by the last summed term $2^{n-1}$ always produces a :term:`perfect number <numero-perfetto>`:

   $$P_n = 2^{n-1}(2^n - 1)$$

   In the dyadic rectangle :g:`DEFG <summa.I.1.figurate:< D E F G >>` for $n = 3$, the base :g:`DE <summa.I.1.figurate:/ D E />` has length $1 + 2 + 4 = 7$ (prime) and the altitude :g:`GD <summa.I.1.figurate:/ G D />` has length $2^2 = 4$ (:eq:`equal <summa.I.1.figurate:/ G D / = summa.I.1.figurate:/ E F />` to :g:`EF <summa.I.1.figurate:/ E F />`), beginning from the unit strip :g:`DHKG <summa.I.1.figurate:< D H K G >>` of area $1 \cdot 4 = 4$. Their product $7 \times 4 = 28$ equals the exact sum of its proper aliquot divisors:

   $$1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 = 28$$

Take care not to be deceived, as many are who suppose that every odd place yields a prime: for whenever the sum is composite (such as $1 + 2 + \dots + 256 = 511 = 7 \times 73$ or $2047 = 23 \times 89$), it does not hold, and must be passed over; and their ending always terminates alternately in **$6$** or in **$8$**.

.. qed::

   Just as in the universe perfect things are exceedingly rare (*rarissime sonno le cose perfette*), so among the infinite multitude of numbers the :term:`perfect numbers <numero-perfetto>` generated by :just:`IX.36` stand sparse and incorruptible (*cf.* :ref:`*De Divina Proportione*, Architectura Cap. III <de-divina-proportione-index>`).
