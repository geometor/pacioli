================
Index of Persons
================

.. rubric:: Prosopographical Register of Mathematicians, Patrons, Printers & Commentators

Biographical register of ancient, Islamic, medieval scholastic, Renaissance *abbaco*, Venetian patrician, and early modern figures connected to Pacioli's 1494 *Summa*.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-alberti" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Alberti, Leon Battista (1404 – 1472)
------------------------------------

*Florentine Humanist, Architect, and Theorist*

Hosted young Pacioli in Rome; cited on fol. 68v alongside Vitruvius and Piero della Francesca on architectural and pictorial symmetry.

**Principal Works:** *De Re Aedificatoria*, *De Pictura*, *Ludi Rerum Mathematicarum*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-archimedes" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 – 212 BC)
----------------------------------------

*Greek Geometer and Hydrostatician*

Cited in Summa Part II, Dist. III (fol. 31r) for the 96-gon bounds 3 10/71 < pi < 3 1/7, Dist. V for sphere-and-cylinder mensuration, and Dist. VIII (fol. 70r, prob. 20) for hydrostatic volume measurement by displaced fluid.

**Principal Works:** *Dimensio Circuli*, *De Sphaera et Cylindro*, *On Floating Bodies*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-agostino-barbarigo" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Barbarigo, Agostino (1419 – 1501)
---------------------------------

*Doge of the Republic of Venice (1486 – 1501)*

Doge of Venice under whose signory Paganino de' Paganini and Fra Luca Pacioli were granted a ten-year printing privilege recorded in the colophon of Part II, fol. 76r.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-prosdocimo-de-beldomandi" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Beldomandi, Prosdocimo de' (c. 1380 – 1428)
-------------------------------------------

*Paduan Mathematician, Astronomer, and Music Theorist*

Cited by Pacioli on Part I, fol. 1v among his foremost Italian predecessors in algorism and musical proportion.

**Principal Works:** *Algorismus de integris (1410)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-boethius" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Boethius, Anicius Manlius Severinus (c. 480 – 524 AD)
-----------------------------------------------------

*Roman Philosopher and Codifier of the Quadrivium*

Transmitted Nicomachus's arithmetic and musical ratio nomenclature (multiplex, superparticularis, superpartiens) into the Latin scholastic curriculum expounded in Summa Part I, Dist. I & VI.

**Principal Works:** *De Institutione Arithmetica*, *De Institutione Musica*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-campanus" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Campanus of Novara (c. 1220 – 1296)
-----------------------------------

*Medieval Commentator and Editor of the Latin Euclid*

Papal chaplain whose Latin edition and scholia on Euclid's Elements (printed at Venice by Erhard Ratdolt in 1482 and revised by Pacioli in 1509) supplied Pacioli's proposition numbering and proportion commentary.

**Principal Works:** *Euclidis Elementa cum commentariis Campani (1482)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-cardano" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Cardano, Gerolamo (1501 – 1576)
-------------------------------

*Milanese Physician and Algebraist; Author of the Ars Magna (1545)*

Opened Chapter I of his Ars Magna (1545) by recalling Fra Luca Pacioli's 1494 declaration that cubics were as impossible as squaring the circle before publishing the general cubic and quartic solutions of del Ferro, Tartaglia, and Ferrari.

**Principal Works:** *Artis Magnae, sive de Regulis Algebraicis (1545)*, *Liber de Ludo Aleae*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-euclid" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)
------------------------------------

*Greek Geometer; Author of the Elements*

Revered throughout the Summa as the supreme deductive authority across arithmetic (Books VII–IX), proportion (Book V), irrational binomials and apotomes (Book X), plane geometry (Books I–VI), and the regular solids (Books XI–XIII).

**Principal Works:** *Elements (Books I–XIII)*, *On Divisions of Figures*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-fermat" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Fermat, Pierre de (1607 – 1665)
-------------------------------

*French Mathematician and Co-Founder of Probability and Number Theory*

Resolved both two-player and multi-player cases of Pacioli's Problem of Points in 1654 via equiprobable completion sequences and discovered Fermat's Little Theorem governing Mersenne primes (cf. Summa fol. 8r).

**Principal Works:** *Correspondence with Blaise Pascal (1654)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-scipione-del-ferro" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Ferro, Scipione del (1465 – 1526)
---------------------------------

*Bolognese Mathematician; Discoverer of the Depressed Cubic Formula*

Colleague of Pacioli at the University of Bologna (1501–1502) who, inspired directly by Pacioli's 1494 challenge on fol. 150r, discovered the radical solution to x^3 + px = q around 1515.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-piero-della-francesca" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Francesca, Piero della (c. 1412 – 1492)
---------------------------------------

*Master Painter and Mathematician of Borgo Sansepolcro*

Fellow townsman and mathematical mentor of Pacioli; praised on fol. 68v as 'el monarca a li tempi nostri de la pictura' and author of the Trattato d'abaco, De prospectiva pingendi, and Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus.

**Principal Works:** *Trattato d'abaco*, *De prospectiva pingendi*, *Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-heron" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Heron of Alexandria (fl. 1st century AD)
----------------------------------------

*Alexandrian Geometer and Engineer*

Discoverer of the semiperimeter area formula sqrt(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)) transmitted via Arabic intermediaries and Leonardo Pisano into Summa Part II, Dist. I, Cap. 8 (fol. 9v).

**Principal Works:** *Metrica*, *Dioptra*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-jordanus-nemorarius" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Jordanus de Nemore (Jordanus Nemorarius) (fl. early 13th century)
-----------------------------------------------------------------

*Medieval Mathematician and Statician*

Cited by Pacioli on Part I, fol. 1v and throughout Distinctio VI for speculative arithmetic and proportion theory (De elementis arismetice artis, De numeris datis).

**Principal Works:** *De elementis arismetice artis*, *De numeris datis*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-leonardo-pisano" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Leonardo Pisano (Fibonacci) (c. 1170 – c. 1250)
-----------------------------------------------

*Pisan Mathematician; Principal Medieval Source of the Summa*

Explicitly acknowledged by Pacioli on Part I, fol. 1v as his foremost authority: wherever no other author is named in the Summa, the reader is to understand the doctrine derives from Leonardo Pisano's Liber Abaci (1202/1228) and Practica Geometriae (1220).

**Principal Works:** *Liber Abaci (1202/1228)*, *Practica Geometriae (1220)*, *Liber Quadratorum (1225)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-federico-da-montefeltro" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Montefeltro, Federico da (1422 – 1482)
--------------------------------------

*Duke of Urbino, Condottiero, and Builder of the Palazzo Ducale*

Father of Guidobaldo da Montefeltro; patron of Piero della Francesca, Francesco di Giorgio Martini, and Luciano Laurana, celebrated by Pacioli on prelim. fol. 2v for the architectural proportion of the Ducal Palace of Urbino.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-guidobaldo-da-montefeltro" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Montefeltro, Guidobaldo da (1472 – 1508)
----------------------------------------

*Duke of Urbino; Dedicatee of Pacioli's Summa (1494)*

Son of Federico da Montefeltro and Battista Sforza; humanist prince of Urbino to whom Pacioli dedicated both the Italian and Latin dedicatory epistles of the 1494 Summa and fashioned wooden models of the regular and dependent polyhedra.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-pacioli" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Pacioli, Fra Luca (c. 1447 – 1517)
----------------------------------

*Franciscan Mathematician of Borgo Sansepolcro; Author of the Summa (1494) and De Divina Proportione (1509)*

Born at Borgo Sansepolcro, educated in the workshop of Piero della Francesca and hosted in Rome by Leon Battista Alberti; lectured on Euclid and practical mathematics across Perugia, Zara, Naples, Rome, Milan (alongside Leonardo da Vinci), Florence, Pisa, Bologna, and Venice, seeing his encyclopedic Summa through Paganino de' Paganini's press in November 1494.

**Principal Works:** *Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita (Venice, 1494)*, *De Divina Proportione (Venice, 1509)*, *Euclidis Opera (Latin edition, Venice, 1509)*, *De Viribus Quantitatis (Bologna MS, c. 1508)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-paganino-de-paganini" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Paganini, Paganino de' (fl. 1483 – 1538)
----------------------------------------

*Master Printer from Brescia in Venice*

Master typographer from Brescia operating in Venice who cut the marginal mathematical woodblocks and printed both Pacioli's Summa (Nov. 10–20, 1494) and De Divina Proportione plus Euclidis Opera (1509).

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-pascal" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Pascal, Blaise (1623 – 1662)
----------------------------

*French Geometer and Co-Founder of Mathematical Probability*

In his July–October 1654 correspondence with Pierre de Fermat, resolved the interrupted-game Problem of Points first printed in Pacioli's 1494 Summa (fol. 197r–197v) via backward expectation and the arithmetic triangle.

**Principal Works:** *Traité du triangle arithmétique (1654)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-biagio-pelacani" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Pelacani da Parma, Biagio (Blasius of Parma) (c. 1347 – 1416)
-------------------------------------------------------------

*Scholastic Natural Philosopher and Theorist of Proportions*

Cited on Part I, fol. 1v and in Distinctio VI on kinematic and geometric proportions.

**Principal Works:** *Quaestiones de proportionibus*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-pompilio" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Pompilio, Paolo (c. 1455 – 1491)
--------------------------------

*Roman Humanist Poet*

Author of the Latin elegiac epigram in praise of Fra Luca Pacioli printed on prelim. fol. 1v of the 1494 Summa.

**Principal Works:** *Epigramma ad Lucam Paciolum (Summa, prelim. fol. 1v)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-ptolemy" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Ptolemy, Claudius (c. 100 – c. 170 AD)
--------------------------------------

*Greco-Egyptian Astronomer and Geometer*

Cited in Summa Part II, Dist. III for chord tables and cyclic quadrilaterals from Book I of the Almagest.

**Principal Works:** *Almagest (Syntaxis Mathematica)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-sacrobosco" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Sacrobosco, Johannes de (John of Hollywood) (c. 1195 – c. 1256)
---------------------------------------------------------------

*Scholastic Astronomer and Algorist at Paris*

Acknowledged on Part I, fol. 1v as a standard authority on Hindu-Arabic algorism and spherical astronomy.

**Principal Works:** *Algorismus Vulgaris*, *Tractatus de Sphaera*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-marco-sanudo" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Sanudo, Marco (fl. 1490 – 1505)
-------------------------------

*Venetian Patrician, Mathematician, and Financial Patron of the 1494 Summa*

Learned Venetian nobleman praised by Pacioli on prelim. fol. 1v as 'astrologiae, geometriae, arithmeticaeque cultor exquisitissimus', whose financial patronage underwrote the printing of the 1494 Summa in Venice.

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-giorgio-sommaripa" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Sommaripa, Giorgio (c. 1435 – 1504)
-----------------------------------

*Veronese Patrician and Vernacular Poet*

Author of the Italian caudate sonnet celebrating Pacioli's universal synthesis of arithmetic, geometry, and Venetian double-entry ledger craft on prelim. fol. 1v.

**Principal Works:** *Sonetto a Fra Luca Pacioli (Summa, prelim. fol. 1v)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-tartaglia" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Tartaglia, Niccolò Fontana (1499 – 1557)
----------------------------------------

*Venetian Mathematician and Translator of Euclid and Archimedes*

Rediscovered the cubic solution in Venice (1535), criticized Pacioli's Problem of Points rule in his General Trattato (1556), and published the first Italian translation of Euclid (1543).

**Principal Works:** *Quesiti et inventioni diverse (1546)*, *General Trattato di numeri et misure (1556)*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-vitruvius" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

Vitruvius Pollio, Marcus (c. 80 – c. 15 BC)
-------------------------------------------

*Roman Architect and Engineer*

Cited on Summa Part I, fol. 68v on architectural proportion and human symmetry.

**Principal Works:** *De Architectura Libri Decem*

.. raw:: html

   <div id="person-al-khwarizmi" class="apparatus-anchor"></div>

al-Khwārizmī, Muḥammad ibn Mūsā (c. 780 – c. 850)
-------------------------------------------------

*Founder of Hisāb al-jabr wa'l-muqābala in Baghdad*

Established the six canonical positive-coefficient linear and quadratic equations and their geometric gnomon demonstrations inherited by Leonardo Pisano and expounded in Summa Part I, Dist. VIII (fols. 145r–146r).

**Principal Works:** *Al-Kitāb al-mukhtaṣar fī ḥisāb al-jabr wa'l-muqābala*
