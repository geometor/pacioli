About This Edition
------------------

**GEOMETOR • pacioli** is the scholarly Digital Scriptorium edition and symbolic analysis platform dedicated to the mathematical, proportional, and architectural treatises of **Fra Luca Pacioli of Borgo Sansepolcro, O.F.M.** (c. 1447–1517). Created and curated by **phi ARCHITECT**, the edition reconstructs and unifies Pacioli's two monumental printings published in Venice by Paganino de' Paganini: *De Divina Proportione* (1509; collated with Constantin Winterberg's 1889 Vienna critical edition) and the encyclopedic *Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita* (1494).

The implicit mathematical and visual structures of both treatises are extracted into machine-addressable semantic elements presented through parallel, synchronized reading tracks (diplomatic Renaissance Italian, modern English translation, Winterberg's 1889 German, Latin/Greek citations, and critical notes). In *De Divina Proportione*, the edition formalizes Pacioli's exploration of the Golden Ratio (*proportione avente el mezzo e doi extremi*), the Thirteen Marvelous Effects of Euclid's Book XIII, **Leonardo da Vinci's** sixty perspective polyhedra (*vacui* and *solidi*), Vitruvian architectural proportions, and the geometric construction of the Roman capital alphabet. In the *Summa*, the edition extracts the foundational texts of Renaissance mathematics: Hindu–Arabic algorism, Cossic algebra (*Arte Maggiore*), mercantile arithmetic including the world's first printed treatise on double-entry bookkeeping (*Particularis de computis et scripturis*), and Euclidean stereometry.

Within the GEOMETOR ecosystem, ``geometor.pacioli`` serves as the vital Renaissance nexus linking ancient Greek geometry to the early modern scientific revolution. It directly realizes the Platonic and extreme-and-mean ratio theorems codified in ``geometor.elements`` and ``geometor.euclid``, provides the historical and philosophical core for the automated discovery canon ``geometor.divine``, and connects directly to Albrecht Dürer's ``geometor.durer`` (*Underweysung der Messung*, 1525), which drew directly upon Pacioli's perspective methods, regular polyhedra, and typographic canons.

The GEOMETOR Mission
--------------------

Founded by **phi ARCHITECT**, the GEOMETOR project is dedicated to three foundational pillars:

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge
    Moving beyond passive page scans and OCR to recover the living deductive structure of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises. GEOMETOR elevates prose, marginal citations, primary-source folios (via ``publish folio``), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector ``.euclid-graphic`` diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs.

Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``)
    Anchoring geometric constructions in 100% exact symbolic algebra (`SymPy <https://www.sympy.org>`_) and Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio) over exact algebraic number fields rather than floating-point approximations. Every geometric theorem and construction is mathematically verified and permanently reproducible.

Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums
    Re-deploying extracted semantic and algebraic structures across synchronized multi-track **Book Codices** and EPUB 3 editions (``photon-platform/publish``), computable deductive canons (``geometor.elements``, ``geometor.etc``, ``geometor.pappus``, ``geometor.divine``), interactive SVG laboratories (``geometor.explorer``), QHD visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (``geometor.seer``) across `geometor.com <https://geometor.com>`_.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology
---------------------------------

The Digital Scriptorium edition of Pacioli's corpus is established on primary archival witnesses and authoritative critical scholarship:

- **De Divina Proportione (Venice, 1509)**: *Divina proportione: Opera a tutti glingegni perspicaci e curiosi necessaria...*, printed by Paganino de' Paganini (Venice, 1 June 1509), including Leonardo da Vinci's sixty woodcut plates of regular and semi-regular solids.
- **Winterberg Critical Edition (Vienna, 1889)**: Constantin Winterberg, *Fra Luca Pacioli: Dass Buch von der Göttlichen Proportion*, in *Quellenschriften für Kunstgeschichte*, Neue Folge, Band II (Vienna: Wilhelm Braumüller, 1889).
- **Summa de Arithmetica (Venice, 1494)**: *Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita*, printed by Paganino de' Paganini (Venice, 10 November 1494).
- **Archival Manuscript Witnesses**: Collation against the presentation manuscripts for Ludovico Sforza (*Codex Ambrosianus MS. 170 sup.*, Milan, 1498) and Galeazzo Sanseverino (*Bibliothèque de Genève, MS. Langues Étrangères 210*).
- **Digital Scriptorium Methodology**: Every polyhedral model, letterform, and algebraic problem is transcribed into a structured multi-track codex, paired with calibrated vector diagrams and exact symbolic models in :mod:`geometor.model` compiled via the Photon Publisher framework.
