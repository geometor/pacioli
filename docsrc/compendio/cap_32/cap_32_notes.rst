Historical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------

- **Hypsicles' Book XIV**: Book XIV of the *Elements* was written by :person:`Hypsicles <hypsicles>` of Alexandria (2nd century BCE), who cites the lost treatise *On the Comparison of the Five Figures* by Aristaeus the Elder and the treatise on the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` by Apollonius of Perga.
- **Mathematical Identity**: For a sphere of diameter :math:`D`:
  - The circumradius :math:`\rho` of a face of the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and of a face of the :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>` is identical: :math:`\rho^{2} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{30} D^{2}`? Wait: :math:`a_{20}^{2} = 3\rho^{2}` and :math:`a_{20}^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{10}D^{2}`, so :math:`\rho^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{30}D^{2} = \frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}\cdot\dots` Wait: for the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`, :math:`a_{12} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\frac{D}{\sqrt{3}}`, so :math:`a_{12}^{2} = \frac{6-2\sqrt{5}}{12}D^{2} = \frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}D^{2}`. The circumradius :math:`\rho_{12}` of a regular pentagon of side :math:`a_{12}` satisfies :math:`a_{12}^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}\rho_{12}^{2}`, so :math:`\rho_{12}^{2} = \frac{2}{5-\sqrt{5}}\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{6}D^{2} = \frac{(5+\sqrt{5})(3-\sqrt{5})}{60}D^{2} = \frac{10 - 2\sqrt{5}}{60}D^{2} = \frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{30}D^{2} = \rho_{20}^{2}`!
  - Because both solids have the exact same face circumradius :math:`\rho` and therefore the exact same insphere radius :math:`r_{\text{in}} = \sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{4} - \rho^{2}} = D\sqrt{\frac{5+2\sqrt{5}}{60}}`, the ratio of their volumes equals the ratio of their surface areas: :math:`\frac{V_{20}}{V_{12}} = \frac{A_{20}}{A_{12}} = \frac{s_{\text{cube}}}{s_{20}} = \sqrt{\frac{1/3}{(5-\sqrt{5})/10}} = \sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{6}} \approx 1.09818`.
