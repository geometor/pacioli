CHAPTER XLVI (English)
======================

.. rubric:: How the Exacedron and the Tetracedron Are Placed in the Duodecedron, and Why Only Twelve Inscriptions Are Possible.

Now we complete the twelfth proposition of the fifteenth book (:cite:`Campanus XV.12 <campanus:euclid:XV.12>` and its corollary), showing how both the **cube** (:term:`*exacedron* <exahedron>`) and the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` are inscribed in the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>`, and explaining why only **twelve** of the twenty theoretical inscriptions can be regularly formed.

.. enunciation:: Inscribing the Cube and the Tetrahedron in the Dodecahedron (Campanus XV.12)

   In a given regular body of twelve pentagonal bases (:term:`*duodecedron* <dodecahedron>`), to inscribe the cube (:term:`*exacedron* <exahedron>`) and the pyramid of four triangular bases (:term:`*tetracedron* <tetrahedron>`) so that their vertices stand at vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`.

.. assertion:: Construction and Proof (:just:`XIII.17`, :just:`XIII.8`, Campanus XV.2)

   1. **Inscribing the Cube in the Duodecedron**: As was already demonstrated in :ref:`Chapter XXX <compendio-cap_30-en>` (:just:`XIII.17`) and used in :ref:`Chapter XLI <compendio-cap_41-en>`, across each of the twelve pentagonal faces of the given :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` draw one **pentagonal chord** (:term:`*corda pentagonica* <corda-pentagonica>`, subtending one pentagonal angle) so that the twelve chords meet three by three at eight of the twenty :term:`solid angles <angulo-solido>` (vertices) of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (see the marginal diagram ``0041.g3.png`` on Folio 0041).
      - By :just:`XIII.8` and :just:`XIII.17`, all twelve pentagonal chords are equal, and they form six equal square faces bounding a **cube** whose eight vertices are eight of the twenty vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`!
      - Furthermore, by :just:`XIII.8`, if the pentagonal chord (which is the edge of this inscribed cube) is divided according to our :term:`**Divine Proportion** <divina-proportione>`, its greater segment is the edge of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`!
   2. **Inscribing the Tetracedron in the Duodecedron**: Having inscribed the cube in the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` so that its eight vertices are eight vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, inscribe the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` in that cube by the method of :ref:`Chapter XXXVI <compendio-cap_36-en>` (:cite:`Campanus XV.2 <campanus:euclid:XV.2>`), joining four alternating corners of the cube by the six face diagonals of the cube's six squares.
      - Because the eight vertices of the cube are vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, the **four vertices of the tetracedron** are likewise four of the twenty vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (and its six edges are six interior diagonals of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`)!

.. rubric:: Why Only Twelve Inscriptions Are Regularly Assigned, and the Mystery of the Holy Cross in the Octocedron

Let Your Ducal Highness now consider why, out of the :math:`5 \times 4 = 20` combinations that one might imagine by pairing each of the five regular bodies with the other four, our author and :person:`Campanus <campanus>` assign only these **twelve**:

- **The Duodecedron Receives All Four**: As the figure of the :term:`fifth essence <quinta-essentia>` and of the universal heaven (*il cielo*) which embraces all things created, the :term:`**duodecedron** <dodecahedron>` alone receives all four of the other regular bodies within itself: the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` at its 12 face-centers (:ref:`Cap. XLIII <compendio-cap_43-en>`), the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` at 6 of its 30 edge-midpoints (:ref:`Cap. XLV <compendio-cap_45-en>`), the cube at 8 of its 20 vertices (:ref:`Cap. XLVI <compendio-cap_46-en>`), and the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` at 4 of its 20 vertices (:ref:`Cap. XLVI <compendio-cap_46-en>`).
- **Why the Ycocedron Receives Three, Denying Only the Octocedron**: In the :term:`**ycocedron** <icosahedron>` (20 triangular faces, 12 vertices, 30 edges), we inscribed the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (:ref:`Cap. XL <compendio-cap_40-en>`), the cube (:ref:`Cap. XLI <compendio-cap_41-en>`), and the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` (:ref:`Cap. XLII <compendio-cap_42-en>`), because 20 face-centers accommodate the 20 vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`, 8 of those 20 accommodate the cube, and 4 of those 8 accommodate the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`. Why then does the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` refuse to receive the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>`?
  - Because the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` has **six** vertices at the ends of **three axes that intersect one another at right angles** (*ad angoli recti in croce*), forming the **glorious sign of the Holy Cross** (*el glorioso segno dela sancta Croce*)! Neither the 20 face-centers nor the 12 vertices of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` contain six points at the extremities of three mutually perpendicular diameters; and just as water (represented by the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`) yields to the Cross, or as the devil flees the sign of the Cross, so the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` cannot enclose the :term:`octocedron <octahedron>` at its face-centers or vertices! (Though if one wished to use the midpoints of six opposite edges among the 30 edges of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`, just as we did in the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` in :ref:`Chapter XLV <compendio-cap_45-en>`, three orthogonal axes do pass through six edge-midpoints of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>`.)
- **Why the Other Eight Combinations Fail at Face-Centers or Vertices**: In the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` (4 faces, 4 vertices, 6 edges), only the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>` (6 vertices) can be inscribed using the 6 edge-midpoints (:ref:`Cap. XXXV <compendio-cap_35-en>`), for neither the cube (8 vertices), the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` (12 vertices), nor the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>` (20 vertices) can fit symmetrically on 4 faces, 4 vertices, or 6 edges. Similarly, the :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>` (8 faces, 6 vertices, 12 edges) receives the cube (8 vertices at its 8 face-centers, :ref:`Cap. XXXVIII <compendio-cap_38-en>`) and the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` (4 vertices at 4 of those 8 face-centers, :ref:`Cap. XXXIX <compendio-cap_39-en>`), but its 8 faces, 6 vertices, and 12 edges cannot symmetrically seat the 12 vertices of the :term:`ycocedron <icosahedron>` or the 20 vertices of the :term:`duodecedron <dodecahedron>`.

.. qed::
