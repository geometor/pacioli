Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Citation Convention (`la prima del primo`)**: Pacioli codifies the standard medieval and Renaissance citation formula for :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* (in the fifteen-book :person:`Campanus of Novara <campanus>` edition, where Book XIV is :person:`Hypsicles <hypsicles>`' treatise on the :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>` and :term:`icosahedron <icosahedron>`, and Book XV is the late-antique treatise on inscribing the five Platonic solids in one another). Note that propositional numbering in the :person:`Campanus <campanus>` Latin edition occasionally differs by one or two from the modern Heiberg Greek text (notably in Books VI, X, XIII, and XIV); in our critical commentary we cross-reference both the :person:`Campanus <campanus>` number cited by Pacioli and the standard Heiberg/GEOMETOR G-Index identifier.
2. **Algebraic Abbreviations (*Cosa, Censo, Cubo*)**: Pacioli connects the symbolic syncopation of Renaissance Italian abacus algebra (*R.* = *radice*, *p.* = *più*, *m.* = *meno*, *co.* = *cosa*, *ce.* = *censo*, *cu.* = *cubo*) to the professional notation systems of apothecaries, goldsmiths, astrologers, and merchants.
3. **Geometric-Arithmetical Isomorphism**: Pacioli explicitly equates the arithmetical operations of *multiplicatione* / *producto* ($a \cdot b$) and *quadrato* ($a^2$) with their Euclidean Book II geometric counterparts: *rectangolo* (the rectangle contained by two segments) and :term:`*potentia* <potentia>` (the square on a segment, rendering the Greek $\delta\acute{\upsilon}\nu\alpha\mu\iota\varsigma$, as in the Pythagorean Theorem, :cite:`Euclid I.47 <euclid:elements:I.47>`, *l'ultima del primo*).
