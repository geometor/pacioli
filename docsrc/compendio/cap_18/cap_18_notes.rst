Historical and Philological Commentary
--------------------------------------

1. **Intersecting Diagonals of the Regular Pentagon (Euclid XIII.8 and Euclid XIII.11)**: Proposition 8 of Book XIII proves that if two diagonals :g:`ac` and :g:`be` of an equilateral and equiangular pentagon :g:`abcde` intersect at :g:`f`, both diagonals cut one another in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extrema-et-media-ratio>` ($ac : cf = cf : af$ and $be : ef = ef : bf$), and their greater segments equal the pentagon's side ($cf = ef = ab$). Pacioli also cites :cite:`Euclid XIII.11 <euclid:elements:XIII.11>` (which proves that the side of the regular pentagon inscribed in a circle with rational diameter is the irrational line called a *minor*, $\dot{\varepsilon}\lambda\acute{\alpha}\sigma\sigma\omega\nu$, constructed from a fourth binomial and fourth :term:`apotome <residuo>`) because his *Libellus* (Tractatus I) and *Summa* extensively use :cite:`Euclid XIII.8 <euclid:elements:XIII.8>` and :cite:`Euclid XIII.11 <euclid:elements:XIII.11>` together to calculate all dimensions of the pentagon and :term:`dodecahedron <dodecahedron>`.
