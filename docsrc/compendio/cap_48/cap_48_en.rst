CHAPTER XLVIII (English)

.. rubric:: On the Form and Disposition of the Plane (Solid) and Hollow (Skeletal) Tetracedron, Regular and Truncated (Plates I–IV), and Elevated (Plates V–VI).

Having demonstrated the theoretical generation, proportions, and mutual inscriptions of all five regular bodies, we now present to Your Ducal Highness's eyes their true perspective images—drawn by the hand of our :person:`**Leonardo da Vinci** <leonardo>`—together with their **truncated** (:term:`*abscisi* <abscisum>` or *tagliati*) and **elevated** (*elevati* or :term:`*pontuti* <elevatum>`) dependent bodies, beginning with the first regular body, the :term:`**tetracedron** <tetrahedron>` (:ref:`Plates I–VI <plates-polyhedra-en>`).

.. rubric:: 1. The Regular Tetracedron — Solid (Plate I) and Hollow/Skeletal (Plate II)

- **Plate I (Folio 0187, Tetracedron Planum Solidu[m])** and **Plate II (Folio 0189, Tetracedron Planum Vacuum)**:
  This first figure is the **regular tetracedron** itself, whose formation in the sphere we proved in :ref:`Chapter XXVI <compendio-cap_26-en>` (:just:`XIII.13`). It has **4** equilateral triangular bases (faces), **4** :term:`solid angles <angulo-solido>` (each contained by 3 plane angles of :math:`\frac{2}{3}R`), and **6** equal edges.
  - Under number **I** it is shown **solid** (*piano solido*), so that only the front faces turned toward the eye are seen, while the back faces are hidden by its corporeal mass.
  - Under number **II** it is shown **hollow** or **skeletal** (*piano vacuo*), framed by solid ribs (*pilastrelli*) along its six edges with open windows in the middle of its faces, so that the eye sees simultaneously both the front and the back edges and vertices in true perspective.

.. rubric:: 2. The Truncated Tetracedron (*Tetracedron Abscisum*) — Solid (Plate III) and Hollow (Plate IV)

- **Plate III (Folio 0191, Tetracedron Abscisum Solidu[m])** and **Plate IV (Folio 0193, Tetracedron Abscisum Vacuum)**:
  This second body is derived (*dependente*) from the regular :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` by **truncation** (*abscissione* or *taglio*):
  - If each of the **6 edges** of the regular :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` is divided into **three equal parts** (:math:`\frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}`), and the **4 solid angles** (corners) are sliced off by four plane cuts passing through those trisection points, then:
    1. In place of the **4 cut-off vertices** there appear **4 new equilateral triangles** (whose sides equal :math:`\frac{1}{3}` of the original edge of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`).
    2. Each of the **4 original triangular faces** of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` loses its three corners and is transformed into an **equilateral and equiangular hexagon** (of side :math:`\frac{1}{3}` of the original edge)!
  - Thus the :term:`*tetracedron absciso* <tetrahedron>` has **8 plane bases** in all (**4 equilateral triangles** and **4 regular hexagons**), **12 solid angles** (each formed by two hexagons and one triangle), and **18 equal edges** (6 remaining middle thirds of the original edges plus 12 new edges formed by the 4 triangular cuts).
  - Note well: here the cut was made at **one-third** (:math:`\frac{1}{3}`) of each edge. If instead the cut is carried all the way to the **midpoint** (:math:`\frac{1}{2}`) of each edge, the hexagons vanish into triangles and the body becomes the regular :term:`**octocedron** <octahedron>` of 8 triangular bases, exactly as we proved in :ref:`Chapter XXXV <compendio-cap_35-en>`!

.. rubric:: 3. The Elevated Tetracedron (*Tetracedron Elevatum*) — Solid (Plate V) and Hollow (Plate VI)

- **Plate V (Folio 0195, Tetracedron Elevatum Solidu[m])** and **Plate VI (Folio 0197, Tetracedron Elevatum Vacuum)**:
  This third body is derived from the regular :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` by **elevation** (*elevatione*):
  - Upon each of the **4 equilateral triangular bases** of the interior regular :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`, erect outward an **equilateral triangular pyramid** (a small :term:`*tetracedron* <tetrahedron>`) whose base is that face of the :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` and whose three rising lateral faces are equilateral triangles.
  - The resulting star-like body is bounded externally by :math:`4 \times 3 =` **12 equilateral triangular faces** (while the 4 bases of the inner :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` remain hidden inside, except in the hollow skeletal view of Plate VI where the interior frame is visible!).
  - It has **8 vertices** in all: **4 interior re-entrant vertices** (*angoli de dentro*, the 4 vertices of the core :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>`) and **4 exterior projecting vertices or cones** (*coni / ponti de fore*, the 4 apices of the elevated pyramids), and **18 equal edges** (the 6 edges of the core :term:`tetracedron <tetrahedron>` plus :math:`4 \times 3 = 12` rising edges, called *corausti*).
